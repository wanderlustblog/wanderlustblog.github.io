Is India Safe for Japanese Tourists? What MOFA Actually Says

September 4, 20267 min readby WanderLust Blog

India and Japan maintain an active, growing relationship, and a steady number of Japanese travelers make this trip every year. Two things are genuinely specific to planning it from Japan: how the visa-on-arrival option works, and what MOFA's advisory actually says once you read past the headline rating.

Visa on Arrival: A Genuine Advantage for Japanese Citizens

Japanese citizens have a genuinely convenient option compared to travelers from many other countries: alongside the standard online e-Tourist Visa, Japan is one of a limited number of countries whose citizens can also obtain a Visa on Arrival at select Indian airports, for tourism, business, conference, or medical purposes, for stays of up to 60 days without extension. This has been available since March 2016, in addition to the continuing e-Visa option.

A few practical points regardless of which route you choose:

  • Eligibility for Visa on Arrival requires that you have no residence or employment in India and that your passport has at least six months of remaining validity.
  • The e-Visa route remains fully available and is often the more predictable option if you'd rather have everything confirmed before you depart Japan.
  • For long-term stays (work assignments, study, extended business travel), a different visa category applies, arranged through the Embassy of India in Tokyo or the Consulate-General in Osaka.
  • Requirements and eligible categories are updated periodically — confirm the current details directly with the Embassy of India or India's official visa portal before booking flights.

What MOFA Actually Says

Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs currently rates most of India, including Delhi, Mumbai, Agra, and Varanasi, at Level 1: exercise sufficient caution — a notably lower baseline rating than some other countries currently apply to India, though the practical advice behind it is similar: stay alert, take standard precautions, and check the latest information before you travel.

A few things worth understanding about how this actually breaks down:

  • Srinagar and its surrounding areas carry a distinctly higher Level 3 warning (avoid travel), and areas near the Line of Control in Jammu and Kashmir carry the highest Level 4 warning (evacuate/do not travel) — both well outside standard first-time itineraries.
  • MOFA's own safety guidance for India specifically flags petty crime — pickpocketing, theft, scams, and drink-spiking-related robbery — as more common concerns for travelers than terrorism, and notes that even at Level 1, incidents targeting tourists do occur in popular destinations, so ordinary vigilance still matters. Our guide on common tourist scams in India covers the specific patterns worth recognizing.
  • Registering your trip with Tabireji (たびレジ), Japan's free overseas travel registration service, is a sensible step that helps MOFA reach you with updates or in an emergency.
  • Solo travelers, and women in particular, are advised to take extra care around late-night solo movement and unfamiliar or isolated areas — standard, sensible guidance rather than something specific to India alone.

Check the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' overseas safety website (海外安全ホームページ) directly before finalizing your itinerary, since ratings and specific regional guidance are updated periodically.

Getting There: Flights from Japan

Direct flights connect Tokyo (Narita and Haneda) with Delhi and Mumbai, operated by both Japanese and Indian carriers, with additional one-stop options via hubs like Singapore, Hong Kong, or the Gulf from other Japanese cities. Given the flight time and the time difference between Japan and India, building a lighter first day or two into your itinerary before intensive sightseeing is a sensible way to start the trip — our India itinerary guide for first-time visitors covers how to structure that time.

Power Adapters: A Genuine Voltage Difference

Japan uses Type A and B plugs at 100V, while India uses Type C, D, and M sockets at 230V, so a universal travel adapter is essential — and this is a genuine voltage difference, so check that your devices are rated for 100–240V before assuming a simple adapter alone is sufficient.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is India safe for Japanese tourists?

Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs currently rates most of India, including Delhi, Mumbai, Agra, and Varanasi, at Level 1: exercise sufficient caution, with distinctly higher warnings limited to Srinagar and areas near the Line of Control in Jammu and Kashmir.

Can Japanese citizens get a visa on arrival in India?

Yes — Japan is among a limited group of countries whose citizens can obtain Visa on Arrival at select Indian airports for tourism, business, conference, or medical purposes, for stays of up to 60 days, alongside the standard e-Visa option.

Should I register with Tabireji before traveling to India?

It's recommended — the free service helps Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs reach you with safety updates and assistance if needed.

What kind of crime should Japanese tourists be aware of in India?

Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs specifically flags petty crime — pickpocketing, theft, scams, and drink-spiking-related robbery — as more common concerns for travelers than terrorism, even in Level 1 areas.

Will my Japanese power adapter work in India?

No — Japan uses Type A and B plugs at 100V, while India uses Type C, D, and M at 230V, so you'll need both a plug adapter and to confirm your devices support the higher voltage.
