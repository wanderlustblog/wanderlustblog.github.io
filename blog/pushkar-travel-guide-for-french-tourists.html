Pushkar Travel Guide for French Tourists: Things to Know Before You Visit

September 3, 202611 min readby WanderLust Blog

A good Pushkar travel guide needs to cover more than the postcard version of the town — the pastel houses reflected in the lake, the camels, the ghats at sunset. Pushkar is a genuinely small, walkable place, but it's also one of Hinduism's most sacred pilgrimage towns, with its own specific customs worth knowing before you arrive.

This guide covers what to actually do, when to go, how to get there, and the practical and cultural details worth knowing before you arrive — written for a first visit, without assuming French travelers need anything fundamentally different from any other first-time visitor, beyond a few specific, genuinely useful notes flagged along the way. Best Things to Do in Pushkar

Pushkar's sights cluster tightly around its holy lake, which makes a first visit here easier to plan than most Rajasthani towns.

Pushkar Lake and the Ghats

The lake sits at the physical and spiritual center of the town, ringed by 52 ghats — sets of steps leading down to the water, each with its own history and local significance. Pilgrims bathe here, and the atmosphere shifts noticeably between a quiet early morning and a busier midday. Shoes come off before you approach the water's edge, and it's worth knowing in advance that individuals sometimes present themselves as priests offering a "puja" or blessing, then request a specific, often inflated payment afterward — a well-documented pattern rather than an occasional oddity, similar to others covered in our guide to common tourist scams in India. Politely declining before anything is placed in your hands is the simplest way to avoid it.

Brahma Temple

Pushkar is home to one of the very few temples in the world dedicated to Brahma, the Hindu creator deity, and it's the town's most significant religious site. Shoes, bags, cameras, and leather items are generally not permitted inside, and photography inside the sanctum is not allowed. Modest dress — shoulders and knees covered — is expected, and quiet, respectful behavior matters more here than at a typical tourist attraction. Our fuller guide on what to wear in India as a tourist covers this kind of temple etiquette in more depth.

Other Temples and the Bazaar

Pushkar has several hundred smaller temples beyond Brahma Temple, including Savitri Temple (reachable via a hike or cable car, with views over the town) and Rangji Temple, built in a distinct South Indian architectural style. The main bazaar runs alongside the lake and is worth a slow wander for Rajasthani textiles, silver jewelry, and handicrafts, alongside the general market energy of stalls, cattle, and motorbikes sharing the same narrow lane.

Best Time to Visit Pushkar

October through March is the most comfortable window, with manageable daytime temperatures and cooler evenings — broadly the same recommendation that applies to Rajasthan and North India generally. April through June brings intense desert heat, genuinely difficult for walking the town's sun-exposed lanes and viewpoints.

The Pushkar Camel Fair, one of the world's largest livestock fairs combined with a major Hindu pilgrimage, is the town's biggest annual event, drawing large crowds for camel trading, cultural competitions, and the climactic bathing rituals on Kartik Purnima (the Kartik full moon). Exact dates shift each year with the Hindu lunar calendar, generally falling in October or November — check the current year's specific dates well in advance if this is part of your plan, since accommodation fills up fast and prices rise accordingly during the fair.

How to Reach Pushkar

  • From Ajmer: Pushkar sits roughly 11–15 kilometers from Ajmer, which has the nearest railway station with connections to major Indian cities. Taxis and shared transport run frequently between the two.
  • From Jaipur: Around 140 kilometers by road, typically a few hours' drive; Jaipur also has the nearest major airport, making it the common gateway for reaching Pushkar from outside India.
  • From other Rajasthan destinations: Pushkar is commonly combined with Jaipur, Jodhpur, and Udaipur as part of a broader Rajasthan circuit, connected by road via taxi, private driver, or bus.

Our complete transport guide for India compares these options — private drivers, buses, and trains — in more depth.

Where to Stay, Getting Around, and Food

Pushkar's accommodation ranges from simple budget guesthouses to more comfortable heritage-style properties, with plenty of options within easy walking distance of the lake given how compact the town is. The town itself is small enough that walking is the most practical way to get around the central area; auto-rickshaws cover longer distances or trips slightly outside the town center.

One detail worth knowing clearly before you arrive: Pushkar is a strictly vegetarian and largely alcohol-free town, in respect of its status as a major pilgrimage site. This applies broadly across the town rather than just religious sites specifically, which is a genuinely different norm from most other Indian tourist destinations and worth factoring into expectations around meals and evenings out.

Dress Code and Cultural Etiquette

  • Cover shoulders and knees, particularly near the ghats and at any temple — this applies to all visitors regardless of gender.
  • Remove shoes before approaching the lake's edge or entering any temple.
  • Avoid leather items (belts, bags, wallets) inside Brahma Temple specifically.
  • Ask before photographing people directly, especially pilgrims performing rituals at the ghats.
  • Public displays of affection are best kept low-key, consistent with norms across most of India, and particularly so in a town this religiously significant.

None of this requires perfection — genuine effort at these customs is noticed and appreciated, and small missteps are generally met with patience rather than offense.

Practical Advice: Safety, Scams, Money, and Communication

  • The "priest at the ghat" scam described above is the single most commonly reported issue for tourists in Pushkar specifically — know the pattern, and it loses most of its effectiveness.
  • Shopping in the bazaar involves normal, expected bargaining; comparing prices across a couple of stalls before committing is a sensible habit.
  • UPI and digital payments are increasingly accepted in Pushkar's more tourist-facing shops and restaurants, alongside cash, which remains useful for smaller vendors and informal transactions. See our guide to UPI for foreign tourists for how to set it up.
  • SIM cards and connectivity work the same way here as elsewhere in India — a local SIM or eSIM set up in a larger city like Jaipur or Delhi before arriving in Pushkar is generally more convenient than trying to sort one out in a smaller town.
  • French speakers shouldn't expect widespread French-language signage or service — English is the practical fallback throughout most of India's tourist circuit, including Pushkar, and a few basic Hindi phrases are appreciated but not required.

A Note for French Travelers Specifically

There's little about Pushkar that requires different preparation for French visitors specifically compared to other first-time international travelers — the practical groundwork (visas, vaccinations, travel insurance) is the same regardless of nationality. Two details are worth flagging directly rather than assuming: Pushkar's alcohol-free status is a more noticeable shift for travelers used to wine with dinner as a matter of course, and it's worth knowing in advance rather than being surprised by it on arrival. And as with any trip abroad, it's sensible to check the French Ministry for Europe and Foreign Affairs' travel advice for India before departure, simply as standard travel preparation rather than anything Pushkar-specific.

Suggested 2-Day Pushkar Itinerary

Day 1: Follow the 1-day itinerary above at a slightly slower pace, allowing more time at the ghats and bazaar.

Day 2: A quieter second day — a longer walk through Pushkar's smaller temples beyond Brahma Temple, a slower morning at the lake without a fixed schedule, and time set aside for a day trip toward Ajmer if you're interested in seeing the Dargah Sharif, one of India's most significant Sufi shrines, roughly 15 kilometers away.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Pushkar safe for tourists?

Generally yes — the main issue reported by visitors is the ghat "priest" donation scam rather than anything more serious; standard travel-safety habits apply as anywhere.

Can I drink alcohol in Pushkar?

Pushkar is a strictly vegetarian and largely alcohol-free town out of respect for its religious significance, which is a notable difference from many other Indian tourist destinations.

How do I get from Jaipur to Pushkar?

By road, roughly 140 kilometers, typically a few hours by taxi, private driver, or bus; Jaipur's airport is the common gateway for international arrivals.

What should I wear when visiting Brahma Temple?

Modest clothing covering shoulders and knees, with shoes removed before entering and leather items left behind.

When is the Pushkar Camel Fair?

It generally falls in October or November, following the Hindu lunar calendar, with exact dates shifting each year — check the current year's dates well in advance.

Is one day enough to see Pushkar?

A full day covers the main sights comfortably given how compact the town is, though a second day allows a slower pace and time for a side trip to nearby Ajmer.

Do I need a car to get around Pushkar?

No — the central area is easily walkable, with auto-rickshaws available for anything slightly further out.

Can I take photos inside Brahma Temple?

No, photography is generally not permitted inside the sanctum; ask before photographing people at the ghats as well.

Is Pushkar suitable for a day trip from Jaipur?

It's possible given the road distance, but an overnight stay allows time to properly experience the ghats at both sunrise and sunset, which are genuinely different atmospheres.

Do I need cash in Pushkar, or is card/UPI enough?

Carry both — digital payments and UPI are increasingly accepted in tourist-facing spots, but cash remains useful for smaller vendors and informal transactions.
