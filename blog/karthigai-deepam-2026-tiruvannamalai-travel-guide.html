Karthigai Deepam 2026 Tiruvannamalai: Date, Maha Deepam, Girivalam and Travel Tips

October 28, 202612 min readby WanderLust Blog

Once a year, a giant flame is lit on top of a sacred hill in Tamil Nadu, and hundreds of thousands of people gather below to see it, making Karthigai Deepam one of South India's most powerful festivals.

Karthigai Deepam is the festival of lights celebrated in Tamil Nadu in the month of Karthigai. Its most famous celebration is at Tiruvannamalai, a temple town at the foot of the sacred Annamalai hill, which is revered as a form of Shiva. On the final evening of the festival, a huge flame, the Maha Deepam, is lit on the summit, visible for miles around. The festival draws very large crowds, but with planning it can be a deeply moving experience. This guide covers the date, the rituals, how to reach Tiruvannamalai, where to stay, how to join the Girivalam walk and how to stay safe in the crowds.

Karthigai Deepam 2026: Date and What Happens

Karthigai Deepam is expected on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, which falls on the same full moon as Kartik Purnima in north India. The temple publishes the schedule for the ten-day festival, including processions of deities around the temple streets and the lighting of the Bharani Deepam in the early morning and the Maha Deepam in the evening. Exact timings are announced by the Arunachaleswarar Temple administration, so check their announcements or the Tamil Nadu tourism site before you travel. The cauldron on the hilltop is filled with ghee and a wick, and the flame is lit at dusk, then remains visible for several days.

Why Tiruvannamalai Is Special

Tiruvannamalai is considered one of the five great Shiva temple towns, associated with the element of fire. The Arunachaleswarar Temple, with its tall gateways, is one of the largest temple complexes in India. The town is also known for its spiritual communities and ashrams, including the one associated with the sage Ramana Maharshi, and for the hill itself, which pilgrims and visitors walk around throughout the year.

Girivalam: Walking Around the Hill

Girivalam is the practice of walking around the sacred hill, a circuit of roughly fourteen kilometres. Many people walk barefoot, particularly on full-moon nights, and on Karthigai Deepam night the route is crowded with pilgrims. If you want to try it, here is what to know.

  • Choose your timing. The best time for a calm walk is another full-moon night or an ordinary day. On Karthigai Deepam night, crowds are very large and movement is slow.
  • Walk responsibly. Follow the flow of the crowd, stay on the road and avoid stopping in narrow places.
  • Wear suitable footwear. Many devotees walk barefoot, but visitors may prefer sturdy sandals or shoes, particularly on hot or rough surfaces.
  • Carry water and a small snack. Stalls exist along the route, but they get crowded.
  • Know your limits. The walk takes several hours, so rest if you feel tired or unwell.

How to Reach Tiruvannamalai

Tiruvannamalai is roughly 190 km from Chennai, which usually takes about four hours by road, depending on traffic. The nearest major airport is Chennai, and the town has a railway station with connections to Chennai, Villupuram and other cities. During the festival, the government runs special buses and trains, and roads near the temple are closed to vehicles. Plan to park outside the town and use shuttle buses. For booking tips, read our guide to using trains in India and travelling around India.

Where to Stay

  • Hotels and guesthouses in Tiruvannamalai: rooms near the temple and the Girivalam path sell out weeks ahead and prices rise during the festival.
  • Ashrams and retreat centres: some offer accommodation, but often with rules, such as modest dress, quiet hours and sometimes minimum stays. Book directly and check requirements.
  • Hotels in nearby towns: cheaper and easier to find, but you will need to travel in for the festival and plan the return.

A Simple 2-Day Plan

Day 1: Arrival and temple visit

Arrive in the morning, check in, and visit the temple in the afternoon. Explore the town and its markets, and rest before the evening.

Day 2: The Maha Deepam

Spend a calm morning, find a safe viewing spot well before dusk, and watch the flame being lit. Plan your return carefully, since traffic and crowds are heavy afterwards.

If you can add a day, visit the ashrams, sit quietly in a meditation hall, or take a day trip to nearby historic sites.

Etiquette and Respect

  • Dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered. Men may be required to remove shirts in some temple areas, following local custom, so follow instructions.
  • Remove footwear before entering the temple and keep phones silent.
  • Photography may be restricted inside the temple, so check signs and ask staff.
  • Do not litter on the hill or the Girivalam path, and follow any restrictions.

Crowd Safety

The festival draws very large numbers of pilgrims. Stay with your group, agree a meeting point, keep your valuables minimal and follow police and volunteer instructions. Avoid being in tight bottlenecks, and leave if you feel unsafe. Climbing the hill to see the flame closely is usually restricted, so do not try to enter closed areas. Carry a copy of your ID and your accommodation address. Read our scams guide for common traps around pilgrimage sites.

Food, Weather and Packing

South Indian temple towns offer simple vegetarian meals, such as rice, sambar, dosa and idli. Drink bottled water, and eat freshly cooked food. November is warm and humid, with a chance of rain during the northeast monsoon, so bring light cotton clothes, a light rain layer, sandals that are easy to remove, sunscreen and a power bank. More Questions Visitors Ask

Can foreigners attend Karthigai Deepam?

Yes. Visitors are welcome if they follow local customs and temple rules, though some inner temple areas may have restrictions.

Is Girivalam suitable for first-timers?

It can be, on a quieter day. On festival night, the crowds make it difficult and slower.

Is Tiruvannamalai suitable for a calm spiritual retreat?

Yes, outside festival periods. If you want quiet, visit on a non-festival week and stay at an ashram or a simple guesthouse.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Arriving on the festival evening without accommodation.
  • Trying to drive into the town on the main day.
  • Walking barefoot on very hot or rough surfaces without preparing.
  • Not leaving buffer time for traffic after the lighting.

Final Checklist

  • Official dates and timings checked with the temple.
  • Accommodation and transport booked early, with flexible terms.
  • Modest clothing, a light rain layer and a power bank packed.
  • A plan for crowds, meeting points and the journey home.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Karthigai Deepam 2026?

It is expected on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, but confirm with the Arunachaleswarar Temple announcements.

What is the Maha Deepam?

A large flame lit in a cauldron at the top of the Annamalai hill at dusk on the final evening of the festival.

What is Girivalam?

A walk of roughly 14 km around the sacred hill at Tiruvannamalai, often done barefoot.

How far is Tiruvannamalai from Chennai?

Roughly 190 km, about four hours by road depending on traffic.

Is the festival crowded?

Yes, very. Book accommodation early, avoid driving into the town and plan your return.

Can tourists climb the hill?

Access is usually restricted around the festival, so follow local rules and do not enter closed areas.
