India Travel Adapters, Electronics & Monsoon Packing Guide

September 2, 20267 min readby WanderLust Blog

Most India packing lists cover clothing in detail and wave vaguely at "bring an adapter." Here's the part they usually skip — what plug India actually uses, whether you need a voltage converter, and what changes in your bag if your trip lands during monsoon season.

For the broader clothing and dress-code side of packing, our guide on what to wear in India as a tourist covers that ground in full — this one sticks to the electronics and weather-specific details that don't fit neatly anywhere else.

Electronics: What You Actually Need

A **universal travel adapter** is the one non-negotiable item. India uses Type C, D, and M sockets at 230V/50Hz — Type D is the most common, though many newer hotels have multi-standard outlets that also accept Type C. Most phone, laptop, and camera chargers are dual-voltage (check the label for "Input: 100–240V"), so a plug adapter alone usually suffices; older single-voltage appliances like some hair dryers or curling irons may need a separate voltage converter.

Beyond the adapter, two more things earn their space in the bag:

  • A portable power bank, genuinely useful for long travel days between charging opportunities — train journeys and full sightseeing days both eat battery fast.
  • A phone with offline map capability downloaded in advance, useful for areas with patchy signal, especially once you're outside major cities.

Packing for Monsoon Season

If your trip falls in the roughly July-to-September monsoon window, a few additions matter: a compact, packable rain jacket or poncho, a dry bag or waterproof pouch for electronics and documents, and quick-drying fabrics over anything that stays damp for hours after a downpour. Footwear that can handle wet pavement without becoming a slipping hazard is worth prioritizing over anything purely fashion-driven for this season.

Exact monsoon timing and intensity shift year to year and by region, so it's worth checking current forecasts closer to your travel dates rather than relying on a general seasonal rule alone — the India Meteorological Department, the official government weather authority, publishes current monsoon tracking and regional forecasts.

What You Can Easily Buy in India (So Don't Overpack It)

  • Toiletries, from major international and well-regarded local brands
  • Basic chargers and adapters, at electronics stores and even many pharmacies, if you forget yours
  • Basic medications, at widely available pharmacies (bring a note of the generic name of any regular prescription, since brand names differ)
  • Umbrellas and monsoon gear, seasonally, in local markets — often cheaper than what you'd pack from home

Connectivity and payments are worth sorting early rather than packed for — our guides on whether you need an Indian SIM card and setting up UPI One World cover both in detail.

A Focused Checklist

  • Universal travel adapter (Type C/D/M)
  • Portable power bank
  • Offline maps downloaded before you lose signal
  • Packable rain jacket or poncho (if traveling June–September)
  • Dry bag or waterproof pouch for electronics and documents (monsoon season)
  • Quick-drying fabrics for anything likely to get rained on

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a voltage converter for India?

Most modern phone, laptop, and camera chargers are dual-voltage and only need a plug adapter; older single-voltage appliances like some hair dryers may need a separate converter — check the label first.

What plug type does India use?

India uses Type C, D, and M sockets at 230V/50Hz, with Type D being the most common, though many newer hotels also accept Type C.

What should I pack differently for the monsoon season?

A packable rain jacket, a dry bag for electronics and documents, and quick-drying fabrics over anything that stays wet for hours.

Can I buy a phone charger or adapter in India if I forget mine?

Yes — electronics stores and even many pharmacies in Indian cities sell basic adapters and chargers, so forgetting one isn't a trip-ending problem.

When exactly is monsoon season in India?

Roughly June through September for most of the country, though the exact onset and intensity vary by region and year — check current forecasts closer to your travel dates.
