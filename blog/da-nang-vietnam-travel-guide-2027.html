Da Nang Vietnam Travel Guide 2027: Beaches, Hoi An, Hue, Food and a 4-Day Plan

November 4, 202613 min readby WanderLust Blog

Da Nang has beaches, mountains, a river city, ancient towns within an hour and a food scene that rivals Hanoi's, which explains why it keeps appearing on 2027 trending lists.

Da Nang is Vietnam's fourth-largest city and the gateway to the central coast. It sits between mountains and sea, with a long white-sand beach, a lively riverfront, a modern skyline lit up at night and some of the country's best food. It is also the starting point for Hoi An's lantern-lit old town, Hue's imperial citadel and the spectacular Hai Van Pass. In recent travel trend reports for 2027, Da Nang appears among the destinations to watch, and it is easy to see why: it works for beach lovers, culture seekers, families, remote workers and food travellers alike. This guide explains when to go, what to see, where to stay, what to eat and how to plan a four-day trip.

Why Da Nang Is Trending

A major 2027 travel trends report lists Da Nang among its top destinations, and part of its appeal is the combination of convenience and variety. It has an international airport, modern infrastructure, reasonable prices and a very walkable beach area. Many travellers also think about whether a destination would be somewhere they might live or work, and Da Nang is popular with digital nomads and expats because of good internet, cafés and coworking spaces. For a first-time visitor, it is an easier and less overwhelming introduction to Vietnam than a big city.

Best Time to Visit Da Nang

The best months are generally February to May, when the weather is warm, the skies are clearer and the sea is calm. Summer, from June to August, is hot and sunny, good for beaches but very warm in the middle of the day. The wet season, roughly from September to December, brings heavy rain and the risk of typhoons, and sea conditions can be rough. Check forecasts and local advisories, and have flexible plans if you travel in the wet season. January and February can be cool and drizzly in some years, so pack a light rain layer.

The Main Things to See and Do

  • My Khe Beach: a long beach with fine sand, popular for swimming, sunrise walks and seafood. Swim only where lifeguards are on duty, and follow flag signals.
  • Ba Na Hills and the Golden Bridge: a mountain resort reached by a long cable car, famous for a bridge held up by giant stone hands. It is very busy, so go early and check ticket prices and opening times.
  • Marble Mountains: five limestone and marble hills with caves, temples and viewpoints, a short ride from the beach. Wear good shoes and be respectful in the temples.
  • Son Tra Peninsula and Lady Buddha: a forested peninsula with sea views, a very tall Buddha statue and wildlife. Do not feed or disturb the monkeys, and keep to marked roads.
  • Dragon Bridge: a long bridge in the shape of a dragon that breathes fire and water at set times on weekend evenings. Arrive early to find a viewing place.
  • The Han River promenade and night market: a pleasant place for an evening walk, snacks and souvenirs.
  • Museum of Cham Sculpture: a compact museum with a fine collection from the ancient Cham civilisation.

Day Trips: Hoi An and Hue

Hoi An

Hoi An's old town, about 30 km south of Da Nang, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site known for its yellow shophouses, tailors and lantern-lit river at night. It is easily reached in about 45 minutes by taxi or ride-hailing. Visit in the late afternoon and evening, when the lanterns are lit, and avoid weekends if you want fewer crowds. Many visitors stay overnight, and a few days in Hoi An fits well with a Da Nang trip.

Hue and the Hai Van Pass

Hue, about 100 km north, was Vietnam's imperial capital and has a huge citadel, royal tombs and a calm river. The route over the Hai Van Pass, a winding coastal road with sweeping views, is one of the most scenic drives in Vietnam. It is often done as a private car or a motorbike tour with a guide. Allow a full day, and check the weather, since fog and rain can reduce visibility.

A 4-Day Da Nang Itinerary

Day 1: Beach and city

Arrive, check in and spend the afternoon on My Khe Beach. In the evening, walk the riverfront and watch the Dragon Bridge show if it is a weekend.

Day 2: Ba Na Hills and the Marble Mountains

Visit Ba Na Hills early to avoid the crowds, return in the afternoon, and visit the Marble Mountains late in the day when the heat has eased.

Day 3: Hoi An

Travel to Hoi An in the morning, explore the old town, have a custom clothing fitting if you want, and stay for the lanterns at night.

Day 4: Son Tra Peninsula and departure

See Lady Buddha and the coastal views in the morning, enjoy a final seafood lunch and leave with a buffer for the airport.

Food in Da Nang

Da Nang's food scene is a highlight. Try mi Quang, turmeric-coloured noodles served with a little broth, herbs, peanuts and pork, shrimp or chicken. Banh xeo, a crisp savoury pancake, and bun cha ca, a fish cake noodle soup, are also local favourites. Fresh seafood along the coast is excellent and usually good value, though you should check prices before ordering by weight. In Hoi An, try cao lau noodles, white rose dumplings and the famous banh mi sandwiches. Street food is generally safe at busy stalls with high turnover. If you have dietary requirements, learn a few key phrases or use a translation card.

Where to Stay

  • My Khe and An Thuong: beachfront hotels, restaurants and a lively travel atmosphere. Best for first-timers who want a beach holiday.
  • City centre and the river: a more local feel, with easy access to cafés, markets and the Dragon Bridge.
  • Son Tra and resorts: quieter, resort-style stays with more privacy, often a short drive from the city.
  • Hoi An: for a few nights, a homestay or boutique hotel near the old town gives a different experience.

Book early for holidays, and compare cancellation terms. Check whether your hotel is walkable to the beach and restaurants.

Getting There and Around

Da Nang International Airport is close to the city centre, with direct flights from many Asian cities and domestic flights from Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City. Ride-hailing apps such as Grab are the easiest way to get around, and taxis are plentiful. Many visitors rent scooters, but traffic can be chaotic, and you need a valid licence and insurance. If you rent a scooter, never leave your passport as a deposit, and wear a helmet. Entry rules for Vietnam vary by nationality, and many visitors can apply for an electronic visa for stays up to 90 days, so check the official government site well before you travel.

Is Da Nang a Good Place to Live or Work Remotely?

Many travellers now test a destination as a possible place to live, sometimes called vacation vetting. Da Nang has fast internet in most areas, a growing number of coworking spaces and cafés, good healthcare and an international airport. Rent is generally lower than in many Asian cities, and the beach and mountains are close. The trade-offs include the wet season, the need to navigate traffic and visas, and a smaller expat and professional network than Ho Chi Minh City or Bangkok. If you are considering a longer stay, rent a place for a month before committing, and check visa rules and local laws.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Vietnam is good value, but prices change by season and standard. Build your own estimate from flights, accommodation, local transport, food, activities such as Ba Na Hills tickets, souvenirs and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare at least three hotels for your dates, and check taxes and breakfast. Eating at local restaurants is far cheaper than in tourist areas.

Safety and Common Scams

  • Use ride-hailing apps or metered taxis, and agree the fare in advance if there is no meter.
  • Check prices before you order seafood by weight, and confirm the total.
  • Keep your phone and wallet secure on motorbikes and in crowds, since snatch-and-grab theft can occur.
  • Swim only on lifeguarded beaches, and respect warning flags.

What to Pack

Light, breathable clothing, swimwear, a hat, sunglasses, sunscreen, a light rain jacket, comfortable walking shoes and sandals, insect repellent and a power bank. Bring a modest outfit for temples, with shoulders and knees covered, and a reusable water bottle.

Da Nang fits easily into a wider Vietnam route, for example with Hanoi, Ha Long Bay and Ho Chi Minh City. More Questions Visitors Ask

How many days do I need in Da Nang?

Three to four days covers the main sights, and five to six lets you add Hoi An and Hue at a relaxed pace.

Is Da Nang better than Hoi An?

They are different. Da Nang offers beaches and modern comforts, while Hoi An offers a charming historic old town. Many visitors combine them.

Is Da Nang good for families?

Yes. It has safe beaches with lifeguards, many resorts, family attractions and easy transport.

Is it expensive?

It is generally good value compared with Western destinations, though prices rise in peak seasons and in resort areas.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Travelling in typhoon season without flexible plans.
  • Visiting Ba Na Hills at midday, when it is most crowded.
  • Renting a scooter without experience, a licence or insurance.
  • Staying only in Da Nang and skipping the day trips.
  • Leaving your passport with a rental shop.

Final Checklist

  • Entry rules and visa checked, with passport validity confirmed.
  • Hotel booked with flexible terms, and the season and weather checked.
  • Light clothing, a rain layer, sunscreen and modest temple clothes packed.
  • Day trips planned for Hoi An, Hue or the Hai Van Pass.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Da Nang trending for 2027?

A major 2027 travel trends report lists it among top destinations, thanks to its beaches, food, convenience and value.

When is the best time to visit Da Nang?

February to May is generally the best, with warm, drier weather and calm seas.

How far is Hoi An from Da Nang?

About 30 km, roughly 45 minutes by road.

Do I need a visa for Vietnam?

It depends on your nationality, and many visitors can apply for an e-visa. Check the official government site before you go.

How many days do I need?

Three to four days for Da Nang, plus extra days for Hoi An and Hue.

Is Da Nang safe for tourists?

Generally yes, with normal precautions. Be careful on scooters and at the beach, and use metered taxis or ride-hailing.
