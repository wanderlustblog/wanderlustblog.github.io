Moldova Travel Guide: Orheiul Vechi Cave Monastery, Underground Wine Cellars and a 3-Day Plan

November 12, 202613 min readby WanderLust Blog

Moldova is one of Europe's least visited countries, with cliff-side monasteries, endless vineyards and cellars so large that visitors tour them by car.

Moldova lies between Romania and Ukraine, and it is one of the least visited countries in Europe. It is also one of the most interesting for travellers who like wine, rural culture and history. Its vineyards have been tended for centuries, and its wine cellars are famous: two of them, Cricova and Mileștii Mici, are huge underground cities of tunnels that store millions of bottles, and visitors tour them in vehicles. Near the capital, Orheiul Vechi is an archaeological complex built around a cave monastery carved into a limestone cliff above a river valley. Moldova has appeared in travel trend articles as an emerging destination for travellers who want to avoid crowded hubs. This guide explains what to see, how to reach it, how to stay safe, and how to plan three days in and around Chișinău.

Why Moldova Counts as a Hidden Location

Moldova has little mass tourism, which means you can have a vineyard, a monastery or a village to yourself, and prices are low. Visitors often describe it as a country where a local family will invite you for wine and food, and where traditions in the villages are still alive. It is not polished: public transport is basic, signage can be limited and the capital has Soviet-era buildings alongside modern cafés. For many travellers, that is part of its charm. It is best for people with a flexible attitude and an interest in wine, history and rural life.

Orheiul Vechi: The Cave Monastery

Orheiul Vechi (Old Orhei) is an archaeological and religious complex about 60 kilometres from Chișinău, in a deep valley formed by the Răut River. The site has traces of settlements from different eras, including fortresses and an old bath house, but its centrepiece is the cave monastery, dug into the limestone cliff by monks centuries ago, with narrow passages, a small chapel and a cell. Visitors can walk the cliff path and look out over the valley, and visit the nearby village of Butuceni, where family guesthouses and restaurants serve local food. Wear sturdy shoes, and dress modestly at religious sites. The site is popular with day-trippers from the capital, but quiet early in the morning.

The Underground Wine Cellars

Cricova

Cricova is a former limestone mine near Chișinău that has been turned into an enormous underground wine cellar, with a network of streets named after wines and a collection of rare vintages. Tours are conducted in electric vehicles, and include tastings. Book in advance, particularly in high season, and check the languages offered.

Mileștii Mici

Mileștii Mici is another huge underground cellar, known for its record-sized collection of bottles, which is recognised by the Guinness World Records as the largest wine collection in the world. Visitors travel through the tunnels by car or on foot and taste wines. Both cellars are cool, around 12 to 14 degrees Celsius, so bring a warm layer even in summer.

Do not drive if you plan to taste, and arrange a driver or a tour. Many cellars and boutique wineries also offer smaller, more personal tastings with a meal, which can be a good alternative.

Chișinău: A Short City Stay

Chișinău is a green, relaxed capital with wide boulevards, parks, markets and cafés. Visit the Central Market, where farmers sell fruit, honey and cheese, walk through Ștefan cel Mare Park, see the Nativity Cathedral and the Triumphal Arch, and visit the National Museum of Ethnography and Natural History. In the evening, the city has a lively scene of wine bars and restaurants, where you can taste Moldovan wines by the glass. English is spoken by younger people and in cafés and hotels, and Russian and Romanian are widely used.

A 3-Day Plan

Day 1: Chișinău

Arrive, check in and explore the city centre, the market and the parks. Have dinner with local wine and traditional dishes.

Day 2: Orheiul Vechi

Take a morning trip to Orheiul Vechi, visit the cave monastery and the viewpoints, have lunch in Butuceni and return in the afternoon. Visit a smaller winery on the way back, if time allows.

Day 3: Cricova or Mileștii Mici

Visit one of the great cellars with a pre-booked tour, then spend the afternoon on a final walk in the city or a short visit to a second site, before heading to the airport.

If you have more time, visit the monastery of Căpriana or Saharna, the fortress of Soroca on the Dniester River and the vineyards around Purcari, known for historic wines.

Food and Wine

Moldovan food is hearty and rural, with influences from Romanian, Ukrainian and Russian cuisine. Try mămăligă (cornmeal porridge) with cheese and sour cream, sarmale (stuffed cabbage rolls), plăcinte (filled pastries), zeama (chicken soup), fresh vegetables, cheeses and grilled meat. Vegetarian options are available, particularly in summer. The wine is the main attraction: local grapes include Fetească and Rară Neagră, and international varieties are also widely grown. The National Wine Festival, held in early October in Chișinău, celebrates the harvest. Drink responsibly, and choose a driver or a tour for winery visits.

Best Time to Visit

May to October is the best period, with warm weather and green vineyards. September and early October bring the harvest and the wine festival, and are the busiest times for local events. Summers can be hot, and winters are cold and grey, with fewer visitors and some places closed. The cellars stay cool all year, so a visit works even in bad weather.

Getting There and Around

Chișinău International Airport has flights from several European cities, and many travellers connect through Istanbul, Vienna or other hubs. Buses connect Chișinău with Romania and nearby countries. Inside Moldova, minibuses called marshrutkas link towns, but schedules can be confusing, and rural access is limited. Taxis and ride-hailing are cheap in the capital. For day trips to Orheiul Vechi and the cellars, hiring a driver or joining a tour is the easiest option. Always agree on prices in advance.

Entry Rules for Indian Travellers

Entry rules for Moldova depend on your nationality and other visas you hold. Some travel guidance notes that Indian travellers may apply for an electronic visa, or may be allowed entry if they hold certain valid visas, but rules change, so check the official Moldovan government website or embassy before you book. See our visa guide for a step-by-step method.

Safety and Responsible Travel

  • Check your government's travel advice before you travel. The region of Transnistria, on the eastern border, is a separatist area with its own checkpoints and rules. Visitors should be careful and not travel there without checking the latest guidance.
  • Moldova is generally safe for visitors, but take normal precautions with valuables, particularly in markets and on public transport.
  • Do not drink and drive. Police checks are common.
  • Ask before photographing people, and respect religious sites.
  • Buy travel insurance; see our insurance guide.

What to Pack

Comfortable shoes, a light jacket for cellars and evenings, modest clothes for monasteries, a hat, sunscreen in summer and a power bank. Bring some cash in Moldovan lei, since small places may not accept cards, and a reusable bottle.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Moldova is among the cheapest countries in Europe. Build your estimate from flights, accommodation, driver or tour costs, cellar tour fees, meals, tastings, insurance, any visa fees and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare options for your dates, and check what is included in tours.

More Questions Travellers Ask

Is Moldova safe for tourists?

Generally yes, though you should follow your government's travel advice and avoid conflict-affected border areas.

Can I visit the wine cellars without a car?

Yes, by tour or taxi. Pre-book tours, since slots can fill in high season.

How many days do I need?

Three days covers Chișinău, Orheiul Vechi and one big cellar, with extra time for villages and monasteries.

Is Moldova good for vegetarians?

Mostly yes, with plenty of vegetable dishes and dairy, though check for meat stock.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not booking cellar tours in advance.
  • Driving after tastings.
  • Ignoring government advice about border regions.
  • Not checking entry rules and insurance.
  • Wearing light clothes in the cool cellars.

Final Checklist

  • Entry rules, insurance and travel advice checked.
  • Cellar tours and a driver booked.
  • Warm layer, comfortable shoes and cash in lei packed.
  • A flexible plan for Orheiul Vechi and Chișinău.
