Can Foreign Tourists Use UPI in India? A Complete 2026 Guide

August 31, 20269 min readby WanderLust Blog

Spend even a day in India and you'll notice it: almost nobody pays with cash anymore. From street food stalls to five-star hotels, everyone's scanning a small black-and-white QR code and tapping their phone. That's UPI — the Unified Payments Interface — and it's genuinely everywhere. So it's a fair question before you land: can foreign tourists use UPI in India, or is this one convenience that's off-limits to visitors?

The short answer is yes, foreign tourists can use UPI in India — just not in exactly the same way residents do. There's a dedicated pathway built specifically for visitors, and understanding it before you arrive saves a lot of fumbling with cash and card machines once you're there.

What Is UPI, Quickly

UPI is India's real-time payment system, built and run by the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI). It lets people pay instantly using a linked bank account, a phone app, and a UPI PIN. It launched in 2016 and is now the default way most Indians pay for everything.

Normally, using UPI means having an Indian bank account and an Indian phone number — neither of which a tourist has on day one. That's exactly what NPCI's tourist-facing solution was built to solve.

Can Foreign Tourists Use UPI in India?

Yes. Since mid-2024, NPCI extended its UPI One World wallet to inbound international travellers broadly, not just delegates at specific events. It's designed so a visitor can tap into India's UPI network without becoming a full banking customer first.

It's worth being precise about what this looks like in practice, though: tourists aren't logging into a regular Indian banking app the way a resident would. Instead, they use a prepaid wallet product that runs on the same UPI rails, issued through NPCI's authorised partners.

What Is UPI One World?

UPI One World is a prepaid payment instrument (PPI) — essentially a prepaid digital wallet — created by NPCI specifically for foreign visitors and non-resident Indians. It was first piloted for a limited set of nationalities around the G20 summit, then expanded to be available to inbound travellers more broadly.

The idea is simple: you load Indian rupees onto the wallet, then scan and pay at any merchant that accepts UPI, just like a local would. NPCI has partnered with entities such as IDFC First Bank and Transcorp International to issue and manage these wallets, with other partner apps joining the ecosystem since.

One reassuring detail: any balance you don't spend during your trip can generally be transferred back to your original payment source when you leave, in line with foreign exchange regulations, so you're not meant to be stuck with rupees you can't recover.

For the source information straight from the organisation that runs it, NPCI's own overview of UPI access for international and NRI users is available on the official NPCI website.

How Tourists Can Set Up UPI

Setting up UPI One World generally follows this pattern: find an authorised issuer touchpoint — a counter at major international airports, a partner bank, a money exchange location, or a partner app you download before or after arrival; complete KYC verification using your passport and valid visa (some issuers also ask for a quick selfie); set your UPI PIN, the same way a local user would; load the wallet using an international debit or credit card, or in some cases foreign currency cash converted to rupees; then start paying by scanning UPI QR codes at participating merchants. Exact steps, documents, and load limits can vary by issuer, so check directly with the specific partner app or bank you choose rather than assuming every provider works identically.

Do You Need an Indian Bank Account or Phone Number?

No. That's the entire point of UPI One World. You don't need to open a local bank account, and you don't need an Indian SIM card. Your KYC is based on your passport and visa instead of local banking documents, and the wallet can typically be linked to your existing international mobile number — a meaningful difference from how UPI normally works for residents, where a linked Indian bank account is the whole foundation.

Where Can Tourists Use UPI in India?

Because UPI One World runs on the same infrastructure as regular UPI, it's accepted almost everywhere you'd see a UPI QR code — which, across most Indian cities and many smaller towns, is nearly every retail transaction: shops, restaurants, cafés, local transport, and a wide range of merchants. This held true even in the smaller Himalayan towns we covered in our Spiti Valley itinerary guide, where digital payments have reached further than the remoteness of the region might suggest.

One limitation worth knowing: UPI One World is built for merchant payments, not for sending money to individuals the way residents transfer to each other. Splitting a bill directly with a local friend isn't quite what the tourist wallet is designed for.

UPI vs Cash vs Cards for Tourists

Each option carries a real trade-off. UPI via UPI One World is fast and widely accepted, skips the need to carry cash, and avoids friction at small vendors without card machines. Cash still works as backup in very rural areas or with vendors who aren't UPI-enabled, but carrying large amounts has its own risk — something worth weighing alongside the general safety habits we cover in our guide on solo travel safety in India. International cards are convenient for hotels and larger stores, but many small shops, street vendors, and local transport simply don't take cards — exactly the gap UPI fills. Most experienced travellers mix all three: UPI for daily spending, a card as backup for bigger purchases, and a little cash for the rare vendor who takes neither.

Common Problems Foreign Tourists Face With UPI

A few recurring friction points worth knowing ahead of time: KYC delays at busy touchpoints like airports during peak season; app or connectivity issues, since the system depends on a working internet connection; load limits and top-up methods that differ by issuer, since separate partners manage their own wallets; confusion with regular Indian UPI apps, which are tied to local bank accounts and generally won't work for tourists; and refund timing, since getting an unused balance back follows standard forex processing rather than being instant. None of these are dealbreakers, but knowing them ahead of time means fewer surprises.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreign tourists use UPI in India without an Indian bank account?

Yes — UPI One World is designed to work using KYC based on your passport and visa instead of a local bank account.

Do I need an Indian SIM card to use UPI as a tourist?

No, it can generally be linked to your existing international mobile number.

What is UPI One World?

A prepaid wallet created by NPCI for foreign tourists and NRIs, enabling UPI merchant payments without a full local bank account.

Where can I set up UPI One World when I arrive?

Common touchpoints include partner counters at major international airports, designated banks, money exchange locations, and partner apps.

Can I send money to individuals with UPI One World?

It's primarily built for merchant payments, not person-to-person transfers.

What documents do I need?

Typically a valid passport and visa for identity verification with the issuing partner.

Can I get my unused balance back when I leave?

Yes, generally transferable back to your original payment source under forex regulations, though not necessarily instant.

Is UPI accepted everywhere in India?

Very widely in cities and increasingly in smaller towns, though some rural or very small vendors may still be cash-only.

Is UPI safer than carrying cash?

It reduces the risk of carrying large cash amounts, though it still requires basic precautions like never sharing your PIN and checking QR codes before scanning.

Can I use a regular Indian UPI app instead of UPI One World?

Standard UPI apps are tied to Indian bank accounts, so tourists without one should use a tourist-specific wallet like UPI One World.

Bottom Line

UPI has genuinely changed how payments work in India, and NPCI has built a real pathway for tourists to use it without opening a local bank account or getting an Indian SIM. It's not identical to using UPI as a resident — expect a prepaid wallet, merchant-only payments, and some issuer-specific setup — but for a first-time visitor trying to skip cash queues and card friction, UPI One World is worth setting up before or right after you land.
