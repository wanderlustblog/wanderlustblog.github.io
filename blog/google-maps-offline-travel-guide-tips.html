How to Use Google Maps Offline When You're Off the Grid

August 21, 20268 min readby WanderLust Blog

No signal doesn't mean no map. After getting lost more than once in places where "nearby" meant three hours away, downloading maps before we lose signal has become as automatic as packing a charger.

Quick TakeDetails
Best forRoads, driving directions, general navigation without signal
Not built forHiking trails, live traffic, real-time transit
When to downloadDay before departure, on strong wifi
Storage neededA few hundred MB to a few GB per region

The first time we genuinely needed an offline map, we were driving toward a stretch of highway in rural Uzbekistan with a rental car, a paper backup that turned out to be outdated, and a phone that had quietly lost signal an hour outside Bukhara. We'd downloaded the region the night before almost as an afterthought. It ended up being the only reason we found our turn.

Downloading before you actually need it

Offline maps only work if you set them up while you still have a connection, which sounds obvious but is easy to forget in the rush of a departure day. Search your destination in the app, tap your profile icon, choose "Offline maps," then select the region you want stored locally. For anywhere genuinely remote, we now do this the night before rather than at the airport, since larger regional downloads can take longer than expected and airport wifi is rarely reliable enough to finish the job.

What offline mode actually gives you

Downloaded regions store the base map, points of interest, and turn-by-turn driving directions — enough to get from A to B on roads without a live connection. What it doesn't store is real-time traffic, current transit schedules, or live place details like whether somewhere is open right now. It's a navigation tool, not a full offline version of the app, and treating it as anything more leads to the kind of confident-but-wrong turns that cost you an extra hour on a dirt road.

Where it genuinely saved us

Beyond Uzbekistan, offline maps mattered most in Madagascar, where signal disappears for long stretches between towns, and again in Socotra, where there's barely a functioning network to begin with. In both places, having the region downloaded in advance meant the difference between a confident drive and pulling over every twenty minutes hoping for a bar of signal.

Where it falls short — and what to bring instead

In Svalbard, offline Google Maps was close to useless outside the one town with a real road network; there simply isn't enough mapped infrastructure for it to matter, and any movement beyond town limits requires a guide, a snowmobile, and entirely different navigation tools built for polar terrain. The lesson generalized well beyond the Arctic: for hiking trails, backcountry routes, or anywhere without real roads, a dedicated offline hiking app or a downloaded GPX track is far more reliable than Google Maps' driving-focused offline mode.

A few habits worth building

We now download the region a full day ahead, screenshot key addresses as backup in case the app itself misbehaves, and star our hotel or guesthouse location before losing signal so it's always one tap away. For general trip logistics before departure — flight times, basic destination research — we still lean on Google's travel tools while we have a connection, then switch entirely to offline mode once we're actually moving.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far in advance should I download an offline map?

Download it while you still have strong wifi, ideally the day before you lose signal, since large regional maps can take a while and downloads can fail on weak connections.

Does offline Google Maps show real-time traffic or transit?

No — offline mode only stores the base map and turn-by-turn driving directions; live traffic, transit times, and place details need a connection.

Will offline maps work for hiking trails?

Only loosely — Google Maps offline is built for roads, not trails, so a dedicated hiking app or downloaded GPX file is more reliable in true backcountry.
