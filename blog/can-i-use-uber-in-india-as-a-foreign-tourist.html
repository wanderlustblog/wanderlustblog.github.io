Can I Use Uber in India as a Foreign Tourist? (2026 Guide)

August 31, 20268 min readby WanderLust Blog

One of the more pleasant surprises for first-time visitors to India is discovering that the transport question — often the most stressful part of arriving somewhere unfamiliar — is largely solved before you even land.

Uber operates across India, and yes, foreign tourists can use it, with the same account most travellers already have on their phone.

That said, "it works" and "it works exactly like it does at home" aren't quite the same thing. Here's what the WanderLust team has learned about getting Uber actually working smoothly in India, from account setup through your first ride.

Can Foreign Tourists Use Uber in India?

Yes. Uber's registration and verification system is global, which means an account created and verified with your home country's phone number works in India the same way it works anywhere else Uber operates. You don't need to create a separate India-specific account, and you don't need a new number just to use the app.

How to Create or Use an Uber Account in India

If you already have Uber installed and use it at home, you're mostly set — just open the app once you land and it should recognise your existing account. If you're setting one up for the first time, download the app, sign up with your email and phone number, and complete SMS verification on that number before you travel, since you'll need to be able to receive that verification text.

Phone Number and Account Requirements

You do not need an Indian SIM card or local phone number to use Uber in India. Your existing account, tied to your home country number, functions normally — you'll just need a working data connection (Wi-Fi, roaming, or a local SIM/eSIM) to actually run the app and communicate with your driver through it. According to Uber's own help documentation, the phone number linked to your account is what's used for account verification and driver communication, and it doesn't need to change just because you've crossed a border. If you do want a local number and data plan for the rest of your trip, our guide on getting a SIM card in India as a foreigner covers exactly how that process works.

Can Tourists Use an International Credit or Debit Card?

Generally, yes — Uber has historically supported international cards as a payment method in India alongside local options. One detail worth knowing: Uber does not accept prepaid cards, so if your primary card abroad is a prepaid or virtual one, you may need a backup. If a card you've added stops working properly while you're in India, Uber's support suggests checking that the country field on the card matches where you're currently using it, and re-adding it if needed.

Cash and Other Payment Options

Cash is a genuinely available payment method for Uber rides across India — you simply select cash before booking and pay your driver directly at the end of the trip. Beyond cash and cards, Uber India also supports UPI and other local digital wallets as payment options within the app. For a first-time visitor without a local card or UPI set up yet, cash is often the simplest way to get moving on day one — though setting up UPI One World before or shortly after arrival opens up the most flexible payment option once it's active.

Which Indian Cities Have Uber

Uber operates across a wide range of major Indian cities, including Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata, and Pune, among many others. Coverage is strongest in large metro areas and airport transfers are a particularly well-used part of the service. If you're heading somewhere smaller or more remote, it's worth checking availability directly in the app before relying on it as your only transport plan, since coverage in smaller towns can vary.

How to Book an Uber Step by Step

  1. Open the app and confirm your pickup location, adjusting the pin if needed.
  2. Enter your destination.
  3. Choose your ride type and review the fare estimate before confirming.
  4. Select your payment method — cash, card, UPI, or wallet.
  5. Confirm the booking and check your driver's name, photo, and license plate as they're assigned.
  6. Track the car's arrival on the map and meet it at the pinned location.
  7. At the end of the trip, pay (if cash) or let the app process your chosen payment method automatically.

Uber vs Local Taxis for Tourists

The biggest advantage Uber offers tourists is predictability: the fare is shown upfront, the route is tracked, and there's no negotiation involved, which removes a common source of friction for first-time visitors unfamiliar with local taxi norms. Local taxis and auto-rickshaws can be cheaper in some situations and are sometimes the only option in areas with limited Uber coverage, but they typically involve more negotiation, especially for tourists who may not know the going local rate for a given route.

Safety Tips for Foreign Visitors

Before getting in, always check that the driver's name, photo, and license plate in the app match the car in front of you. Share your trip status with someone you trust using the app's built-in sharing feature, particularly for late-night rides or unfamiliar routes — a habit that pairs naturally with the location-sharing routine covered in our guide on solo female travel safety in India. Sit in the back seat rather than the front where possible, and use the in-app emergency or safety features if something feels off during a ride rather than waiting until after the trip.

Common Problems

A few recurring issues foreign travellers run into: not receiving the SMS verification code because a mobile carrier is blocking short-code messages from foreign numbers (contacting your carrier usually resolves this); a card that worked at home suddenly failing in the app, often fixed by updating the country field on the payment method; and drivers occasionally calling a masked in-app number rather than your real one, which is expected behaviour and not a technical error.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreign tourists use Uber in India?

Yes, Uber operates across major Indian cities and works with an existing international account and phone number.

Do I need an Indian phone number to use Uber in India?

No, your existing home-country number and Uber account generally work without needing a local number.

Can I use my international credit or debit card on Uber in India?

Generally yes, though Uber does not accept prepaid cards, so having a backup payment method is worth considering.

Can I pay cash for Uber rides in India?

Yes, cash is an available payment option in most areas — select it before booking and pay the driver directly at the end of the trip.

Which cities in India have Uber?

Uber operates in most major cities, including Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata, and Pune, with coverage varying in smaller towns.

Is Uber safer than local taxis for tourists in India?

Uber offers more predictability with upfront fares, driver details, and trip tracking, which many first-time visitors find easier to navigate than negotiating with local taxis.

What should I do if I don't receive the SMS verification code?

Check that your number is entered correctly, then contact your mobile carrier to confirm they're not blocking short-code messages from foreign senders.

Can I use UPI to pay for Uber rides in India?

Yes, UPI is supported as a payment method within the Uber app in India alongside cards and cash.

Do I need mobile data to use Uber while travelling in India?

Yes, you'll need an internet connection through Wi-Fi, international roaming, or a local SIM/eSIM to use the app.

What should I check before getting into an Uber in India?

Confirm the driver's name, photo, and license plate in the app match the vehicle in front of you before getting in.

Bottom Line

Uber in India works close enough to how it works everywhere else that most foreign tourists can pick it up within one ride. The differences worth knowing ahead of time are mainly around payment — cash and UPI sit alongside cards as normal options — and city coverage, which is strong in major metros but worth double-checking for smaller destinations. For a first-time visitor trying to get from the airport to a hotel without any local currency or SIM sorted yet, it's often the easiest way to start a trip to India, which is exactly why it's one of the first things the WanderLust team sets up before landing.
