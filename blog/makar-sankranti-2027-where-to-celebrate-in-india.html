Makar Sankranti 2027: Where to Celebrate in India (Kites, Pongal, Magh Mela and More)

October 28, 202612 min readby WanderLust Blog

Makar Sankranti is celebrated across India under a dozen different names, with kites in Gujarat, rice pots in Tamil Nadu and holy dips at the Magh Mela, and each place gives a very different trip.

Makar Sankranti marks the day the sun enters the zodiac sign of Capricorn and begins its northward journey. It falls in mid-January, and it is celebrated as a harvest festival across India, with regional names and customs. In Gujarat it is Uttarayan, in Tamil Nadu it is Pongal, in Punjab it is linked to Lohri, in Assam it relates to Magh Bihu, and in many places it is a day for bathing in sacred rivers. For a traveller, this means you can choose among very different experiences on the same days. This guide compares the main options and shows how to choose.

Makar Sankranti 2027 Date

Makar Sankranti is generally observed on January 14, and in some years on January 15, because it follows the solar calendar. In 2027 it is expected around January 14 to 15. Some regional festivals, such as Pongal, last several days around the date, and Gujarat adds a second day, Vasi Uttarayan. Check the local panchang and official announcements for exact timings.

Where to Celebrate: The Main Options

1. Gujarat: Uttarayan and the kite festival

Ahmedabad's old city rooftops are the heart of a joyful, competitive kite-flying festival. Choose it if you want colour, energy and food. See our Uttarayan 2027 guide, and read the safety section about kite string.

2. Tamil Nadu: Pongal

A four-day harvest festival with rice cooked in new pots, decorated courtyards and cattle celebrations. Choose it for culture, food and a family-centred experience. Read our Pongal 2027 guide.

3. Prayagraj: Magh Mela

The Magh Mela opens around Makar Sankranti, with the first major bath at the Sangam. Choose it for pilgrimage atmosphere and a large temporary city on the riverbank. See our Magh Mela 2027 guide.

4. Rajasthan: kites in Jaipur

Jaipur also celebrates with kite flying and sweets, with events at palaces and public grounds. It is easier to combine with other Rajasthan sights, though it has a less intense atmosphere than Ahmedabad.

5. Punjab and North India: Lohri and Sankranti

Lohri, celebrated the night before Makar Sankranti, is marked with bonfires, songs and winter foods in Punjab and Haryana. Choose it if you want to experience local family and community traditions through a host.

6. West Bengal: Gangasagar Mela

At the mouth of the Ganga where it meets the Bay of Bengal, pilgrims gather for a holy dip on Makar Sankranti. It is a very large pilgrimage and is best suited to experienced travellers with local help, since access involves boats and heavy crowds.

7. Assam: Magh Bihu

Magh Bihu is Assam's harvest festival, with community feasts, bonfires and traditional games, usually around January 14 to 15. It is a good choice for travellers who want a very local, food-centred experience in the north-east.

Sample Routes

Route A: Gujarat in five days

Fly into Ahmedabad, spend two nights in the old city before Uttarayan, enjoy the festival with a host or at an organised venue, and add a day in Gandhinagar or Kutch. Read our Rann Utsav guide if you want to add the white desert.

Route B: Tamil Nadu in seven days

Fly into Chennai, travel to Thanjavur or Madurai, spend Pongal with a host or at a cultural programme, and return via Chennai. Add Puducherry for a relaxed finish.

Route C: Prayagraj and Varanasi in five days

Fly into Varanasi, travel to Prayagraj for the Magh Mela, and return. Plan for cold weather and fog, and keep buffer days.

Planning Tips That Apply Everywhere

  • Book early. Mid-January is peak travel time in India, with trains and flights filling up. See our guide to using trains in India.
  • Plan for fog and cold in north India. Flights and trains are often delayed, so avoid tight connections.
  • Dress for the region. Warm layers in the north, light cotton in the south. See what to wear in India.
  • Respect local customs. Ask before photographing people and religious events.
  • Check official dates. Regional events differ by a day or two.

Food Across the Festival

Each region has its own festival foods. Sesame and jaggery sweets such as tilgul and chikki are common across western and northern India. Gujarat adds undhiyu and jalebi, Tamil Nadu has sakkarai pongal and ven pongal, Punjab has gajak and sarson ka saag, and Assam has pitha and laru. Try regional specialities and ask your host about the meaning behind them.

Safety and Responsible Travel

  • Avoid riding two-wheelers during kite festivals because of sharp string.
  • Keep a safe distance from bonfires and fireworks.
  • Stay in groups at large pilgrim gatherings and agree a meeting point.
  • Do not litter in rivers or at religious sites.
  • Read our scams guide for common traps.

More Questions Visitors Ask

Is Makar Sankranti the same everywhere?

The date is the same, but the celebration changes by region. The astronomical meaning is shared, while customs, food and names differ.

Which place is easiest for first-time visitors?

Ahmedabad for Uttarayan and Chennai or Madurai for Pongal are both accessible, though Magh Mela is more demanding.

Can I combine two festivals?

Not on the same day, but you can see one on January 14 and another a day or two later if the distance is short.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Booking late, when flights and hotels are scarce.
  • Assuming one festival style applies to all of India.
  • Ignoring fog and cold in the north.
  • Trying to cross the country in a few days.

Final Checklist

  • One region chosen for January 14 to 15, with a backup plan.
  • Accommodation and transport booked early, with flexible terms.
  • Warm or light clothing packed to match your region.
  • Official dates and local safety advice checked.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Makar Sankranti 2027?

It is expected around January 14 to 15, 2027. Confirm with a local calendar.

Where is the best place to celebrate Makar Sankranti in India?

Gujarat for kites, Tamil Nadu for Pongal and Prayagraj for the Magh Mela.

What is the difference between Pongal and Uttarayan?

Both are Makar Sankranti festivals. Pongal is a Tamil harvest festival centred on cooking rice, while Uttarayan is Gujarat's kite festival.

Is Makar Sankranti a good time to travel in India?

Yes, with advance booking and attention to fog in the north.

Which festival is the most crowded?

The Magh Mela and Gangasagar are the most crowded, with the largest pilgrim numbers.

Can tourists join the celebrations?

Yes. Visitors are welcome if they are respectful and follow local customs.
