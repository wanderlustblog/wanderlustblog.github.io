Kuldiga, Latvia: Europe's Widest Waterfall, a Brick Bridge, Wooden Old Town and a 2-Day Plan

November 12, 202612 min readby WanderLust Blog

A small town in western Latvia is home to what is often called the widest waterfall in Europe, a long brick bridge and a quiet old town of wooden houses.

Quick TakeDetails
Known forVenta Rapid (Ventas Rumba), a very wide, low waterfall
From RigaAbout 160 km, roughly two hours by road

HeritageThe old town is UNESCO-listed

Kuldīga is a small town in the Kurzeme region of western Latvia, about two hours by road from Riga. It has a compact old town of wooden and brick houses, a long historic brick bridge over the Venta River, and a waterfall, the Venta Rapid, which is commonly described as the widest waterfall in Europe. It may not be the highest, but it stretches across the whole river, creating a roaring curtain of water. In 2023 the old town was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage list as an outstanding example of a northern European small town with continuity of wooden architecture. Despite this, Kuldīga remains little known beyond Latvia and nearby countries. This guide explains why it deserves a visit, how to reach it, what to see, where to stay and how to plan two days.

The Venta Rapid (Ventas Rumba)

The Venta Rapid is a natural waterfall on the Venta River in the centre of town, about 249 metres wide and only a couple of metres high. Its main appeal is its width, and the fact that you can walk right up to it from the old brick bridge or from the riverside paths. In spring, usually around April and May, fish called vimba swim upstream to spawn and attempt to leap over the waterfall, drawing crowds who come to watch. In winter, parts of the river can freeze, giving the falls a different look. Take care near the edge, and do not climb on wet rocks. Swimming near the waterfall is not recommended.

The Old Brick Bridge

The brick bridge across the Venta, built in the nineteenth century, is one of the longest brick bridges in Europe, at around 164 metres. It is closed to heavy traffic, and is now mostly used by pedestrians and cyclists, with benches and views of the waterfall. It is the best place to start and end an evening walk, particularly at sunset when the light reflects off the water.

The Old Town

The old town has a grid of narrow streets, cobbled squares and low wooden and brick houses in soft colours, with courtyards, workshops and small cafés. Highlights include the town hall square, the church of St. Catherine, the old pharmacy, craft workshops and small museums. Wander slowly, and look for details such as carved doors, old signs and quiet courtyards. The town also hosts art events and a growing creative scene, with residencies, galleries and artisan shops. Many buildings are still lived in, so be respectful, particularly in the quieter streets.

Things to Do Around Kuldīga

  • Riverside walks: paths along the Venta lead past parkland, old mills and viewpoints. Cycling is popular, and bikes can be hired in town.
  • Alekšupīte stream and Rumba: a small, picturesque stream with a historic mill area and gardens.
  • Local museums: the town museum and small specialised museums explain the history of the Duchy of Courland, the area's trade and crafts.
  • Craft shops and workshops: pottery, textiles, woodwork and food products from local makers.
  • Day trips: Ventspils and the Baltic coast to the north, and the small town of Sabile, known for its vineyard, to the east.

A 2-Day Plan

Day 1: The town and the waterfall

Arrive from Riga, have lunch, explore the old town and walk to the Venta Rapid. Cross the bridge and walk along the river, then enjoy dinner in a local restaurant. Return to the bridge in the evening light.

Day 2: Slow morning and a day trip

Spend the morning in the craft shops and museums, hire a bike and ride along the river, and take a short trip to the coast or the countryside in the afternoon before returning to Riga.

How to Reach Kuldīga

The town is roughly 160 kilometres west of Riga, and the journey takes about two hours by car, with regular buses from the main bus station. A taxi or hire car gives flexibility to explore the surrounding countryside. Check bus timetables in advance, since they vary by season and may be limited on weekends. If you fly in to Riga, you can combine Kuldīga with a few days in the capital and other places in Latvia and the Baltics. In winter, check road conditions and daylight hours.

Where to Stay and What to Eat

Kuldīga has boutique guesthouses and small hotels in restored wooden houses, as well as apartments and hostels. Staying in the old town places you within a short walk of the bridge, the waterfall and the restaurants. Book early for summer weekends and local festivals, and check cancellation terms. Local cuisine includes rye bread, smoked fish, potatoes, mushrooms and berries, dairy products, grey peas with bacon and traditional pastries. Cafés serve coffee and cake in a relaxed way. Vegetarian options are available in many places, particularly in summer.

Best Time to Visit

April and May are good for the jumping fish and fresh spring greenery, but weather can be changeable. June to September has longer days, warmer temperatures and outdoor events, with long evenings in midsummer. October brings autumn colours. Winter is cold and dark, but the town can be magical under snow, and the river and waterfall can look dramatic when frozen. Dress for the cold if you visit between November and March.

Entry Rules for Indian Travellers

Latvia is in the Schengen area, so Indian travellers need a Schengen visa for short stays. Check the official visa application process for the country you plan to apply through, and the required documents and insurance; see our visa guide and insurance guide. Because the process takes time, apply early and keep refundable bookings until your visa is confirmed.

What to Pack

Comfortable walking shoes, layers, a waterproof jacket, an umbrella and a camera. In winter, bring thermal layers, insulated boots, gloves and a hat. In summer, bring mosquito repellent for riverside walks. Carry a reusable water bottle and a small day bag. Latvia uses the euro, and cards are widely accepted, though small shops may prefer cash.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Latvia is often cheaper than western Europe, but costs vary by season. Build your estimate from flights, transport to Kuldīga, accommodation, meals, bike hire, museum entry, insurance, visa fees and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare options for your dates, and check what is included in accommodation.

More Questions Travellers Ask

Is the Venta Rapid really the widest waterfall in Europe?

It is commonly described that way, thanks to its width of around 249 metres. It is not a tall waterfall.

Can I visit Kuldīga as a day trip from Riga?

Yes, but staying one night lets you enjoy the old town in the evening and morning light.

Is Kuldīga suitable for families?

Yes. It is small, safe and walkable, with parks and bike paths, and children often enjoy the waterfall and the bridge.

How many days do I need?

One night is a minimum, and two days lets you slow down and add a day trip.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Expecting a tall, dramatic waterfall instead of a very wide one.
  • Coming only for an hour and missing the evening atmosphere.
  • Not checking bus timetables, particularly on weekends.
  • Climbing on wet rocks near the falls.
  • Not applying for the Schengen visa early.

Final Checklist

  • Schengen visa, insurance and transport arranged.
  • Accommodation booked in the old town, with flexible terms.
  • Comfortable shoes and weather-appropriate layers packed.
  • A plan for the waterfall and the bridge at sunset.

About 249 metres, though only a couple of metres high.
