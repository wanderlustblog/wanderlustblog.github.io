Ushguli and Svaneti, Georgia: Stone Towers, Mountain Hikes, How to Get There and a 5-Day Plan

November 12, 202614 min readby WanderLust Blog

At the top of Georgia's Caucasus, a cluster of villages with medieval stone towers sits beneath a glacier, and it is often described as one of the highest permanently inhabited places in Europe.

Svaneti is a remote region in the northwest of Georgia, in the high Caucasus mountains. Its main villages, Mestia and Ushguli, are known for their Svan towers, tall medieval stone defensive towers that rise above clusters of stone houses. Upper Svaneti is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and the village of Ushguli, at about 2,100 metres, is often described as one of the highest permanently inhabited settlements in Europe, standing beneath the glaciers of Shkhara, Georgia's highest peak at over 5,000 metres. It is a spectacular, quiet and little-visited destination, reached by long mountain roads, and offers some of Europe's most impressive mountain scenery and cultural heritage. This guide explains how to reach Ushguli, when to go, what to see, where to stay, which hikes to consider and how to plan a five-day trip.

Why Svaneti Is a Hidden Location

Georgia is becoming better known as a travel destination, but most visitors focus on Tbilisi, the wine regions and the more accessible mountain town of Kazbegi. Svaneti requires more effort to reach, and facilities are simple, so it still sees a fraction of the visitors. The reward is an unusual landscape: villages that have preserved their towers, churches with medieval frescoes, high alpine meadows and a strong Svan identity, with a distinct language and customs. It is a place to slow down, walk and talk to the people who live there.

The Svan Towers and the Villages

Svan towers were built from the ninth to the twelfth centuries and used as family refuges in times of conflict and as symbols of status. Many are still standing, usually four or five storeys high, and some can be climbed. In Mestia, the main town, you can see towers among the houses, visit the museum of history and ethnography, and look at manuscripts and icons. In Ushguli, a collection of hamlets including Chazhashi, the towers cluster along the valley with the glaciers in the background. The twelfth-century Lamaria church, on a hill above the village, offers some of the best views. Dress modestly in churches, and ask before you photograph people.

How to Reach Svaneti and Ushguli

Most visitors begin in Tbilisi. The journey to Mestia takes many hours by road, usually by marshrutka (shared minibus), private car or a tour, via Kutaisi and Zugdidi. In some seasons, there are domestic flights between Tbilisi and Mestia, but schedules are seasonal and subject to weather, so check current availability. From Mestia, the road to Ushguli is roughly 45 kilometres long, much of it unpaved or rough in places, and takes two to three hours depending on conditions. A 4WD vehicle with an experienced driver is strongly recommended, and shared transport runs in the summer season. Roads can be closed by snow and landslides, so check conditions, and allow spare days in your plan.

Best Time to Visit

  • June to September: the main season, when the roads and trails are open, guesthouses are operating and the meadows are green. July and August are warmest and busiest, but still quiet by European standards.
  • May and October: possible but weather is variable, some trails may be snowy and some services are limited.
  • Winter: Mestia has a ski area and some visitors come for winter sports, but the road to Ushguli can be closed or dangerous, and services are limited. It is only for experienced winter travellers.

Hiking Around Svaneti

Svaneti is a major trekking region, with options for all levels.

  • Mestia to Ushguli trek: a multi-day hike of about four days through high passes and villages, with overnight stays in guesthouses. It requires good fitness, careful planning, a map or guide and favourable weather.
  • Shkhara glacier hike: a day walk from Ushguli to the base of the glacier, taking several hours return, with views of the mountain. Go in good weather, and follow the trail carefully.
  • Chalaadi glacier: an easier day walk from Mestia to a glacier viewpoint, suitable for most fit walkers.
  • Koruldi lakes: a hike or drive above Mestia to alpine lakes with views of Mount Ushba, a dramatic double-peaked mountain.

Mountain weather changes quickly, with afternoon storms in summer. Start early, carry layers and water, and consider hiring a local guide for multi-day treks. For altitude and safety advice on high trails, see our Everest Base Camp guide.

A 5-Day Plan

Day 1: Travel to Mestia

Take an early morning journey from Tbilisi or Kutaisi to Mestia. Arrive in the evening, check in and have dinner at a guesthouse.

Day 2: Mestia and Chalaadi glacier

Visit the museum, climb a tower and walk to Chalaadi glacier in the afternoon. Relax in the evening.

Day 3: Travel to Ushguli

Take the road to Ushguli, stopping at viewpoints and villages. Walk to Lamaria church and the Chazhashi tower cluster in the late afternoon.

Day 4: Shkhara glacier walk

Hike toward Shkhara glacier in the morning, and spend the afternoon in the village. Eat dinner with your host family.

Day 5: Return

Return to Mestia and onward, leaving plenty of time in case of delays. If possible, add an extra night for buffer.

Where to Stay and What to Eat

Svaneti has family-run guesthouses with simple rooms, home-cooked meals and warm hospitality. Hosts typically serve dinner and breakfast, often a large spread of local dishes. Try kubdari, a Svan meat pie, Svan salt (a seasoned salt used in many dishes), khachapuri (cheese-filled bread), pickled vegetables, mountain honey and locally made cheese. Vegetarian meals are possible but ask in advance. Accommodation is limited in peak season, so book early, confirm what is included and carry cash in lari, since cards may not work in villages. Mobile coverage is limited in remote areas.

Entry Rules for Indian Travellers

Entry rules for Georgia depend on your nationality and any other visas you hold. Indian travellers have generally been able to apply for an electronic visa, and there may be conditions linked to holding certain other visas, so check the official Georgian government e-visa portal and embassy for current rules. Safety and Practical Tips

  • Hire a reliable driver for the Ushguli road, and avoid driving yourself unless you have mountain experience.
  • Check weather and road conditions with your guesthouse before you set off.
  • Carry cash, a power bank, a head torch, a first-aid kit and layers.
  • Do not trek alone on remote passes. Tell your host your route and expected return time.
  • Respect livestock and sheepdogs, and keep a calm distance.

Etiquette and Responsible Travel

Ask before photographing people and their homes. Dress modestly in churches, and cover your shoulders and knees. Accept hospitality graciously, and bring a small gift if you are invited to a home. Do not climb on towers or ruins unless it is clearly allowed, and do not leave litter. Buy from local producers, pay fair prices for guides and rooms, and spend time in the villages rather than rushing through.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Georgia is generally affordable, but remote travel costs more in time and transport. Build your own estimate from flights to Tbilisi, transport to Mestia and Ushguli, guesthouse nights with meals, guides, insurance, a visa if needed and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare shared and private transport, and check what is included.

More Questions Travellers Ask

Is Ushguli really the highest village in Europe?

It is often described that way, though claims about the highest inhabited settlement depend on definitions. What matters is that it is very high, remote and beautiful.

Can I visit Ushguli in a day from Mestia?

It is possible with an early start, but it makes for a long day on rough roads. Staying a night gives you much more time.

Is Svaneti safe?

Generally yes, though the main risks are mountain weather, rough roads and remote hiking. Check conditions and use local advice.

How many days do I need?

Four to five days for Mestia and Ushguli, plus travel from Tbilisi.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Visiting outside the season without checking road conditions.
  • Underestimating the travel time from Tbilisi.
  • Attempting the Ushguli road without a suitable vehicle.
  • Not carrying enough cash.
  • Planning a tight schedule with no spare day.

Final Checklist

  • Entry rules and insurance checked, and guesthouses booked.
  • Driver or shared transport arranged, with a spare day.
  • Layers, hiking shoes, cash and power bank packed.
  • Weather and road conditions confirmed before each leg.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where is Ushguli?

In Upper Svaneti, in the Caucasus mountains of northwest Georgia, at about 2,100 metres.

How do I get from Mestia to Ushguli?

By a roughly 45 km road that takes two to three hours, usually in a 4WD vehicle with an experienced driver.

When is the best time to visit Svaneti?

June to September, when the roads and trails are open and guesthouses are operating.

What are Svan towers?

Medieval stone defensive towers, usually four or five storeys high, built from the ninth to twelfth centuries.

Can I hike from Mestia to Ushguli?

Yes. It is a multi-day trek that needs good fitness, planning and favourable weather. A local guide is recommended.

Do Indian travellers need a visa for Georgia?

Indian travellers have generally been able to apply for an e-visa, but rules change, so check the official portal.
