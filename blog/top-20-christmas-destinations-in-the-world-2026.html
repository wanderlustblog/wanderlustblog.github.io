Top 20 Christmas Destinations in the World 2026: Markets, Snow, Beaches and Cities

Twenty places, four kinds of Christmas: traditional markets, Arctic snow, city lights and warm-weather escapes, with links to our full guides for planning.

Where is the best place to spend Christmas in 2026? The honest answer is that it depends on what you want, but there are a handful of cities and regions that keep appearing on travellers' wish lists for good reasons. This list gives you twenty options across Europe, the Arctic, North America, Africa and Asia, with one short reason for each and links to our detailed guides. Use it to build a shortlist of three or four, then compare costs, flight times and weather before you book.

How We Chose This List

We balanced five things: how strong the Christmas atmosphere is, how easy it is to travel to, how good the weather is for the style of trip, what there is to do besides Christmas events and how practical the destination is for a first visit. We included a mix of traditional markets, snow, city lights and warm-weather options, so you can compare very different trips. The order loosely groups similar places, and it is not a strict ranking. 1. Vienna, Austria

Best for: grand markets and coffee houses.

Vienna's markets in front of the town hall and palaces combine music, crafts and classic Austrian food. It is elegant and busy on weekends, and ideal for a three-night trip with museums and cafés.

2. Strasbourg, France

Best for: a compact old town and a classic market district.

Called the Capital of Christmas, Strasbourg spreads its markets through the old town around the cathedral. It is easy to explore on foot and good for a long weekend.

3. Prague, Czech Republic

Best for: fairytale squares and good value.

Prague's Old Town Square and Wenceslas Square hold large markets with traditional food and crafts. It is usually more affordable than Vienna, but very crowded on weekends.

4. Budapest, Hungary

Best for: markets plus thermal baths.

Budapest combines a lively market in front of the basilica with thermal baths, ruin bars and good food. It is a good pick for travellers who want a warmer break in the middle of a cold trip.

5. Cologne, Germany

Best for: cathedral atmosphere.

Cologne's cathedral setting and many themed markets make it a favourite, and its rail links make it easy to combine with other German cities. See our top 5 German Christmas markets.

6. Nuremberg, Germany

Best for: tradition and gingerbread.

Nuremberg's Christkindlesmarkt is among the most traditional in Europe, with lebkuchen and sausages as local specialities.

7. Bruges, Belgium

Best for: storybook canals and small-scale charm.

Bruges has a small, atmospheric market in a medieval square and a canal-side old town. It is best on weekdays, because tour buses fill it on weekends.

8. Edinburgh, Scotland

Best for: a castle backdrop and a long festive season.

Edinburgh's markets, fairground rides and Hogmanay celebrations make it a great winter break. It is cold and wet, so pack waterproofs. See our Hogmanay guide.

9. London, England

Best for: lights, shows and winter walks.

London has famous festive lights, markets, ice rinks and theatre. It is expensive, but offers a lot to do on a cold weekend.

10. Copenhagen, Denmark

Best for: cosy hygge and an amusement park in lights.

Copenhagen's Tivoli Gardens opens a Christmas season with lights and stalls, and the city's cafés and candlelit streets suit couples. Winter is dark and cold.

11. Salzburg, Austria

Best for: music, mountains and Christmas carol heritage.

Salzburg's old town and fortress, together with its musical heritage, make it an elegant Christmas stop, easily combined with Munich or Vienna.

12. Rovaniemi, Finland

Best for: snow, Santa and families.

Rovaniemi is the classic Lapland destination, with huskies, reindeer and a good chance of northern lights. Read our Rovaniemi guide.

13. Tromsø, Norway

Best for: northern lights and Arctic adventure.

Tromsø is an Arctic city with strong aurora tours, wildlife trips and mountain views. See our Rovaniemi vs Tromsø comparison.

14. Quebec City, Canada

Best for: a snowy old town with European charm.

Quebec City's walled old town, German-style market and heavy snowfall give it a postcard Christmas. Dress for very cold weather.

15. New York City, USA

Best for: iconic lights and winter city life.

New York offers store windows, a giant tree, skating rinks and Broadway shows. Hotels are expensive, and crowds are heavy in Midtown.

16. Cape Town, South Africa

Best for: a summer Christmas with beaches and wine.

Cape Town combines mountain, ocean and wine farms in warm weather. See our Cape Town Christmas guide.

17. Dubai, UAE

Best for: sunny weather and festive brunches.

Dubai offers warm days, beaches, desert trips and hotel brunches. See our Dubai Christmas guide.

18. Singapore

Best for: lights in the tropics.

Singapore pairs Orchard Road's light display with warm, humid weather and excellent food. See our Singapore guide.

19. Tokyo, Japan

Best for: illuminations and a different kind of Christmas.

Tokyo's illuminations and Christmas Eve traditions are unlike anywhere else, with fried chicken and strawberry cake as local customs. See our Japan Christmas guide.

20. Goa, India

Best for: beaches and midnight mass.

Goa blends warm beaches with centuries-old churches and festive food. See our Goa Christmas guide.

Planning Tips That Apply to Every Place on This List

Choose the experience first. Decide whether you want snow, markets, beaches or a city break, and then pick a destination, not the reverse.

Compare total cost. Look at flights, hotels, local transport, food and activities for the same dates and travellers.

Book hotels first. Rooms sell out before flights in popular places, so secure accommodation with free cancellation.

Check closures. December 24, 25 and 26 are quiet or closed in many places, so book Christmas meals early.

Pack for the climate. Cold destinations need real winter clothing; see our packing list. Warm destinations need sun protection and light layers.

Leave buffer days. Winter weather and holiday crowds cause delays, particularly in the Arctic and in snowy cities.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Choosing a famous destination for the exact peak days without checking the cost.
  • Booking a warm destination in its rainy season.
  • Forgetting travel insurance and cancellation cover.
  • Overpacking the itinerary and leaving no rest days.
  • Waiting until December to book flights and hotels.

How to Narrow It Down to One

If you want a classic European Christmas, pick between Vienna, Strasbourg, Prague or Nuremberg. If you want snow and northern lights, choose Rovaniemi or Tromsø. If you want a warm Christmas, choose Dubai, Cape Town, Singapore or Goa. If you want a big-city break, choose London, New York or Tokyo. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best place in the world to spend Christmas?

It depends on your preferences: Vienna or Strasbourg for markets, Rovaniemi for snow, and Cape Town, Dubai or Goa for warm weather.

Where is the best place for a white Christmas?

Rovaniemi, Tromsø and Quebec City are reliable for snow, though no destination is guaranteed.

Where can I go for a warm Christmas?

Cape Town, Dubai, Singapore and Goa are good warm-weather options.

When should I book?

As early as possible. Late October to mid-November is a strong window for flights, and hotels sell out earlier.

Which is cheapest?

Prague, Budapest and Goa are generally better value than London, New York or Tokyo, but prices change each year.

How many days do I need?

A long weekend works for a nearby city, and a week suits Arctic or long-haul trips.
