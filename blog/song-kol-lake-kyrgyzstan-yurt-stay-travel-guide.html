Song-Kol Lake Kyrgyzstan: Yurt Stays, Horse Treks, Best Time and How to Get There

November 12, 202613 min readby WanderLust Blog

At about 3,000 metres, a quiet alpine lake sits in a bowl of green pastures where shepherd families put up felt yurts every summer, and visitors can sleep among their horses and flocks.

Song-Kol (also written Song-Köl or Son-Kul) is a high alpine lake in the Tien Shan mountains of central Kyrgyzstan. It is the second largest lake in the country after Issyk-Kul, but it feels nothing like a tourist resort. Around it are wide summer pastures, known as jailoo, where herding families bring horses, sheep and yaks from the lowlands and live in yurts for a few months. Travellers come to sleep in a yurt, ride a horse along the shore, eat home-cooked food and see a sky full of stars. Kyrgyzstan itself is often described in travel trend reports as an under-the-radar alternative to more crowded destinations, and Song-Kol is one of its best known hidden places. This guide explains when to go, how to get there, what a yurt stay is really like, what to pack and how to travel respectfully.

Why Song-Kol Counts as a Hidden Location

The lake is far from any city, with no hotel chains, no mobile coverage in most places and a road that is closed by snow for much of the year. Visitor numbers are still modest compared with famous mountain destinations, and many people who come are on small-group or independent trips. The experience is simple: space, silence and hospitality. It is not a luxury trip. Facilities are basic, nights are cold and plans change with weather. If you want comfort and certainty, choose another destination. If you enjoy simple travel and landscapes, this is one of the most memorable places in Central Asia.

When to Go

Song-Kol is accessible mainly from about June to September, when snow has melted from the passes and families have moved to the pastures. July and August are the warmest and most popular months, with the greenest pastures and the most active camps. June and September are quieter, and also colder and less predictable. Outside the summer season, the area is under snow and the camps are closed. Even in July, temperatures can drop below freezing at night at this altitude, and rain, hail and strong wind can arrive quickly. Check local conditions with your host or tour operator before you travel, since roads can be affected by weather.

How to Reach Song-Kol

Most visitors start in Bishkek, the capital, and travel by road to Kochkor, a small town that serves as the main base for the lake, which takes several hours. From Kochkor, a rough mountain road climbs to the lake, and the final section usually needs a 4WD vehicle with an experienced driver. There are several routes, including one over a high pass from the north and another from the Naryn side to the south, and some visitors travel one way and return by another. Allow a full day for the journey from Kochkor and expect bumpy roads, steep sections and stops for livestock. Hiking and horse-trekking routes also lead to the lake from villages, taking several days and requiring good preparation and a guide. Independent driving is possible but not advised unless you are experienced in mountain conditions.

What a Yurt Stay Is Really Like

  • The yurt: a round tent with a wooden frame covered in thick felt, with colourful carpets and mattresses on the floor. Guests usually sleep on thick blankets in a shared yurt, with groups in the same space.
  • Meals: simple home-cooked food, often including bread, soup, noodles, dumplings, dairy products, jam and tea. Vegetarian meals are possible but may be limited, so tell your host in advance. You may be offered kymyz, a fermented mare's milk drink, which is an acquired taste.
  • Toilets and washing: basic outdoor toilets and limited washing facilities. Bring wet wipes and hand sanitiser, and do not expect hot showers.
  • Electricity: little or none, sometimes a small solar panel. Bring a power bank and a head torch.
  • The atmosphere: hospitable and relaxed, with horse riding, walks, evening chats and a very dark sky.

Community-Based Tourism and Choosing a Host

Kyrgyzstan has a network of community-based tourism organisations that connect visitors with local families and small camps and help to share income locally. Booking through a community tourism office or a reputable local operator can make arrangements simpler and fairer. Ask what is included in the price, such as meals, horses, transport and guides, whether tipping is expected and how you will pay, since cash in the local currency is usually needed and cards do not work at the lake. Read recent reviews, and confirm the details in writing before you travel.

Things to Do Around the Lake

  • Horse riding: the classic activity, from short rides to multi-day treks. Ask about the horse's temperament and your own experience level, and wear sturdy shoes.
  • Walking: gentle hikes along the shore and to nearby viewpoints. Allow for altitude, which can make even easy walks feel tiring.
  • Birdwatching: the lake attracts water birds in summer, and the pastures are home to marmots and other wildlife.
  • Evening culture: some camps offer demonstrations of traditional crafts, music or games, though you should not expect a staged show.
  • Stargazing: with almost no light pollution, the night sky is spectacular on clear nights.

A 3-Day Plan

Day 1: Bishkek to Kochkor

Drive from Bishkek to Kochkor, stopping along the way for views and a meal. Visit the local craft shops in Kochkor, which sell felt items made by village women, and meet your driver or guide.

Day 2: Kochkor to the lake

Drive up to Song-Kol in the morning, check in to your yurt camp, have lunch and ride or walk in the afternoon. Watch the sunset, and eat dinner with your host family.

Day 3: Morning at the lake and return

Enjoy the early morning, when the light on the lake is soft, then drive back via a different pass if possible. Allow plenty of time for the return journey, since weather can delay you.

If you have a few more days, combine Song-Kol with Issyk-Kul, Karakol and the Altyn-Arashan valley for a longer Kyrgyzstan route.

Altitude, Cold and Health

At about 3,000 metres, some people feel mild altitude symptoms such as headache, breathlessness or poor sleep. Take it easy on the first day, drink plenty of water, avoid alcohol and let your body adjust. Nights are cold, so bring a warm sleeping bag liner, thermal layers, a hat and gloves, even in summer. Medical help is far away, so bring a basic first-aid kit and any personal medication, and buy travel insurance that covers remote travel and evacuation; see our guide on international travel insurance for Indians. For more on altitude, see the safety section in our Everest Base Camp guide.

Entry Rules and Money

Entry rules for Kyrgyzstan depend on your nationality, and many visitors can apply for an electronic visa or enter visa-free for a limited period, so check the official government site well before booking. Carry some cash in the local currency, the som, since rural areas do not accept cards, and keep small notes for tips and snacks. See our guide to visa-free countries for Indian passport holders for how to check entry rules properly.

Packing List

  • Warm layers, a windproof and waterproof jacket, a warm hat and gloves.
  • Thermal underwear and a sleeping bag liner.
  • Sturdy walking shoes and spare socks.
  • Sun protection: sunscreen, sunglasses and a hat. UV is strong at altitude.
  • Power bank, head torch and spare batteries.
  • Wet wipes, hand sanitiser, toilet paper and a small first-aid kit.
  • Cash, snacks, a reusable water bottle and any dietary supplies.

Etiquette and Responsible Travel

  • Accept hospitality graciously. Take off your shoes before entering a yurt, and do not step on the threshold.
  • Ask before photographing people, their animals or inside their yurt.
  • Do not litter, and carry out your waste where there are no bins.
  • Buy crafts and food directly from local families, and pay a fair price.
  • Respect livestock, keep a distance from sheepdogs and do not feed animals.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Costs depend on the operator, the group size and the season. Build a list of flights to Bishkek, transport to Kochkor and the lake, yurt stay with meals, horse hire, guide fees, insurance, tips, a visa if needed and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare quotes from two or three operators for the same dates, and check what is included.

More Questions Travellers Ask

Is Song-Kol suitable for beginners?

Yes, for travellers who are comfortable with basic conditions and a bumpy road. The yurt stay does not need special skills, but the altitude and cold require preparation.

Can I visit Song-Kol in winter?

Generally no. The passes are under snow, and the camps are closed.

Is it safe for solo travellers?

Many people visit alone or in small groups, but it is remote. Go with a reputable host or guide, share your plans and carry a charged power bank.

How many days do I need?

Two nights at the lake is ideal, with a day each way from Bishkek if you are short on time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Going outside the summer season without checking snow conditions.
  • Underestimating the cold at night and packing too little.
  • Not carrying enough cash.
  • Attempting the road without a suitable vehicle and driver.
  • Treating the camps as a show instead of someone's home.

Final Checklist

  • Season, road conditions and entry rules checked.
  • Yurt stay, transport and meals confirmed in writing.
  • Warm layers, power bank, cash and first-aid kit packed.
  • Insurance that covers remote travel arranged.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the best time to visit Song-Kol?

July and August are the most popular and warmest months, with June and September quieter and colder. The area is closed by snow outside summer.

How do I get to Song-Kol Lake?

Usually by road from Bishkek to Kochkor, then by 4WD up a mountain road to the lake.

What is a yurt stay like?

You sleep on mattresses in a felt tent, eat home-cooked meals and use basic outdoor toilets, usually without hot showers or reliable electricity.

Is there mobile coverage at Song-Kol?

Coverage is poor or absent in most places, so download maps and tell someone your plans.

Do I need cash?

Yes. Cards do not work at the lake, so carry local currency for camps, horses and tips.

Is the altitude a problem?

At about 3,000 metres, some people feel mild symptoms. Go slowly, drink water and avoid alcohol.
