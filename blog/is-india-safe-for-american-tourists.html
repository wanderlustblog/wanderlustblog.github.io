Is India Safe for American Tourists? What the State Department Advisory Actually Says

September 4, 20266 min readby WanderLust Blog

The United States is actually India's single largest source of foreign visitors, so most of what an American traveler needs to know is the same as any first-timer — but two things are worth understanding specifically: what the State Department advisory actually rates, and how the long flight should shape your itinerary.

What the State Department Advisory Actually Says

The US State Department currently rates India at Level 2: Exercise Increased Caution — the same level assigned to France, Italy, and Germany. This is a common rating for popular international destinations and does not mean travel is discouraged; it reflects general advice to stay alert to crime and, in specific border and conflict-affected regions, more serious risks.

A few things worth understanding about how the advisory actually works:

  • The national Level 2 rating applies broadly, but specific areas — parts of Jammu and Kashmir, Manipur, and regions along the India–Pakistan border, along with several northeastern states — carry higher, more specific warnings. These areas are generally well outside standard first-time tourist itineraries like the Golden Triangle, Rajasthan, or Kerala.
  • The advisory recommends enrolling in the Smart Traveler Enrollment Program (STEP), a free US government service that sends security updates and helps the embassy reach you in an emergency.
  • Solo travel, particularly for women, is flagged with additional caution in current guidance — worth reading in full on the official advisory page for your specific itinerary rather than relying on a summary. Our own guide on solo female travel safety in India covers the ground-level detail behind that caution.
  • Ordinary precautions — securing valuables at airports, being cautious with ATMs, avoiding unmarked transport late at night — cover most of the realistic day-to-day risk for tourists on a standard circuit.

Check the current advisory directly at travel.state.gov before you finalize your itinerary, since it's periodically updated and region-specific guidance can shift.

Getting There: The Flight Actually Matters

Direct flights connect several major US hubs — including New York, Chicago, and San Francisco — to Delhi and Mumbai, with connecting itineraries covering the rest of the country from there. Given the flight time (typically well over 14 hours nonstop, longer with a connection), building in a lighter first day or two to recover from jet lag is a sensible way to start the trip rather than diving straight into a packed itinerary.

This is also why, for American travelers specifically, we'd lean toward the longer end of a first-timer's itinerary: given the distance, 10 to 14 days is a more practical range than a quick week, allowing time to adjust to jet lag alongside actual sightseeing.
