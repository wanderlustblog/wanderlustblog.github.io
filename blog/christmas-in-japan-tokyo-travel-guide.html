Christmas in Japan: Tokyo Illuminations, Traditions, KFC and a 4-Day Plan

October 26, 202612 min readby WanderLust Blog

Christmas in Japan is not a public holiday, but it is one of the most beautifully lit times of year, with illuminations, romantic dinners and traditions that are entirely Japanese.

Christmas in Japan is a secular, commercial and romantic festival rather than a family religious holiday. Cities are decorated with lights and Christmas trees, shops sell Christmas cake and special sets, and couples go out on Christmas Eve. For visitors, it is a good time to see the country at its most glittering, as long as you understand what is open, what is booked out and what the traditions mean. This guide explains how Japan celebrates, where to see the best illuminations, how to plan food and reservations and how to avoid New Year closures.

How Japan Celebrates Christmas

Christmas Day is a normal working day, and most offices and schools operate as usual. Christmas Eve is the main evening, often treated as a date night for couples, with restaurants and hotels fully booked. Families may have a small celebration at home with Christmas cake and chicken. The main national holiday season is New Year, not Christmas, so the days between December 25 and December 31 are a transition into the busiest travel and holiday period of the year.

Traditions to Know About

  • Christmas cake: a light sponge cake with whipped cream and strawberries, widely sold in bakeries and convenience stores.
  • Fried chicken: thanks to a famous marketing campaign decades ago, many Japanese families order fried chicken from KFC for Christmas, often by pre-order weeks in advance. Expect long queues at KFC around December 24.
  • Illuminations: light displays that start in November and continue into the new year, in parks, shopping districts and along streets.
  • Gift-giving: couples exchange gifts, and children may receive gifts at home, but it is less central than in Western countries.

Best Christmas Illuminations in Tokyo

Tokyo has many illumination spots, and the themes and dates change each year. Popular areas include Marunouchi, near Tokyo Station, with a tree-lined avenue of lights, the Roppongi area, Shibuya and Omotesando, plus waterfront locations such as Odaiba and Shiodome. Many shopping complexes put up large Christmas trees and indoor displays. Check each venue's official website for 2026 dates and hours, and go on weekdays if you want a quieter visit. Illuminations are generally free, though some special venues charge entry.

Kobe Luminarie and Other Cities

The Kobe Luminarie is a famous light festival held in December, created in memory of the 1995 Hanshin earthquake. Elaborate arches and lanterns fill a historic area, and crowds are very large, with controlled entry. Check the official programme for 2026. Other cities, including Osaka and Yokohama, also hold light events and Christmas markets, so you can combine them with a route through Japan.

Christmas Markets in Japan

Several cities hold German-style Christmas markets, with mulled wine, sausages and decorations. Yokohama's Red Brick Warehouse market and Tokyo's markets in areas such as Roppongi and Hibiya are popular. They are smaller than European markets, but atmospheric, particularly in the evening. A 4-Day Tokyo Christmas Plan

Day 1: Arrive and walk Marunouchi

Arrive, check in and rest, and then visit Tokyo Station and the Marunouchi lights after dark.

Day 2: Shibuya, Harajuku and Omotesando

Visit shrines and shops in the morning, and see Omotesando and Shibuya's lights in the evening. Book dinner in advance.

Day 3: Day trip or Yokohama

Take a train to Yokohama for the Christmas market and harbour views, or choose another day trip such as Kamakura. Return to Tokyo in the evening.

Day 4: Christmas Eve or Day

Choose a quiet museum or garden by day. If you go out on Christmas Eve, reserve a restaurant weeks ahead and plan your return trains, since stations are busy.

What Is Open and What Is Closed

On December 24 and 25, most shops, restaurants, museums and public transport operate normally. The key problem is reservations: popular restaurants and hotel dinners sell out weeks in advance on December 24. The bigger closure period is from about December 29 to January 3, when many businesses, restaurants, markets and some attractions close for the New Year holiday, and transport is crowded on the days before and after. If you travel then, check opening days for each place, book trains early and consider convenience stores and large department stores as backup options.

Getting Around and Practical Tips

Tokyo's trains and subways are efficient but busy, particularly in the evening. Use a prepaid transport card or a phone wallet for fares. Allow extra time around Christmas lights areas, where stations and streets are crowded. Download offline maps and a train app; see our offline maps guide. Check Japan's entry requirements and any digital arrival procedures for your nationality before you book.

Where to Stay

Stay near a major station such as Tokyo, Shinjuku or Shibuya for easy access to transport and illuminations. Hotels in these areas are pricey around Christmas, so book early. Business hotels are a practical option, but rooms are small. Check cancellation terms, and ask whether luggage can be stored if you arrive early or leave late.

What to Wear and Pack

December in Tokyo is cold and dry, with cool nights and occasional rain, and much colder in northern regions. Pack thermal layers, a warm coat, a scarf, gloves, comfortable shoes and a small umbrella. For northern Japan, add snow boots. Our winter packing list has a good layering system.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Prices rise around Christmas and New Year. Build your budget from flights, hotel nights, trains, food, activities, gifts and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare hotels in two or three neighbourhoods, and check whether breakfast is included. Free illuminations keep costs down, while special dinners and Christmas Eve hotel packages can be very expensive.

More Questions Visitors Ask

Is Christmas a good time to visit Japan?

Yes, if you like lights, food and cold, clear weather, and you plan around reservations and New Year closures.

Do I need to book restaurants for Christmas Eve?

Yes. Popular places book out weeks in advance. Have a backup plan, such as a department store food hall or a casual restaurant.

Can I see snow at Christmas in Japan?

In northern regions such as Hokkaido, yes. Tokyo rarely has snow in December.

Is it expensive?

Hotels and special dinners are costly, but transport, convenience-store food and free illuminations keep the rest reasonable.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming Christmas Day is a holiday, and planning around closures that do not exist.
  • Not booking Christmas Eve restaurants and hotels early.
  • Travelling between December 29 and January 3 without checking closures.
  • Wearing light clothing and underestimating the cold nights.

Final Checklist

  • Illumination dates and opening hours checked on official sites.
  • Hotels, trains and Christmas Eve dinner booked, with cancellation terms.
  • Warm layers, comfortable shoes, transport card and offline maps ready.
  • New Year closure dates noted if your trip continues into January.

A Simple Reservation Strategy for Christmas Eve

If you want a proper Christmas Eve dinner in Japan, reserve as early as the restaurant allows, often one to two months ahead, and confirm any set-menu rules or deposits. Many restaurants offer only special set courses on that night, so check the price and what is included. If you miss out, book an early or late seating, or eat Christmas cake and fried chicken from a shop and enjoy the lights outside. Department store food halls and convenience stores sell festive food at all hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Christmas a public holiday in Japan?

No. December 25 is a normal working day in Japan, though cities are decorated and Christmas Eve is popular for couples.

Why do people in Japan eat KFC at Christmas?

It is a tradition that grew from a successful marketing campaign decades ago, and many families pre-order a Christmas bucket.

Where can I see Christmas illuminations in Tokyo?

Marunouchi, Roppongi, Shibuya, Omotesando and Odaiba are popular, plus Yokohama and Kobe for other cities.

Are shops and restaurants open on Christmas Day?

Yes, in most cases. Reservations are the bigger issue on December 24.

When do New Year closures start?

Many businesses close from about December 29 to January 3, so check opening days carefully.

How many days do I need?

Four days covers Tokyo's main lights and a day trip.
