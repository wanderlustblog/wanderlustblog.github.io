Pingxi Sky Lantern Festival 2027 Taiwan Guide: Dates, Shifen, Getting There and Responsible Tips

November 13, 202612 min readby WanderLust Blog

In a narrow valley east of Taipei, a railway line cuts through a village street where visitors write wishes on paper lanterns and release them into the night sky.

The Pingxi district, in New Taipei City in northern Taiwan, is famous for its sky lanterns. Visitors write wishes on large paper lanterns, light them and release them into the air, often next to an old railway line where trains pass slowly through the street. The scene is photogenic, communal and symbolic, and it is one of the best-known festival experiences in Taiwan. Once a year, around the Lantern Festival at the end of the Lunar New Year season, an official Pingxi Sky Lantern Festival is held, with mass releases, performances and many visitors. This guide explains the expected dates for 2027, how to get to Pingxi from Taipei, how the lantern release works, how to enjoy the area outside festival days, and how to take part responsibly.

Dates: When Is the Festival in 2027?

The Lantern Festival falls on the fifteenth day of the first month of the lunar calendar, the final day of the Lunar New Year celebrations. In 2027, Lunar New Year is on February 6, so the Lantern Festival is expected around February 20. The Pingxi Sky Lantern Festival is organised by local authorities around this time, and exact dates, locations, schedules and any ticketing or registration are announced by the New Taipei City government and Taiwan's tourism bureau. Because details change from year to year, check the official announcements closer to the date. See our Lunar New Year 2027 guide for travel planning around the holiday period.

What Happens at Pingxi

The tradition of releasing sky lanterns in Pingxi has roots in sending messages to say that the villagers were safe, and today it is a symbol of wishes and good fortune. Shops along Shifen Old Street sell large paper lanterns in different colours, and staff help you write messages with brush pens. Traditionally, different colours are linked to different wishes, such as health, success, wealth or love, though the meanings can vary by shop. You carry the lantern to a designated area, light it with the help of staff, make a wish and release it. The lanterns rise slowly and drift away, often by the dozen at once, creating a spectacular scene. On festival nights, there are also performances, food stalls and a large crowd.

Getting to Pingxi from Taipei

The easiest way is by train. Take a Taiwan Railway train from Taipei to Ruifang, which takes about 40 to 60 minutes depending on the service, then change to the Pingxi Line, a branch railway that winds through the valley and stops at Shifen, Pingxi, Jingtong and other stations. The Pingxi Line has frequent services, but trains can be crowded on weekends and festival days, and tickets may need to be bought in advance or on the day, with day passes available. You can also join a guided day tour, which often combines Shifen, Jiufen and other sights, or travel by taxi or a private car. Check timetables and any special festival arrangements, and allow extra time.

Shifen and Other Places Along the Line

  • Shifen Old Street: the heart of the lantern experience, with the railway running through the street, shops selling lanterns and snacks, and the sight of lanterns rising from the tracks.
  • Shifen Waterfall: one of the widest waterfalls in Taiwan, about a short walk or ride from Shifen station. It is often called the Niagara of Taiwan, and is best seen in the morning or after rain.
  • Pingxi Old Street: a quieter village with a calmer atmosphere and its own lantern shops.
  • Jingtong: a former mining town with a historic station and a peaceful feel.
  • Houtong Cat Village: a former coal-mining village that has become known for its many cats, on a different branch of the railway.
  • Jiufen: a hillside town with narrow lanes, teahouses and lantern-lit streets, often visited on the same day.

A One-Day Plan

Morning: Head out early

Leave Taipei in the morning, take the train to Ruifang and change to the Pingxi Line. Visit Shifen Waterfall first, when it is quieter.

Afternoon: Lanterns at Shifen

Walk along Shifen Old Street, choose your lantern colour, write your wishes and release the lantern in the designated area. Eat local snacks.

Evening: Jiufen or return

Continue to Jiufen for sunset and teahouses, or return to Taipei. Check the last trains, since services can be crowded and time-limited, especially on festival evenings.

If you want to see the main festival, plan to stay in Taipei and travel on the day, or book accommodation in the area well ahead.

Rules, Safety and the Environment

Sky lanterns are an open-flame activity and have environmental impacts. Local authorities regulate where and when lanterns can be released, and some years have seen restrictions or special arrangements for the festival. Follow the instructions of staff, release lanterns only in the designated places, keep away from trees, power lines and the railway when releasing, and never release lanterns in strong wind or in other areas. After release, lanterns can land in forests and valleys, and the paper and bamboo frames can become litter. Some operators now use environmentally friendly materials, and local groups organise clean-ups. Consider alternatives, such as writing a wish on a lantern that stays tied to a display, or choosing a smaller lantern release, and always choose vendors that follow the rules.

Weather and What to Wear

February in northern Taiwan is cool, damp and often drizzly, with temperatures often between about 10 and 18 degrees Celsius, and the mountains and valley can feel colder. Wear layers, a waterproof jacket, comfortable shoes with grip for wet streets and tracks, and bring a small umbrella. Pack a power bank, since cold shortens battery life, and carry cash for small stalls, although cards and mobile payments are common in many places.

Food to Try

Along the old streets, try grilled sausages, stinky tofu, sweet potato balls, taro desserts, Taiwanese fried chicken and bubble tea. In Jiufen, try taro balls in sweet soup and tea in traditional teahouses. Vegetarian options are available, including many Buddhist-style vegetarian dishes, but ask about ingredients such as fish sauce and meat broth.

Entry Rules for Indian Travellers

Entry rules for Taiwan depend on nationality and the visas you hold. Indian citizens may be able to apply for an online travel authorisation if they hold certain valid visas or residence permits from other countries, or may need a visa, but rules change. Check the official Taiwanese government and Bureau of Consular Affairs websites for the latest requirements before booking. See our visa guide for a step-by-step method, and our insurance guide.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Taiwan can be good value, but prices vary around the Lunar New Year. Build your estimate from flights, accommodation, rail passes or tickets, lantern costs, tours if you want one, meals, entry fees, insurance, any visa fees and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare hotel options in Taipei and nearby areas, and check cancellation terms.

More Questions Travellers Ask

Can I release a lantern outside the festival?

Yes, in designated areas throughout the year, with vendors who provide the lanterns and instructions. Follow the rules.

Is it crowded?

Yes, especially on weekends, holidays and festival nights. Go early on weekdays for a calmer experience.

Is it suitable for children?

Yes, but keep children close because of open flames, crowds and railway tracks.

How many days do I need?

One full day for Pingxi, plus several days for Taipei and other parts of Taiwan.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Not checking festival dates and arrangements.
  • Releasing lanterns outside designated areas.
  • Going on a weekend afternoon and getting caught in crowds.
  • Standing on the railway tracks when trains are due.
  • Forgetting a rain jacket in cool, damp weather.

Final Checklist

  • Festival dates, entry rules and insurance confirmed.
  • Train times and tickets checked.
  • Layers, rain gear and comfortable shoes packed.
  • A plan to release lanterns responsibly, and to keep clear of the tracks.
