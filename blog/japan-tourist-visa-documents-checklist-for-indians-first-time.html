Japan is the dream trip for a lot of Indian travellers: cherry blossom, bullet trains, temples, food that surprises you at every corner. It also has a reputation for careful paperwork, which makes first-timers nervous. The good news is that the tourist visa checklist is short and logical. If your documents are tidy, consistent and honest, the process is straightforward.

This guide turns the official checklist and recent guides into a plain-language list for a first-time Indian applicant: what to collect, what each document proves, what to avoid and how to prepare an itinerary that looks right. Sticker Visa or eVisa: Which One Applies to You?

Indian citizens need a visa for Japan. Business Today says there are two routes: a sticker visa and an eVisa, and that the eVisa has become the preferred method for many since April 2024. It also says Indians residing in countries such as the US, UK, Singapore, Australia or Saudi Arabia can apply through Japan's eVisa portal, while applicants based in India go through VFS Global. The article itself says that wording is somewhat unclear, so ask VFS Japan which route you should use.

Either way, the tourist visa is for tourism only. The article says the eVisa is single-entry, allows a stay of up to 90 days, does not allow paid work and cannot be extended or renewed under any circumstances. It also says a tourist visa is valid for three months from issue, so apply close enough to your trip that it will still be valid when you land.

The Official Document List

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs checklist for a single-entry short-term stay visa lists these documents from the applicant for a tourism application where you pay for your own trip.

Valid passport

Your passport must be valid. Business Today also says you need two blank pages and an A4 photocopy of the biodata page.

Visa application form

One completed form, signed. Download the version for your city, according to the article.

One photo

One passport-style photo. The checklist does not state photo specifications, so check them with your visa centre.

Itinerary with flights

A schedule showing flight or ship numbers and your arrival and departure dates for Japan.

Proof you can pay

If you pay yourself, an income certificate or tax return from a public agency, or a bank statement or balance certificate.

Itinerary in Japan

A day-by-day schedule using the template provided on the checklist.

The checklist says Japan-prepared documents can be submitted as copies but originals may be requested, and that they must be issued within the last three months. It also states that reservations are not required at the time of application.

What Guides Add: Documents Indian Applicants Often Bring

Business Today's list for Indian applicants goes a little further than the official checklist, and many applicants include these extras to make the file clear.

  • Cover letter stating the purpose of the trip and the visa type.
  • Confirmed flight tickets and itinerary. See the warning below about booking before you have a visa.
  • Income tax return documents.
  • Proof of accommodation.
  • Relationship proof if you are applying with dependents, such as a spouse or children.

The official checklist says reservations are not required at application, while the article lists accommodation proof. The safest course is to ask VFS Japan what they want to see, and to book refundable options if you are unsure. How to Build a Clean Itinerary

The itinerary is the document first-timers worry about most. It does not need to be fancy. It needs to be consistent with everything else in your file.

  1. Pick your dates and cities. Decide your arrival and departure dates and the cities you will visit, such as Tokyo, Kyoto and Osaka.
  2. Write a day-by-day plan. Use the template on the checklist and list one or two activities per day. Our slow-travel Kyoto itinerary shows a realistic pace.
  3. List where you will sleep. Add hotel names and addresses for each night, even if you have not paid.
  4. Match your flights. The flight numbers and dates must match the plan exactly.
  5. Check the numbers. Make sure the number of days, the cities and your funds all make sense together.
  6. Keep it honest. Do not invent hotels or bookings you cannot explain. Inconsistencies are the problem, not simplicity.

Proof of Funds: What to Show

The checklist asks for proof of your ability to pay your travel expenses, if you are self-funded: an income certificate or tax return from a public agency, or a bank statement or balance certificate. It does not say how much money you need, so do not rely on a number you read online. Ask VFS Japan or the embassy what level they expect for your trip length.

  • Use statements that show regular income, not a single large deposit just before applying.
  • Make sure the account holder's name matches your passport.
  • Keep income tax returns consistent with your bank statements.
  • If someone else is paying, ask the visa centre which sponsor documents are needed, because the self-funded checklist does not cover it.

Applying, Fees and Timing

Business Today says applicants submit documents at the nearest VFS Japan Visa Centre and that no appointment is needed except in Chennai and Bengaluru. It lists a visa fee of ₹500 and a VFS service fee of ₹800. Fees change, so check with VFS before you pay.

Mistakes First-Timers Make

  • Buying non-refundable flights and hotels before the visa is approved.
  • Itineraries that do not match flights or dates.
  • Documents with different spellings of your name.
  • Income documents that do not match bank statements.
  • Applying too close to departure, or too early, since the tourist visa is valid for three months from issue.
  • Forgetting that the tourist visa cannot be extended or renewed.
  • Not checking whether the eVisa or VFS route applies to you.

On the Day You Submit

A little preparation at the visa centre makes the whole visit calmer. Arrive early, carry originals and a clear set of photocopies, and put everything in the order of the checklist so the staff can scan it quickly. Take a pen, extra passport photos and a charged phone with digital copies, since small gaps are easier to fix on the spot than later.

  • Bring originals of your passport and any old passports with visas.
  • Keep documents in a plastic folder, with the checklist on top.
  • Ask for a receipt and a tracking number, and write down when to collect your passport.
  • If you receive an eVisa, remember the article says the visa issuance notice must be shown at immigration and that screenshots and PDFs are not accepted, so ask exactly what format you need.

Keep your travel insurance details with the file too, even if the checklist does not ask for them, since you will want them for the trip.

Final Thoughts

A Japan tourist visa is mostly about clarity: clear documents, a clear plan and a clear match between them. Start with the official checklist, add what your visa centre asks for, keep everything consistent and apply early. Then you can get on with planning the fun part, from temples at dawn to quiet side streets.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents do I need for a Japan tourist visa from India?

The Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs checklist lists a valid passport, a visa application form, one photo, an itinerary with flight details, proof of ability to pay and an itinerary in Japan. Guides add a cover letter, income tax returns and accommodation proof. Confirm with VFS Japan.

Do I need hotel bookings for a Japan tourist visa?

The official checklist says reservations are not required at application, while one guide lists accommodation proof. Ask VFS Japan what they want to see and avoid non-refundable bookings.

How long does a Japan tourist visa take for Indians?

The checklist says about one week, and guides report 3 to 5 working days for the eVisa and 5 to 7 working days for the sticker, with 2 to 4 weeks including slot and courier. Plan for the longer end.

How much is the Japan tourist visa fee for Indians?

Business Today reports a visa fee of ₹500 and a VFS service fee of ₹800. Fees change, so check with VFS.

How long is a Japan tourist visa valid?

One article says a tourist visa is valid for three months from the date of issue, with a stay of up to 90 days, and that it cannot be extended or renewed.

Is Japan's eVisa available to everyone in India?

The article is unclear about which applicants use the eVisa portal and which use VFS. Confirm your route with VFS Japan or the Embassy of Japan before applying.
