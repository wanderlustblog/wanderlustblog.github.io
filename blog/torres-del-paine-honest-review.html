The W Trek's wind is not a minor detail — it's a genuine trip-planning factor that shapes almost every decision on the trail. Gusts can exceed ninety kilometers an hour on exposed sections, and more than once we had to crouch low and simply wait it out rather than attempt to keep walking through it.

Is the W worth it over the full O Circuit?

For a first visit, yes, in our experience. The W hits the three signature viewpoints — the base of the Torres themselves, the French Valley, and Grey Glacier — in four to five days without the full O Circuit's extra remoteness, additional permits, and considerably heavier logistics. The O adds several genuinely spectacular but more isolated days that, for a first Patagonia trip, felt like more commitment than we were ready for.

Day by day, roughly

We started from the eastern end near the Torres, spending the first night at a refugio close enough to attempt the pre-dawn climb to the base of the towers the following morning. From there, the trail runs west along the lake shore toward Los Cuernos, then up into the French Valley — a full day's round trip from base camp that we nearly skipped due to tired legs and were extremely glad we didn't. The final stretch toward Grey Glacier brought us alongside genuinely enormous ice formations, some of which calved audibly while we watched from a safe distance.

The Torres at sunrise

The final pre-dawn scramble to the base of the towers, in the dark, cold, and half-convinced the whole thing wasn't worth losing sleep over — and then the first light hits the granite spires and turns them gold, and every complaint from the climb up evaporates almost instantly. We arrived at the viewpoint alongside maybe a dozen other hikers, all of us mostly silent, all of us clearly feeling the same thing at the same moment.

What the refugios are actually like

Refugio accommodation ranges from basic dormitory bunks to slightly more comfortable private rooms at a higher price, and meals are generally included or available for purchase on-site, which meant we didn't need to carry full cooking gear for most of the trek. That said, availability is genuinely limited, and showing up without a reservation in peak season is a real risk, not just a cautious warning from guidebooks.

Weather windows

Patagonian weather shifts fast enough that we learned to treat any forecast as a loose suggestion rather than a plan — clear skies at breakfast gave way to horizontal sleet by mid-morning more than once, then cleared again by afternoon. Building slack into the schedule for weather delays, rather than a tight day-by-day itinerary, made the whole trek considerably less stressful.

Gear that actually mattered

  • A properly windproof outer shell, not merely water-resistant — this made more difference than almost any other single item
  • Trekking poles, especially for the French Valley's steeper sections and the loose scree near the Torres base
  • A headlamp with genuinely fresh batteries for the pre-dawn Torres approach

Book refugios and campsites months ahead if you're aiming for peak season. This is not a trek where you can reasonably improvise accommodation once you've already arrived in Puerto Natales.

If this kind of trip appeals to you, you might also like another wind-exposed hiking destination: the Faroe Islands and gentler trails near Hallstatt if you want to build up first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I book the W Trek in advance?

Yes — refugios and campsites have limited capacity and regularly sell out in peak season (Dec–Feb).

Is the full O Circuit harder than the W?

Yes, it's longer and more remote, typically 7-9 days versus the W's 4-5, with fewer bailout points.

What should I pack for the wind?

A genuinely windproof (not just water-resistant) outer layer — Patagonia's gusts are a real factor, not an exaggeration.
