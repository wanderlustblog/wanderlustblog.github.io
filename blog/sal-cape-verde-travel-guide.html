Sal, Cape Verde: Beaches, Salt Crater, Blue Eye, Kitesurfing and How to Plan Your Trip

November 12, 202613 min readby WanderLust Blog

A flat, sunny Atlantic island with golden beaches, a floating salt crater and steady winds, Sal is a short flight from Europe and still unknown to most travellers.

Cape Verde is a group of volcanic islands in the Atlantic Ocean, off the coast of West Africa. Sal, in the northeast of the archipelago, is its most popular beach island, with long sandy beaches, constant sun, steady winds and an airport that connects it to Europe in around six hours from central Europe. A major 2027 travel trends report lists Sal among the destinations with the sharpest growth in flight searches, up 67 percent year on year from the United Kingdom, and lists of underrated destinations frequently include Cape Verde because it welcomes far fewer visitors than the Canary Islands across the water. This guide explains what Sal offers, when to go, where to stay, the main sights, entry rules for Indian travellers and how to plan four days.

Why Sal Is a Hidden Location

Sal is flat, dry and desert-like, with little vegetation, so it does not look like a typical tropical island. Its appeal lies in the beaches, the sea, the wind and the music-filled Creole culture. It is relatively unknown in India and many other countries, and many travellers think of it as an add-on to European holidays. Santa Maria is the main resort town, with hotels, restaurants and watersports, and much of the island is quiet, with small fishing villages and wide empty landscapes. It suits travellers who want a beach and activity holiday without crowds, and those who like to combine sun with a little adventure.

Best Time to Visit

Sal has a dry, warm climate with very little rain, and visitors come all year. November to June is generally considered the best period, with sunshine, steady winds and comfortable temperatures, which is excellent for kitesurfing and windsurfing. July to October is hotter and more humid, with a short rainy season on some islands and higher chances of dust haze. The Atlantic water is comfortable for swimming for most of the year, but can be cooler in winter. Check the wind forecast if you plan to do watersports, since conditions vary.

The Main Things to See and Do

  • Santa Maria beach: a long white-sand beach with a pier, restaurants, watersports and a lively atmosphere. It is the heart of the island.
  • Pedra de Lume salt crater: a former salt mine inside an extinct volcano, with a hypersaline lake where you can float easily, as in the Dead Sea. Wear water shoes, protect cuts and avoid your eyes.
  • Shark Bay: a shallow bay where harmless juvenile lemon sharks gather. Visitors can see them from the shore or while snorkelling with a guide. Follow the guide's instructions and do not touch the animals.
  • Buracona and the Blue Eye: a rocky coastline with a natural pool where, at certain times of day, sunlight creates a bright blue effect. Go with a guide and watch the sea, since waves can be dangerous near the rocks.
  • Kitesurfing and windsurfing: Sal is known for steady trade winds and good conditions, with schools offering lessons for beginners and rentals for experts.
  • Espargos and local life: the main town near the airport, with a market and a more local atmosphere than Santa Maria.
  • Music and nightlife: live music, including morna and other Cape Verdean styles, in Santa Maria's bars and restaurants.

A 4-Day Plan for Sal

Day 1: Arrive and relax in Santa Maria

Check in, walk along the beach, have a seafood dinner and listen to live music. Keep the first day light.

Day 2: Island tour

Take a half-day or full-day tour or hire a driver to visit Pedra de Lume, Shark Bay, Buracona and the viewpoints. Float in the salt lake, swim and take photos.

Day 3: Water day

Try a kitesurfing or windsurfing lesson, go on a boat trip, or learn to snorkel. Beginners should choose a reputable school, check safety equipment and be realistic about the wind.

Day 4: Slow day and departure

Relax on the beach, visit Espargos for souvenirs and leave a generous buffer for the airport.

If you have a week, add a ferry or short flight to another island, such as Boa Vista for dunes and beaches or Santiago for history and culture.

Entry Rules: Important for Indian Travellers

Reports based on the Henley Passport Index say that Cape Verde ended its visa-on-arrival facility for many nationalities, including India, from January 1, 2026. Indian travellers should therefore check the current entry requirements carefully, including whether a visa must be arranged in advance, and complete any online pre-registration or entry form required by Cape Verde before arrival. Rules may change, so rely on the official Cape Verde government and embassy sources, and your airline's document check. See our visa guide for a 10-step checklist. Carry proof of accommodation, a return ticket and travel insurance.

Getting There and Around

Amilcar Cabral International Airport on Sal receives direct flights from several European cities, and connections from other countries via Europe or other hubs. Taxis are available at the airport, and Santa Maria is about 20 minutes away. Within the island, taxis, shared minibuses (known as aluguers) and rental cars or quad bikes are available. Agree taxi fares before you set off, and rent vehicles from reputable companies. Roads outside the main towns can be sandy and rough.

Where to Stay

Santa Maria has hotels, resorts and apartments in all budgets, with many all-inclusive options aimed at European visitors. Choose a beachfront resort for a relaxing holiday, or a guesthouse in the town for more local atmosphere. If you plan to kitesurf, look for a place close to the kite beach and with equipment storage. Book early for the peak winter period, check what is included in all-inclusive packages and confirm airport transfers.

Food and Drink

Cape Verdean food reflects African, Portuguese and Brazilian influences. Try cachupa, a slow-cooked stew of corn, beans and meat or fish, fresh grilled fish and lobster, and seafood rice. Local drinks include grogue, a sugarcane spirit, and ponche, a drink made with grogue and fruit or molasses. Drink bottled water. Vegetarian options exist but may be limited outside the resorts, so check menus in advance. Currency is the Cape Verdean escudo, tied to the euro, and euros are widely accepted in tourist areas, though you may get change in escudos.

Safety and Practical Tips

  • Swim only where it is safe, and respect flags and lifeguard instructions. Atlantic currents and waves can be strong.
  • Use sun protection. The sun and wind can burn you quickly, even on cloudy days.
  • Keep valuables safe, and avoid walking alone at night away from busy areas.
  • Check your travel insurance covers watersports; see our insurance guide.
  • Check vaccination and health advice for Cape Verde and your route.

What to Pack

Light, breathable clothing, swimwear, a windbreaker for evenings, a hat, sunglasses, strong sunscreen, reef-safe if possible, water shoes for the salt crater and rocks, and a power bank. Bring a plug adapter for European-style sockets. Pack any equipment for watersports you prefer to use, such as a rash guard or wetsuit for winter wind.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Prices vary by season and style of accommodation. Build your own estimate from flights, accommodation, meals, activities, transfers, insurance, any visa fees and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare all-inclusive and room-only options, and check watersports rental and lesson prices. Prices are generally higher in the peak winter months.

More Questions Travellers Ask

Is Sal good for first-time visitors to Africa?

It is a gentle, resort-style introduction, but it is not typical of mainland Africa. It suits beach travellers more than culture or wildlife seekers.

Is Sal good for families?

Yes, with calm beach areas and family resorts, but check wind and wave conditions for children.

How many days do I need?

Four to five days for Sal, or a week if you add another island.

Can I combine Sal with other islands?

Yes, by short domestic flights or ferries. Check schedules, which can change.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming visa-on-arrival is still available for Indian passport holders.
  • Expecting a lush tropical landscape, when Sal is dry and flat.
  • Underestimating wind and sun.
  • Not checking insurance cover for watersports.
  • Not confirming tours and transfers in advance during peak season.

Final Checklist

  • Entry rules, any online registration and passport validity checked.
  • Flights, hotel and airport transfers confirmed.
  • Sun protection, water shoes and a windbreaker packed.
  • Insurance that covers watersports and medical care arranged.
