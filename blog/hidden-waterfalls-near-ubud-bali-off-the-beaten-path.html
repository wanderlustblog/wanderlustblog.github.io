Hidden Waterfalls Near Ubud, Bali (Off the Beaten Path)

August 12, 20269 min readWanderLust Blog

Everyone's phone already has the Tegenungan photo. These six are the ones your feed hasn't seen yet.

If you've spent any time researching Ubud, you've already seen the same three waterfalls over and over — Tegenungan, Tibumana, maybe Gitgit if the algorithm's feeling generous. They're lovely, but they're also crowded by 10am and ringed with selfie queues that eat into your morning. What follows are the ones that took actual asking-around to find, plus honest notes on how much effort each one takes.

None of these require a serious hike, to be clear. This isn't a trekking guide. It's more a list of places where the walk in is part of the appeal rather than an obstacle before it.

Kanto Lampo

Wide, tiered, and shallow enough in the dry months that you can wade across the rock shelves rather than just look at them from a platform. It's technically known now, thanks to a few viral photos, but the crowd thins fast after 10am and almost entirely by 3pm. Bring water shoes — the rock is slick.

Aling-Aling and the slide pools

North of Ubud near the village of Sambangan, this is actually a cluster of seven falls, several of which have natural rock slides locals will (for a small fee) show you how to use safely. It's more adventure than postcard, and worth the 45-minute drive if you want something with a bit of adrenaline attached.

Gembleng

Smaller, quieter, and genuinely local — you'll likely be the only outsiders there on a weekday morning. The trail down is steep in a few places but short, maybe ten minutes each way. This is the one to bring a book and just sit by.

Rule of thumb for all of these: dry season (April–October) means easier access roads and clearer water, but also more visitors. Shoulder months like April and October split the difference nicely.

Getting around

A scooter works for Kanto Lampo and Gembleng if you're comfortable riding on Bali's narrower village roads. For Aling-Aling, a hired driver makes more sense given the distance — most Ubud homestays can arrange one for a half-day rate that's still cheaper than a single tour booking.

What to actually pack

  • Water shoes or old sneakers — flip-flops don't hold on wet rock
  • Cash for small entrance fees, most are under 20,000 IDR
  • A dry bag if you're bringing a phone into the slide pools at Aling-Aling

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to swim at these waterfalls?

Mostly yes in dry season with normal caution. Check current speed before jumping in anywhere, and skip swimming right after heavy rain, when water rises fast and visibility drops.

Do I need a guide?

Not for Kanto Lampo. For Aling-Aling and Gembleng, a local driver who knows current trail conditions saves real time and avoids dead ends on unmarked turns.

What's the best time of day to visit?

Early morning, ideally before 9am. Tour groups arrive by mid-morning, and the light filtering through the canopy is better for photos anyway.

Is rainy season worth avoiding?

Not entirely — the falls run fuller and more dramatic, but trails get slippery and some access roads flood, so it's worth checking conditions the same morning rather than planning too far ahead.
