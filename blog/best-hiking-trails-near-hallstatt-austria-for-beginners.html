Best Hiking Trails Near Hallstatt, Austria for Beginners

August 12, 20269 min readWanderLust Blog

Everyone photographs Hallstatt from the water. These trails show you why the village earned the view in the first place.

Hallstatt gets treated as a two-hour photo stop by most day-trippers, which is honestly a waste of the surrounding terrain. The village sits inside the Salzkammergut, a region laced with lake-view trails that range from flat lakeside strolls to short alpine climbs — and almost none of them require any real hiking background.

Here are five that consistently deliver a good return for beginner-level effort, ordered from easiest to most demanding.

Start with the shoreline

The Lake Hallstatt Shoreline Path is about as easy as trails get — flat, well-signed, and mostly shaded. It's the right choice for a first afternoon to get oriented, and it ends near a few lakeside cafés that make a good excuse to sit down before tackling anything steeper.

Waldbachstrub: the quiet detour

Most visitors skip this one entirely, which is exactly why it's worth doing. A short forest trail leads to a genuinely pretty waterfall with almost no one else around, even in peak July. Round trip is under an hour, making it easy to fit alongside the shoreline walk on the same day.

The Salzberg cable car isn't cheating — it's the standard way locals reach the higher trailheads, and it saves roughly 40 minutes of steep switchbacks that add little to the view.

Stepping up: Salzberg and Echernwand

The Salzberg Skywalk loop takes the cable car up first, then a short, well-built walking path to the famous viewing platform over the lake. From there, the Echernwand Ridge Path continues along the cliffline for those who want a longer walk with the payoff of an elevated, uninterrupted panorama of Hallstatt below.

For a bigger day: Krippenstein

This is the most demanding of the five, but still manageable for a fit beginner — mostly wide alpine paths rather than technical scrambling. The Austrian alpine club maintains the route well, and the plateau at the top opens onto views across the Dachstein range that make the extra climb worth it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need hiking experience for these trails?

No — all five are graded easy to moderate, well-marked, and manageable in normal trail shoes without technical gear.

What's the best time of year to hike near Hallstatt?

Late May through early October, once snow has cleared from the higher trails. June and September strike the best balance of open trails and thinner crowds.

Can I avoid the Hallstatt tourist crowds while hiking?

Yes — nearly all of these trails start a short walk or bus ride from the village center and see a fraction of the foot traffic of the lakeside viewpoint.

Do I need a cable car ticket for any of these?

Only for the Salzberg and Krippenstein routes, which use the Hallstatt cable car for the initial ascent before the walking trail begins.
