Ubud is the cultural heart of Bali, and from about mid-morning it can feel like everyone on the island is there. The good news is that you do not have to leave the Ubud area to find calm. A few villages and valleys within reach are quieter, greener and slower, and some of them are only a short ride from the centre.

This guide covers the quieter villages named in published guides, what each is like, how to get around, when to move to avoid crowds and what to check before you book a stay. If you are choosing between Ubud and the coast, our comparison of Ubud vs Canggu for digital nomads may help, and our last-minute trips from India guide covers entry rules for Indonesia.

A note on accuracySeveral of our sources are commercial: a villa company, a hotel's own blog and a travel magazine. They describe places as quiet but give no crowd measurements. Treat 'quiet' as a general impression, check recent reviews and expect busy periods in high season. Our sources do not cover every village, and they say little about Pejeng and Payangan beyond a hotel address in Payangan.

What 'Without Crowds' Really Means in Ubud

No place in Ubud is crowd-free all day, every day. What you can do is choose areas that sit away from the centre's markets and restaurants, and visit them at quiet times. One villa company advises moving around town before 9 am or after 3 pm, because midday traffic gets messy. A hotel's blog describes the countryside just outside central Ubud as quieter, with rainforest, river valleys and more private spaces. Neither source measures crowd levels.

Quieter Villages and Areas

Use the filter to compare. Distances are not given in most of our sources, so check travel times on a map before you commit.

Penestanan

Rice-field lanesYoga and cafes

A villa company calls it Ubud's cool younger sibling, with quiet rice-field lanes, yoga spots, artsy cafes and a relaxed pace. Winding lanes and steps can be hard for cars, so you may need to carry bags. It is described as good for wellness seekers, remote workers and slow travellers.

Sayan

Above the Ayung RiverPrivate and resort-style

Perched above the Ayung River Valley with jungle views and privacy. The trip to central Ubud is longer, so plan your days to avoid frequent back-and-forth. Described as good for couples and special occasions.

Tegalalang and north Ubud

TerracesQuiet mornings

Known for layered rice terraces and misty mornings, which the same source describes as quiet. It is popular, though, and you will need more travel for markets, dinners and shows.

Payangan

Rainforest valleyOutside central Ubud

A hotel's blog places its property in Desa Buahan, Payangan, in a rainforest-valley setting with private transfers. The blog is promotional, and our sources say little else about the area.

Sidemen

Rice fields and volcano viewsAbout 45 minutes by car

Lonely Planet describes Sideman as about a 45-minute drive from Ubud, with rice fields and views of Gunung Agung, relatively quiet though drawing growing numbers of travellers. A hotel's blog calls it ideal for complete stillness.

Munduk

Waterfalls and forestNorth Bali

A relaxed mountain town with forest hikes through spice plantations and swimming waterfalls, presented as an alternative to Sideman for solitude. Its exact distance from Ubud is not given in our sources.

Gunung Batukau region

Jatiluwih rice fieldsIsolated but popular with day-trippers

Known for World Heritage-listed rice fields at Jatiluwih and Pura Luhur Batukau temple. Lonely Planet notes it is relatively isolated yet popular with day-trippers.

Timing: Beat the Crowds, Not the Calendar

You cannot avoid every busy period, but timing helps.

  • Move early or late. The villa company advises travelling in town before 9 am or after 3 pm.
  • Choose quiet mornings. The same source describes Tegalalang as having quiet mornings.
  • Watch the season. One hotel blog gives June to August and December to January as peak months for coastal areas like Canggu, Seminyak and Uluwatu, though it does not give crowd data for Ubud. Expect more people everywhere in those months.
  • Ride out of the centre. Lonely Planet suggests using Ubud as a starting point and exploring the surrounding countryside.

Getting Around Without Stress

Transport shapes how quiet your trip feels, because a long, slow ride erases the benefit of staying outside the centre. The villa company says walking is comfortable only in the centre. Outside it, hills, lanes and missing sidewalks make walking hard.

A Sample Three-Day Quiet Plan

This plan is our own suggestion built from the places above, not a tested itinerary. Adjust it to where you are staying.

Day 1

Settle inPenestanan

Arrive, unpack and walk the rice-field lanes of Penestanan. Have a slow lunch at an artsy cafe, take a yoga class if you like and go back before dark, since the villa company advises avoiding dark lanes at night.

Day 2

Terraces earlyTegalalang

Leave early for Tegalalang, which the villa company describes as having quiet mornings. Be back in a cafe by midday, then use the afternoon for rest or a short trip into central Ubud after 3 pm.

Day 3

Further outSidemen

Hire a driver for a day trip to Sidemen, about 45 minutes by car according to Lonely Planet. Walk the rice fields, enjoy the views of Gunung Agung and return by late afternoon. Our sources do not give walking routes, so ask your host.

What a Quiet Stay Might Cost

We could not find a reliable price list for these villages, and the villa company gives no accommodation prices. For a rough sense of Ubud overall, a nomad-data site updated in November 2026 lists a median hotel price of about $24 per night and a median Airbnb of about $90 per night, and a dinner at about $3. The same page contradicts itself on other points, such as describing meals at $10 to $20, so treat these as indicative only. Prices in quieter villages may differ. Being a Good Guest in Quiet Villages

Quiet villages are quiet because people live there. A few habits keep them that way, and they are general courtesy rather than rules from our sources.

  • Keep noise low in the evenings, especially in residential lanes.
  • Ask before photographing people, offerings or ceremonies. The villa company mentions watching locals make offerings, so keep a respectful distance.
  • Do not step on offerings placed on the ground.
  • Support small local cafes and warungs rather than only the largest places.
  • Take your rubbish with you and avoid drones in residential areas unless you are sure it is allowed.

Practical Tips for Quiet Areas

  • Pack light. Many villas and lanes have steps or narrow access paths, according to the villa company.
  • Wear good shoes and carry a flashlight, since quiet lanes can be dark at night.
  • Lock valuables and avoid dark lanes at night.
  • Check the transport. Ask your host how pickups work and what the nearest main road is.
  • Plan your days. Fewer back-and-forth trips make a long ride worthwhile.
  • Read recent reviews. Villa and hotel sites are marketing, so cross-check on independent platforms.

Before You Fly

Indian travellers should check entry rules before booking. The Travel Dham visa-on-arrival guide we use elsewhere lists Indonesia as a 30-day e-VOA at about IDR 500,000, with a customs card required within three days of landing and a Bali tourist tax. Rules change, so confirm on Indonesia's official immigration site, and read our last-minute trips guide and insurance guide. Final Thoughts

The quiet side of Ubud is mostly about where you sleep and when you move. Choose a village outside the centre, plan your transport, travel early or late and check recent reviews. You will still enjoy the markets and temples, and you will go home to rice fields and birdsong.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Ubud villages are quiet?

Guides name Penestanan, Sayan and Tegalalang as quieter areas near Ubud, and Sidemen, Munduk and the Batukau region as quieter places further out. Our sources do not measure crowds, so check recent reviews.

What time should I avoid the crowds in Ubud?

A villa company advises travelling in town before 9 am or after 3 pm to avoid midday traffic, and describes Tegalalang as having quiet mornings.

Is Penestanan walkable?

It has quiet rice-field lanes, but winding lanes and steps can be hard for cars, so you may need to carry bags. Outside central Ubud, walking is generally hard because of hills and missing sidewalks.

How do I get around quiet areas of Ubud?

Options include scooters at about IDR 50,000 to 85,000 per day, Grab or Gojek and private drivers at about IDR 500,000 to 800,000 per day, according to one guide.

How far is Sidemen from Ubud?

Lonely Planet describes it as about a 45-minute drive from Ubud.

When is Bali most crowded?

A hotel blog gives June to August and December to January as peak months for coastal areas. It gives no Ubud-specific crowd data.
