Booking refugios in Patagonia is the part of the trip that surprises first-timers most. There is no single website, beds sell out months ahead, the two companies that run the lodges use separate systems, and your park entry is a third thing to buy. Nothing about it is hard, but the order matters, and a gap in your nights can break the whole route.

This guide is a practical booking plan for first-timers, mostly based on the W trek in Torres del Paine, the most popular route. It covers who to book with, what to book first, how to line up your nights, what to have ready at the gate and the mistakes to avoid. For the route itself and the costs, read our guide to the Torres del Paine W trek without a tent.

Check the latest rulesOur sources are a W trek planning page checked on 8 October 2026, a booking article checked on 13 October 2026, a campsite and refugio guide for the 2024/25 season (last edited March 2026) and a tour operator's page. They differ on details such as which camps are run by which company, so confirm opening dates, prices and cancellation terms on the official sites before you pay.

How the Booking System Works

Reservations are mandatory for every night inside Torres del Paine National Park, and rangers check them at the ranger stations. Two companies run the lodges and campsites on the W: Las Torres Patagonia in the east, and Vertice Travel in the west. You book each separately on its own website, and one guide says itineraries cannot be changed once booked. A third purchase is park entry, sold online by the park service, which one source says must be bought at least 24 hours in advance.

The Booking Order That Works

Follow these steps in order so you do not buy something you cannot use.

  1. Choose your dates. Pick a window in the October to April season, and check the camp opening dates before you decide.
  2. Pick your route and number of nights. Decide whether you will do the classic W in four or five days, and which direction.
  3. Check availability on both company sites. Look at Las Torres and Vertice before you pay either, because both must have beds on consecutive nights.
  4. Book the scarcer night first. One guide says Paine Grande and the Las Torres sector fill earliest, so secure the hardest night first and build the rest around it.
  5. Book the remaining nights. Save every confirmation and receipt, printed and on your phone.
  6. Buy park entry online for your dates, and save the QR code offline.
  7. Book transport. Buses, the Laguna Amarga shuttle and the catamaran on Lago Pehoé are separate.

Not every camp is open in early season. In the 2024/25 guide, Central and Francés opened on 15 September, Paine Grande and Chileno on 1 October, Los Cuernos on 1 November and Grey on 4 November. These are last season's dates, so confirm for 2026-27.

Mar and Apr

QuieterCamps close early

Colder and quieter, with some camps closing early. In 2025-26, Cuernos and Serón closed on 30 March, and Central and Chileno on 25 April, according to one booking article. Check the closing dates before you plan a late trip.

Refugio, Camp or Package: What You Are Actually Booking

Before you pay, be clear about what each booking includes. Sources describe three kinds.

Prices in our sources range widely. Las Torres beds are listed from about USD 200 to 230 per night, bed only, while Vertice bunks are listed at about USD 46 to 66, so your choice of nights changes the total. The details are in our W trek refugio guide.

A Sample Planning Timeline

Our sources do not give booking-window dates for 2026-27, so this timeline is our own suggestion for working backwards, not an official schedule.

  1. As soon as you pick a month: check the Las Torres and Vertice sites for opening dates and availability.
  2. Straight after: book the scarcest nights and the nights around them.
  3. Once beds are confirmed: book flights and a hotel in Puerto Natales, since your refugio dates are now fixed.
  4. A few weeks before: buy park entry online if the dates are open, and book buses and the catamaran.
  5. The week before: reconfirm bookings, check the weather and print or save every confirmation.

Questions to Ask Before You Pay

Our sources do not describe cancellation or refund terms, so ask each company directly.

  • What is included in the bed price: bedding, meals, hot water?
  • What are the cancellation and change terms, and what deposit is taken?
  • If a trail or camp closes for weather, what happens to my booking?
  • Is the price in dollars or Chilean pesos, and does the foreigner rate need a passport and entry card?
  • What does the company need me to show on arrival?

Keep the answers in writing. If you book through a travel agent, our guide to choosing an international travel agency from India explains what to ask about refunds.

Planning for Wind, Weather and Closures

Patagonian weather is part of the plan. A tour operator says westerly winds are strongest from November to January, and one article says trails close at set times and walking without daylight is prohibited. Winter has its own rules, including a guide requirement and full closure of the W in June and July 2026.

  • Build a spare day into your plan if you can, since wind can change what is sensible to walk.
  • Pack waterproofs and warm layers even in summer.
  • Check with refugio staff each morning before you commit to a viewpoint.
  • Keep your insurance details handy. Our travel insurance guide for Indians explains what to look for.

What to Have Ready at the Gate and the Refugio

  • Booking confirmations for every night, printed or on your phone. One page says you need your confirmation ready at the gate.
  • Passport. One article says foreign visitors receive dollar rates for some beds only with a passport and entry card.
  • Park entry QR code, saved offline since mobile signal is limited.
  • Cash in pesos for shuttles and small purchases.
  • Sleeping liner or bag, since a tour operator says Vertice beds come with only a mattress cover and pillow.

Mistakes First-Timers Make

These are the common ones, based on the rules in our sources.

Booking one company only

A W trek crosses two companies' sectors. Booking only the east or west side leaves gaps.

Leaving entry to the last day

Park entry has to be bought online at least 24 hours ahead, and it needs a connection you may not have at the trailhead.

Assuming rules never change

One source lists free campsites as closed from 2025-26, with 2026-27 status unpublished at the time. Check the latest CONAF announcements.

Trusting a single old guide

Prices, camp names and stove rules differ between sources. Cross-check against the official sites.

Skipping insurance

Mobile signal is limited and help can take hours, according to a tour operator. Read our insurance guide for Indians.

Final Thoughts

Booking Patagonian refugios is mostly a sequencing problem: choose dates, check both companies, lock the scarcest night, buy park entry and keep every confirmation handy. Do that early and the trek itself becomes the easy part.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to book refugios in Patagonia in advance?

For Torres del Paine, yes. Reservations are mandatory for every night inside the park, and rangers check them. One source says January and February are peak months and advises booking well ahead.

Who do I book the W trek refugios with?

Two companies run the lodges and camps: Las Torres Patagonia in the east and Vertice Travel in the west. You book each separately on its own website.

Can I change my W trek booking later?

One booking article says itineraries cannot be changed once booked. Check each company's cancellation terms before you pay.

Do I need to buy park entry separately?

Yes. One source says entrance tickets must be bought online at least 24 hours in advance, and a QR code is needed. Prices differ by source, so check the official site.

What should I book first?

Book the scarcest night first, usually in the sector that sells out earliest, then fit the other nights around it. Check both companies' availability before you pay.

Is it easy to find beds at short notice?

Not in peak season. If your dates are sold out, try shoulder dates or a different mix of lodges and camps, and consider a package from a tour operator.
