Diwali 2026: Where to Celebrate in India as a Tourist (Dates, Etiquette and Tips)

October 22, 20269 min readby WanderLust Blog

Diwali is India's biggest festival of lights, and being there is unforgettable, but it is also peak travel season, so where you celebrate and how early you book matters.

Diwali, the festival of lights, spans several days of lamps, sweets, family gatherings and fireworks. Regional customs differ, so the experience in one city can be very different from another. Dates follow the lunar calendar and can vary by a day, so confirm with a local calendar before you finalise bookings.

Where to Celebrate

  • Varanasi: the ghats glow with lamps around Diwali and again on Dev Deepawali (November 24 in 2026), which makes it one of the most photogenic places to be.
  • Jaipur: illuminated markets, palaces and old-city streets, with a festive shopping atmosphere.
  • Ayodhya: a major pilgrimage destination for Diwali celebrations, so expect crowds.
  • Delhi: easy to reach and lively, with markets and family events, but check air quality and pack accordingly.

Booking Tips

Train tickets on popular routes sell out weeks in advance during Diwali and Durga Puja, and Indian Railways opens bookings 120 days ahead. Book hotels three to four weeks ahead at the very least. See our guides to using trains in India as a foreigner and travelling around India.

Etiquette for Tourists

  • Dress modestly, especially for temple visits; see what to wear in India.
  • If you're invited to a home, bring sweets or a small gift and remove your shoes at the door.
  • Ask before photographing people, rituals or religious ceremonies.
  • Many shops close or shorten hours on the main evening, so plan meals and shopping ahead.

Safety and Practical Notes

Fireworks are noisy and widespread, so keep a safe distance and protect hearing and pets. Crowded markets are prime spots for pickpockets and scams; read our tourist scams guide. Keep a charged phone and a power bank, since mobile networks can be congested on festival nights.

After Diwali: What Else Is on

The festival season continues into late November with Pushkar Camel Fair and Dev Deepawali. See our Pushkar Camel Fair 2026 guide.

Understanding the Five Days of Diwali

Diwali is not a single night. It is a cluster of days, and each has its own customs, though traditions vary by region and community. In many places the festival begins with Dhanteras, a day associated with buying new items, followed by Naraka Chaturdashi, the main Lakshmi Puja evening, Govardhan Puja and Bhai Dooj. Knowing the sequence helps you choose which days to be in a particular city and when shops, banks and restaurants may be closed.

Because the dates follow the lunar calendar, listings can differ by a day. Check with your hotel, a local calendar or a trusted panchang source once you are closer to travel.

A Sample 5-Day Diwali Trip: Jaipur and Varanasi

  • Day 1, Jaipur: arrive, walk the lit-up markets in the old city and eat a light dinner.
  • Day 2, Jaipur: visit forts and palaces in the morning, rest in the afternoon, and join your hotel or a local family for evening festivities if invited.
  • Day 3, travel: fly or take a train to Varanasi, allowing extra time since festival travel is busy.
  • Day 4, Varanasi: boat ride at sunrise, old-city walk and evening aarti on the ghats.
  • Day 5, Varanasi: temple visits, silk and craft shopping, and a quiet evening by the river.

If you prefer a single base, choose Delhi for convenience or Jaipur for atmosphere. Do not try to visit three or four cities in five days, since festival transport is crowded.

How to Be a Good Guest at Diwali

  • Accept invitations: if a family invites you, it is a genuine honour. Arrive on time, remove your shoes and accept food and sweets politely.
  • Greeting: "Happy Diwali" or "Shubh Deepavali" is always welcome.
  • Gifts: boxes of sweets, dried fruit or a small decorative item are thoughtful. Avoid leather items and alcohol unless you know the family well.
  • Rangoli and lamps: do not step on rangoli patterns at doorways, and be careful around lit diyas.
  • Photography: ask permission before photographing people and religious rituals.

Fireworks, Air Quality and Noise

Fireworks are widely used, and in some cities air quality worsens significantly around Diwali. Check local advisories and restrictions, since some areas limit fireworks or require green crackers. If you are sensitive to smoke or have breathing problems, consider a mask, avoid long outdoor stays at night and choose accommodation with air filtration. Earplugs help if you are staying in a busy neighbourhood. Keep a safe distance from fireworks and keep children and animals away from them.

Where to Stay and What to Book First

Book accommodation and long-distance transport first, because they are the hardest to find late. Look for places with good reviews for noise, hot water and safety. In cities with congested streets, choose lodging within walking distance of the places you want to see, or near a metro station. Confirm check-in and check-out times if you are arriving late during festival nights. For trains, see our train guide for foreigners.

Shopping and Food Tips

Markets are at their liveliest before Diwali, with lamps, sweets, textiles, jewellery and home decor. Agree on prices before buying, compare a few stalls and carry small notes. Try regional sweets such as laddoos, barfi, jalebi and gujiya, and eat at busy stalls with fresh turnover. If you have allergies, ask about nuts and dairy before eating sweets.

Money, Connectivity and Safety

  • Carry some cash for small shops, but also see our guide to using UPI as a tourist.
  • Keep your phone charged and consider a local SIM or eSIM; read whether foreigners need an Indian SIM card.
  • Watch for overcharging by taxis and rickshaws during busy nights, and use app-based services where possible.
  • Avoid carrying valuables in crowded markets.

Mistakes First-Time Visitors Make

  • Arriving on Diwali night without booked accommodation.
  • Assuming shops and restaurants will be open all day on the main festival day.
  • Wearing clothes that are too revealing for temple visits; see what to wear in India.
  • Underestimating travel time in festival traffic.
  • Not leaving room in the schedule for rest.

Final Checklist for Diwali Travel

  • Passport, visa and travel insurance in order.
  • Transport and hotels booked, with cancellation terms checked.
  • Modest clothing, earplugs, a mask, power bank and basic medicines packed.
  • A plan for the main festival evening, whether with a host, hotel or a safe public spot.
  • Emergency contacts saved offline.

More Questions About Visiting India During Diwali

Is Diwali a good time for a first trip to India?

It can be a memorable time, with lights, sweets and a festive mood. It is also busy, and travel and accommodation are more expensive. If it is your first visit, keep your itinerary simple and book early.

Will everything be closed on Diwali?

Some shops and offices close on the main days, and many families celebrate at home in the evening. Tourist-oriented hotels and restaurants often stay open, but hours change, so check ahead.

What should I do if I am invited to a family celebration?

Accept if you feel comfortable, bring a small gift such as sweets, dress modestly and follow your hosts' lead. Remove your shoes at the door and ask before taking photos.

How can I stay safe during fireworks?

Keep a safe distance, avoid crowded areas where fireworks are being lit, protect your hearing and avoid wearing loose, synthetic clothing near open flames. Keep children and pets away.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Diwali in 2026?

The main day is Sunday, November 8, 2026, with celebrations running for several days either side. Confirm locally as some sources list a day's difference.

Where is the best place to celebrate Diwali in India?

Varanasi, Jaipur and Ayodhya are popular for atmosphere, while Delhi is the easiest to reach.

Is it safe for tourists during Diwali?

Generally yes, with sensible precautions for crowds, fireworks and pickpockets.

How early should I book trains and hotels?

As early as you can; train tickets open 120 days ahead and hotels should be booked at least three to four weeks in advance.

What should I wear to Diwali celebrations?

Modest, comfortable clothing; festive Indian outfits are welcome but not required.

Should I bring a gift if invited to a home?

Sweets or a small gift are a thoughtful gesture.
