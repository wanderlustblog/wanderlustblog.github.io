A glass of mint tea on a Marrakech rooftop is one of the best small pleasures in Morocco. You sit above the noise, the minarets glow, the call to prayer rolls across the roofs, and the whole thing can cost less than a coffee back home. It can also cost far more, depending on where you sit and what you order. The difference is knowing how the pricing works.

This guide explains what rooftop tea realistically costs in dirhams, how to turn that into dollars, how to keep your bill under 5 dollars and how to enjoy the view without feeling rushed. If you are still working out the medina, our offline map tips will help you find your way to the terraces.

A note on accuracyPrices come from a Lonely Planet budget article dated November 13, 2025 and from a Marrakech venue's own blog. They are guides, not menus, and they change with season, venue and exchange rate. The sources give no dollar figures, so our conversions are approximate. Always check the menu before you order.

What Under 5 Dollars Means in Dirhams

Morocco uses the dirham (MAD). Neither source we read gives a dollar conversion, so here is a rough rule. At around 9 to 10 dirhams to the US dollar, 5 dollars is about 45 to 50 dirhams. Exchange rates move, so check a currency app on the day you travel and treat our figure as a ballpark.

Why Rooftop Tea Is Worth Planning For

The medina is loud and bright. A rooftop gives you a quiet corner, a place to rest your feet and a way to understand how the city is laid out. One guide suggests taking breaks on rooftop terraces on your first day because they give you a view of the city, which is useful when you are learning the lanes.

It is also a cheap activity. A single glass of tea can buy you an hour above the crowd, and you do not need a reservation at most casual terraces. Still, it is smart to know what to expect before you climb the stairs.

Three Kinds of Rooftop and What They Cost

Not every terrace is the same. Here are the three types you will meet, and how they usually affect your bill.

Simple cafe terrace

A plain cafe with a few tables upstairs. Tea is at the cheaper end, around the Dh6 to 12 range Lonely Planet gives for coffee or mint tea, though a view can add to the price.

Cafe with a view

A cafe known for its outlook, where one venue's blog gives a general range of Dh15 to 30 for mint tea. This is where most visitors land, and it still fits under 5 dollars.

Rooftop bar or restaurant

These focus on meals and drinks. Lonely Planet puts rooftop bar cocktails at Dh120 to 150. A tea is possible, but the venue may expect you to order more.

Riad or hotel terrace

Some riads serve tea to guests only, and some welcome visitors. Ask before you climb the stairs. Our sources do not list any minimum spend.

Our sources do not say whether venues set a minimum spend. If you are watching your budget, ask the waiter before you sit.

Where to Look: Areas, Not Just Names

We would rather give you areas to explore than recommend a long list of venues we have not tested. The sources point to a few places and neighbourhoods.

  • Around Jemaa el-Fnaa. A Marrakech venue's blog suggests cafes around the square for a sunset view, and it highlights the call to prayer across the rooftops as a memorable moment.
  • The Mouassine area. The same blog recommends Mouassine terraces for a sunset view. Mouassine is also home to Le Jardin Secret, which has a cafe overlooking its Islamic garden and tower. The blog describes the setting as quiet, with silence broken only by birdsong.
  • Terrasse des Epices. This terrace appeared in our research as a well-known rooftop option, although we did not confirm its prices.

The blog gives a mint tea range of 15 to 30 dirhams for cafes with a view, but it does not give a price for the garden cafe specifically. Garden admission is a separate charge, so ask whether you need to buy a ticket to reach the cafe before you go in.

How to Keep the Bill Low

  • Read the menu before sitting. If there is no menu, ask the price of a tea and a drink in advance.
  • Order tea, not cocktails. One source lists cocktails at Dh120 to 150, nearly five times the price of a view tea.
  • Skip the tourist set. Combination sets can cost more than a single tea. Ask for tea only.
  • Carry small change. Lonely Planet notes that taxi drivers rarely have change, and the same applies to many cafes at busy times.
  • Eat cheaply elsewhere. Lonely Planet suggests eating breakfast at a local cafe rather than paying extra for a hotel breakfast, and says Jemaa el-Fnaa's food stalls open around 4 pm. Save your money for the view.
  • Check the bill. Add up the items before you pay, and keep the receipt if you are unsure.

Best Time to Go

Morning

QuietCooler light

Terraces are calm in the morning, with softer light and fewer tourists. It is a good time to read your map, plan the day and have a first tea. Opening hours vary, and our sources do not list them, so check before you climb the stairs.

Afternoon

WarmShade helps

A venue's blog says most rooftops have shaded areas for summer. Afternoon is hot in summer, so choose a shaded table and drink water as well as tea.

Sunset

Most popularArrive early

Sunset gives you the call to prayer, warm light on the rooftops and the busiest tables. Arrive early to pick a seat, and be ready for slightly higher demand. Winter evenings can be cool, and the same blog says many rooftops have heaters or glass-enclosed lounges.

Etiquette on the Terrace

Rooftop cafes are friendly places, but a few habits make everyone's visit nicer.

  • Be mindful during the call to prayer. Many people stop to listen. Lower your voice for a moment.
  • Dress comfortably and modestly. Official and independent advice suggests loose clothing that covers shoulders and knees, which also keeps you comfortable in the sun. See our solo female evening safety tips for more on dress and conduct.
  • Ask before photographing people. Taking pictures of the skyline is fine, but ask permission before photographing staff or other guests.
  • Do not rush. One tea buys you time. Do not feel pressured to order more, but do not occupy a prime table for hours at sunset.

Paying, Tipping and Change

Small cafes usually prefer cash, so carry dirhams in small notes and coins. Our sources do not give a standard tipping amount, so follow your own judgment: rounding up the bill or leaving a few dirhams is a polite gesture for good service. Keep a few coins separate in your pocket so you do not have to open your wallet in a crowd, and review our phone and bag safety tips for the walk back.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Ordering without seeing a price, then being surprised by the bill.
  • Treating a rooftop bar like a cafe and ordering cocktails on a tea budget.
  • Assuming every garden or riad terrace is open to the public without a ticket.
  • Arriving at sunset with no time to pick a table.
  • Forgetting that exchange rates change.

Final Thoughts

You do not need a big budget to enjoy Marrakech from above. A glass of mint tea, a quiet table and a view of the minarets are well within 5 dollars if you choose a cafe terrace, read the menu and resist the cocktail list. Check the price, take your time and let the city come to you.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does mint tea cost on a Marrakech rooftop?

A Marrakech venue's blog gives a general range of 15 to 30 dirhams for mint tea at cafes with a view. Lonely Planet gives Dh6 to 12 for coffee or mint tea at simple cafes. Prices vary by venue.

Can I get rooftop tea in Marrakech for under 5 dollars?

Yes, in most cases. At around 9 to 10 dirhams to the dollar, 5 dollars is about 45 to 50 dirhams, which covers a glass of tea with a view. Exchange rates change, so check the day you travel.

How much is a cocktail on a Marrakech rooftop?

Lonely Planet gives Dh120 to 150 for cocktails at a rooftop bar, which is far above a 5 dollar budget.

Is there a minimum spend on Marrakech rooftops?

Our sources do not say. Ask the waiter before you sit, especially at restaurant-style terraces.

When is the best time for rooftop tea in Marrakech?

Sunset is popular for the light and the call to prayer, so arrive early. Mornings are quieter. Most rooftops have shade in summer and heaters or glass lounges in winter, according to one venue's blog.

Do I need to tip on a rooftop in Marrakech?

Our sources do not give a standard amount. Rounding up or leaving a few dirhams for good service is a common courtesy. Carry small change.
