Quebec Winter Carnival 2027: Dates, Passes, Ice Palace, Parades and a 3-Day Plan

October 28, 202613 min readby WanderLust Blog

Ten days of parades, ice sculptures and a giant snowman in the coldest, most festive week of the year in Old Quebec.

The Quebec Winter Carnival is one of the largest winter festivals in the world, held every year in Quebec City, Canada. It combines night parades, ice and snow sculptures, snow slides, music, food and a famous mascot, Bonhomme Carnaval, a friendly snowman. It is a very family-friendly festival, and also a good excuse to see Old Quebec's walled city under snow. The 2027 edition is confirmed for February 5 to 14, and the detailed programme had not been published when this guide was written, so this article focuses on what is confirmed, how the festival works and how to plan around the cold.

Dates and What Is Confirmed

The organisers have confirmed that the 2027 Quebec Winter Carnival will run for ten days, from Friday, February 5 to Sunday, February 14, 2027. The programme of parades and events, and the theme of the Ice Palace, had not yet been announced. Check the official Carnaval de Québec website for updates, ticketing details and any changes. The modern carnival, with Bonhomme as its mascot, dates from 1955, although winter carnivals in the city go back to the nineteenth century.

How the Carnival Works: The Effigie Pass

Many events at the Carnival are free to watch, including some parades, but access to the main official sites and activities requires a pass known as an effigie, a small medallion of Bonhomme that you wear or carry. The effigie is sold at various prices depending on the date of purchase, with early purchase usually cheaper. Check the official website for what the pass covers, since special events, concerts and some activities may need extra tickets. Buy only from the official channels or authorised sellers to avoid counterfeits.

What to See and Do

  • Night parades: large processions with floats, music and costumed performers in the evening. Arrive early to find a good spot along the route, and dress warmly.
  • The Ice Palace: Bonhomme's home and the festival's main gathering point, with a different theme each year. It hosts shows and meet-and-greet events.
  • Ice sculptures: displays in various parks and public spaces, with a special sculpture garden in some years.
  • Snow slides and winter games: large outdoor activity areas on the Plains of Abraham and other sites, popular with families.
  • Winter sports events: races and competitions are part of the programme in some years, including the famous ice canoe race on the St. Lawrence River. Check the schedule for 2027.
  • Food and drink: maple taffy on snow, hot chocolate, poutine and the carnival's traditional warm drinks.

A 3-Day Quebec Carnival Plan

Day 1: Arrive and walk Old Quebec

Check in, get your pass if you have not already, and explore the walled city on foot. See the Château Frontenac, Place Royale and the Petit Champlain district. Warm up with a long lunch.

Day 2: Carnival sites by day, parade by night

Visit the Plains of Abraham in the morning for snow activities, and see the Ice Palace in the afternoon. Arrive early for the evening parade and stay for the music.

Day 3: A day outside the city

Visit a nearby ice hotel or take a day trip to the Beaupré Coast, or spend a slower day in cafés and museums. Leave extra time for transport, since winter roads can slow journeys.

Where to Stay

Old Quebec is the most atmospheric base, with historic hotels and easy walking distances, but rooms are small, parking is hard and prices are highest during the Carnival. Saint-Roch offers nightlife and microbrewery restaurants, Montcalm is quieter and close to the centre, and Sainte-Foy suits those who drive. Book as early as you can, since the Carnival period is peak season, and check cancellation terms. Confirm whether your hotel offers parking and how far it is from the main sites.

Getting There and Around

Quebec City's Jean Lesage International Airport serves the city with flights from several Canadian and US cities. Many visitors arrive by train or bus from Montreal, a journey of a few hours, or drive from Montreal. In winter, check road conditions and tyre rules, and allow extra time. Within the city, walking is the best option for Old Quebec, and buses and taxis fill the gaps. Parking is expensive and limited, so consider leaving the car at your hotel. Check entry rules for Canada, including any electronic travel authorisation required for visa-exempt visitors flying in.

Language and Money

Quebec is French-speaking, though many people in tourist areas speak English. A friendly "bonjour" opens doors. Prices are in Canadian dollars, and cards are widely accepted. Tipping is customary in restaurants, usually around 15 to 20 percent, and sales taxes are added to the listed price.

Winter Clothing: The Most Important Section

Temperatures during the Carnival average around minus 10 degrees Celsius, and windchill can be much colder. Dress in layers and protect every exposed area.

  • Base layer: thermal top and leggings in merino or synthetic fabric.
  • Middle layer: fleece or wool.
  • Outer layer: a long, insulated, windproof and waterproof coat.
  • Feet: insulated, waterproof boots rated for very low temperatures, with grippy soles, and wool socks.
  • Head and hands: a warm hat that covers your ears, a neck gaiter or scarf, insulated mittens (warmer than gloves) and hand warmers.

Cold Safety and Tips

  • Take regular breaks in heated cafés and shops, and drink warm fluids.
  • Keep phones and cameras warm in inner pockets.
  • Walk carefully on icy pavements, using short steps.
  • Watch children closely, since they cool faster.
  • Limit alcohol outdoors, since it makes you lose heat faster.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

No hotel rates for the 2027 Carnival had been verified when this guide was written, so build your own estimate. List flights or train tickets, hotel nights, the effigie pass, food, local transport, optional activities, winter clothing you need to buy and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare hotels in at least two neighbourhoods, and check cancellation terms.

More Questions Visitors Ask

Is the Carnival suitable for young children?

Yes, particularly the daytime snow activities and the Ice Palace. Dress children very warmly, and plan breaks.

Do I need to speak French?

No, but a few phrases are appreciated, and signs and staff in tourist areas often use English as well.

Is it too cold to enjoy?

With the right clothing it is enjoyable. Plan indoor breaks and avoid staying out when windchill is extreme.

Can I combine it with other Canadian cities?

Yes, with Montreal or Ottawa by train or road. Check winter travel conditions.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Booking late during the Carnival.
  • Underestimating the cold and wearing fashion boots.
  • Relying on unofficial pass sellers.
  • Planning long drives without checking winter road conditions.

Final Checklist

  • Dates and programme checked on the official website.
  • Hotel booked with flexible terms, and your pass purchased from official channels.
  • Layers, insulated boots, mittens and hand warmers packed.
  • Entry rules for Canada checked, plus any travel authorisation needed.

Making the Most of Cold Days

The best Carnival days are planned around warmth. Do outdoor activities in short blocks, followed by a hot drink or a meal indoors. Plan the parade evening for a night when you have rested, and eat a proper dinner before you stand outside. Keep a small bag with spare mittens, hand warmers, a neck gaiter and a thermos. If the forecast is extreme, switch to museums, cafés, the covered markets and other indoor parts of Old Quebec, and return to the outdoor events on a milder day.

Combining Quebec With Montreal

Many visitors add a night or two in Montreal, which has its own winter events and a very different, more cosmopolitan atmosphere. Check train or bus times in advance, keep a buffer on travel days for snow, and avoid booking an early flight the morning after a late parade night.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the Quebec Winter Carnival 2027?

February 5 to 14, 2027, confirmed by the organisers.

Do I need a pass for the Carnival?

Access to the main official sites and activities requires a pass called an effigie. Many parades can be watched for free.

How cold is it during the Carnival?

Around minus 10 degrees Celsius on average, and often colder with windchill.

Where is the Ice Palace?

It is the festival's main site, hosting Bonhomme Carnaval. Check the official site for the 2027 location and theme.

Where should I stay?

Old Quebec for atmosphere, Saint-Roch for nightlife and Montcalm for a quieter stay. Book early.

Is it suitable for families?

Yes. It is one of the most family-friendly winter festivals, as long as children are dressed warmly.
