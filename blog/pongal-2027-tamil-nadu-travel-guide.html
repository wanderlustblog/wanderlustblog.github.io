Pongal 2027 in Tamil Nadu: Dates, Four-Day Customs, Where to Go and Travel Tips

October 26, 202612 min readby WanderLust Blog

Pongal is the harvest festival that fills Tamil Nadu's courtyards with rice-flour patterns and boiling pots of sweet rice, and it is one of the warmest, most welcoming festivals in India for visitors.

Pongal is the main harvest festival of Tamil Nadu, celebrated in mid-January to thank the sun, nature and farm animals for the harvest. It is named after a dish: rice boiled with milk and jaggery until it overflows, a sign of abundance. For visitors, it offers the chance to experience Tamil culture at a local, family-oriented level, with village traditions, temples, food and folk performances. This guide explains the four days, where to go, what to eat, how to behave respectfully and how to plan around the busy holiday period.

Pongal 2027 Dates

Thai Pongal, the main day, falls around January 14 or 15, 2027, when the sun enters the Tamil month of Thai. The festival lasts four days, starting the day before. Dates follow the solar calendar and are fairly stable, but check the Tamil Nadu government holiday notification and your accommodation for exact public holidays, since schools and offices often take several days off around the festival. Because many people travel to their home villages, trains, buses and flights fill up weeks ahead, so book early if you plan to travel on or around those days.

The Four Days of Pongal Explained

  • Bhogi (day 1): households clear out old items and some burn them in bonfires to mark a fresh start. Smoke is common in cities in the early morning.
  • Thai Pongal (day 2): the main day. Families cook pongal outdoors in a new clay pot, often facing the rising sun, and shout "Pongalo Pongal" when it boils over. Courtyards are decorated with elaborate kolams, patterns drawn in rice flour.
  • Mattu Pongal (day 3): a day to honour cattle, which are washed, decorated and fed. In some places it includes bull-related events.
  • Kaanum Pongal (day 4): a day for visiting relatives, picnics and community gatherings.

Where to Experience Pongal as a Visitor

Madurai and the south

Madurai combines the Meenakshi Temple with strong local festival traditions, and it is near villages known for rural celebrations. It suits travellers who want temples, markets and traditional Tamil culture in one place.

Thanjavur and the Cauvery delta

This agricultural heartland is where the harvest theme is strongest. Thanjavur's Brihadisvara Temple and the surrounding villages offer a quieter, more rural view of the festival.

Chennai

Chennai is the easiest base for first-time visitors, with cultural programmes, folk performances and food events. The city empties out as residents travel home, so it is calmer but some services have limited hours.

Villages and homestays

Staying in a village homestay can be the most memorable way to experience Pongal, because you can see cooking, kolam drawing and cattle decoration up close. Choose hosts with good reviews, and ask about house rules and what is included.

A 5-Day Tamil Nadu Pongal Plan

Day 1: Chennai arrival

Arrive, see a few heritage sites, and visit a market to buy sugarcane, turmeric and fresh vegetables used in the festival. Watch kolam patterns appear in the streets.

Day 2: Travel to Thanjavur or Madurai

Take an early train or a pre-booked car, check in and rest. Book early since travel is heavy around the holiday.

Day 3: Thai Pongal

Join your host or a local family, if invited, for the cooking and offering of pongal. Visit a temple in the morning, avoiding peak queues, and enjoy a home-cooked festive meal.

Day 4: Mattu Pongal and rural life

Visit a village or farm to see cattle celebrations, if available through your host. Be respectful, and understand that some events involve animals and crowds.

Day 5: Kaanum Pongal and return

Spend a relaxed day with local picnics, markets or beaches, then return to Chennai or continue your trip with a buffer for transport.

Food: What to Eat During Pongal

  • Sakkarai pongal: sweet rice cooked with jaggery, ghee, cardamom, cashews and raisins.
  • Ven pongal: a savoury version with rice, lentils, pepper and cumin, usually eaten at breakfast.
  • Sugarcane and turmeric: displayed and offered during the festival, with sugarcane eaten as a treat.
  • Festive vegetarian meals: often served on banana leaves with several side dishes.

Most traditional Pongal meals are vegetarian. If you have dietary needs, tell your host in advance.

Jallikattu: A Responsible Note

In some districts around Madurai, a bull-taming event called jallikattu is held around Mattu Pongal. It is a long-standing tradition to many Tamils, and also a subject of public debate because of safety and animal-welfare concerns. Crowds are dense, injuries to participants and spectators are reported in many seasons, and rules and legal status can change. If you choose to attend, follow official guidelines, keep to designated viewing areas and stay well away from the arena. Consider whether you are comfortable with the event before attending, and choose alternative cultural experiences if not.

Etiquette and Respect

  • Dress modestly, particularly in temples, and remove shoes where required.
  • Do not step on kolams in doorways, and ask permission before photographing people or family rituals.
  • Accept offerings with your right hand, and learn a few Tamil phrases such as "Pongal vazhthukkal" (Pongal greetings).
  • Do not touch cattle or ritual objects without permission.

Getting Around During the Holiday

Pongal is one of the busiest travel periods in Tamil Nadu. Trains, buses and flights sell out, roads are congested and some services run on reduced schedules. Book early, preferably with flexible options, and add buffer time to journeys. For train bookings and etiquette, read our train guide for foreigners. App-based cabs are widely available in cities, but may be limited in rural areas; see using Uber in India.

Weather and What to Pack

January is generally pleasant in Tamil Nadu, with warm days and mild evenings, though humidity and occasional rain are possible. Pack light cotton clothing, a scarf for temple visits, comfortable sandals that are easy to remove, sunscreen, a hat, insect repellent and a power bank. See our what to wear in India guide. If you stay in a village, bring a torch and basic medicines.

Money and Connectivity

Carry some cash for markets and village stays. Digital payments are widely accepted in cities, and foreign visitors can use specific options; see our guide to using UPI as a tourist. Mobile coverage is generally good, but may be weaker in rural areas; read about Indian SIM cards for foreigners.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Booking trains and hotels at the last minute.
  • Assuming all shops and attractions will be open on the main days.
  • Attending large events without checking safety rules.
  • Photographing rituals without permission.
  • Underestimating travel times because of holiday traffic.

Final Checklist

  • Confirmed dates and public-holiday timings.
  • Transport and accommodation booked well ahead, with flexible terms.
  • Modest clothing, sunscreen, power bank and basic medicines packed.
  • A plan for temple visits, a home meal if invited, and quieter days.

More Questions Visitors Ask About Pongal

Is Pongal a good time for a first visit to South India?

Yes. The weather is pleasant, the festival is welcoming and the cultural programme is rich. The main challenge is transport and hotels, which fill up early, so book as soon as your dates are fixed.

Can visitors take part in cooking pongal?

If a host invites you, yes. Families often welcome guests to stir the pot or shout the traditional call when it boils over. Ask politely, follow instructions and offer to help.

What gift should I bring if I am invited to a home?

Fruit, sweets or a small decorative item are suitable. Avoid leather goods and alcohol unless you know your hosts well.

Are temples and shops open during Pongal?

Major temples are open with special rituals and long queues, while many shops and offices close for part of the holiday. Check opening hours and keep a buffer for meals.

Bhogi, Thai Pongal, Mattu Pongal and Kaanum Pongal.

Where is the best place to experience Pongal?

Sakkarai pongal (sweet) and ven pongal (savoury) are the signature dishes.

Yes, with advance booking, since transport and hotels are busy and some businesses close.
