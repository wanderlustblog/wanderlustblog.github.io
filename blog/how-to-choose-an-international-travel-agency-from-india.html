How to Choose an International Travel Agency From India: A 12-Point Checklist and Red Flags

November 12, 202613 min readby WanderLust Blog

A good travel agency saves time and stress on a complicated international trip, and a bad one can cost you your money, so the choice deserves a proper checklist.

For many Indian travellers, an international trip is a big financial commitment and a logistical puzzle. There are visas, flights, hotels, transfers, insurance and sometimes group arrangements, and mistakes can be costly. A good travel agency can make the process smoother, but the industry includes everything from large, established companies to small operators and, unfortunately, a few unreliable ones. This guide gives you a practical, 12-point checklist for choosing an international travel agency, a set of questions to ask before you pay and the red flags that should make you walk away. It does not recommend or criticise any specific agency; the aim is to help you assess any agency yourself.

When Using an Agency Makes Sense

  • First international trip: an agent can guide you through visas, forex, insurance and planning.
  • Complex itineraries: multi-country trips with several flights, trains and transfers.
  • Visa-heavy destinations: where documents and appointments are demanding.
  • Families, seniors and groups: where comfort, pace and food needs matter.
  • Limited time: when you cannot research for weeks.

If your destination is simple, visa-free and short, you may be able to book yourself. See our comparison, international tour package vs DIY trip from India.

The 12-Point Checklist

1. Check the legal identity of the business

Ask for the company name, registered address, GST registration and contact details. Cross-check them on official or public registries, and make sure the name on invoices matches the name of the business.

2. Ask about industry memberships and approvals

Many reputable Indian agencies belong to industry bodies for travel agents and tour operators, and some hold recognition from the tourism authorities or airline associations. Ask which they hold, and verify directly with the body, since logos on a website are easy to copy.

3. Look for a physical presence and a track record

An office, a landline number and years of operation are not proof of quality, but they are positive signs. Be more careful with agencies that operate only through messaging apps and social media.

4. Read independent reviews

Look at reviews on several platforms, and read the detailed ones, particularly any complaints and how the agency responded. A few negative reviews are normal. A pattern of unanswered complaints about refunds is not.

5. Get a written, detailed itinerary

The itinerary should list dates, cities, hotels or hotel categories, transfers, meals, sightseeing, guides, flights and any optional activities. Vague phrases such as "best hotels" or "full sightseeing" are not enough.

6. Check what is included and excluded

Look for clear lists of what the price covers and what it does not, such as visa fees, insurance, tips, optional tours, airport taxes, city taxes and baggage fees.

7. Understand the visa process

Ask who applies for the visa, who pays the fee, what happens if it is refused and whether the agency charges a service fee. No agency can guarantee a visa, and any promise of guaranteed approval is a warning sign.

8. Read the cancellation and refund policy

Ask how much you can get back at different stages, how long refunds take and who bears airline and hotel penalties. Get this in writing before you pay.

9. Check the payment method

Pay only to the company's official business account, and get a proper invoice and receipt. Be cautious of requests to pay into personal accounts, in cash or through unknown payment links.

10. Ask about taxes and extra charges

Ask whether GST and any tax collected at source on overseas packages are included in the price, and get the details in writing. Tax rules change, so check with the agency and, if needed, a tax professional.

11. Confirm the support during the trip

Ask for an emergency contact number, availability across time zones and what happens if a flight is cancelled or a hotel changes. Ask how they handle problems on the ground.

12. Compare at least three quotes

Ask for quotes with the same dates, hotel category and inclusions, and compare line by line. The cheapest quote often leaves out items that appear later as extra charges.

Questions to Ask Before You Pay

  • Can you send the full itinerary, inclusions and exclusions in writing?
  • Which hotels, or which hotel category, will we stay in, and where are they located?
  • Are flights included, and are they refundable or changeable?
  • Who handles the visa, and what is your fee?
  • What is included in the price, and what extra costs should I expect?
  • What is your cancellation policy, and how fast are refunds processed?
  • Is travel insurance included, and what does it cover?
  • How many people will be in the group, and how many hours per day will we spend in a vehicle?
  • How do you handle vegetarian, Jain or other dietary needs?
  • What support will I have if something goes wrong during the trip?

Red Flags to Walk Away From

  • A price far below every other quote, with few details.
  • Pressure to pay immediately, for example with a "last seat" or "offer ends today" message.
  • Requests to pay into a personal account or by cash, or refusal to give an invoice.
  • No written terms, and promises made only on calls or messages.
  • Guaranteed visa approval.
  • No clear company name, address or registration.
  • Reviews that all look the same, or a total lack of reviews.
  • Unwillingness to answer questions about refunds and cancellations.

How to Compare Two Quotes Properly

Put both quotes in a table. List each item: flights, hotels with names and star category, transfers, meals, tours, guide, visa fees, insurance, taxes and tips. Mark items that are missing from one quote. Add the extra costs you will likely pay on top, and compare the final total. Look at hotel locations, not only star ratings, since a cheap hotel far from the centre adds time and transport costs. Ask for a second call to clarify differences, and note whether the agent explains clearly or avoids questions.

Group Tours, Customised Trips and Combination Options

Some agencies sell fixed-date group tours, which are cheaper but less flexible, while others design customised trips for individuals and families. A hybrid option, where you book flights and some hotels yourself and use an agent for the local portion, is also possible. Read our guide on group tour vs customised international trip from India to decide.

Protecting Yourself After You Book

  • Keep all emails, messages, invoices and receipts, and save screenshots of the offer.
  • Check that flight tickets and hotel bookings are actually confirmed, by looking up the airline booking reference and contacting the hotel.
  • Buy your own travel insurance; see our guide on international travel insurance for Indians.
  • Keep a copy of your passport, visa and itinerary offline.
  • If there is a dispute, write to the agency first, then use the grievance routes of your payment provider and consumer forums if needed.

Choosing a Destination Before Choosing an Agency

Decide on the destination, season and budget first, so that you can ask precise questions. If you need ideas, read our guides to Da Nang, Fes in Morocco or Singapore. Check entry rules in our guide to visa-free countries for Indian passport holders.

More Questions Travellers Ask

Is it cheaper to book directly?

Sometimes, for simple trips. Agencies can offer better prices on packages, group rates and bundled services, but add a service margin. Compare total costs.

Should I choose a big or small agency?

Neither is automatically better. Large agencies may have more resources, while smaller ones may offer personal service. Check the same factors for both.

What if I find a problem after paying?

Contact the agency in writing, keep records and ask for a written response. If it is not resolved, use your payment provider's dispute process and consumer protection channels.

How much advance payment is normal?

It varies, but a staged payment schedule linked to bookings is more reassuring than a demand for full payment up front. Ask for a clear schedule in writing.

Final Checklist

  • Business identity, memberships and reviews checked.
  • Written itinerary, inclusions, exclusions and cancellation terms received.
  • Payment made only to the official business account, with an invoice.
  • Insurance, visa and emergency contacts arranged.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if a travel agency is genuine?

Check its legal name, GST registration, address, memberships and independent reviews, and insist on written terms and official invoices.

Should I pay a travel agency in cash?

It is safer to pay into the company's official business account and get a receipt. Avoid cash and personal accounts.

Can a travel agency guarantee my visa?

No. The destination country makes the decision, and a promise of guaranteed approval is a red flag.

What should be in a written itinerary?

Dates, cities, hotels, transfers, meals, sightseeing, flights, inclusions and exclusions.

What are common hidden costs?

Visa fees, insurance, tips, optional tours, city taxes, baggage fees and tax collected at source.

Is it better to compare several quotes?

Yes. Compare at least three quotes with the same dates and inclusions, line by line.
