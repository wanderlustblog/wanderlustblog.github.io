Rio Carnival 2027: Dates, Sambadrome Tickets, Street Blocos, Safety and Plan

October 27, 202613 min readby WanderLust Blog

Rio Carnival is the biggest party on Earth, and in 2027 it arrives earlier than usual, which means many travellers who plan by habit will already be behind.

Rio de Janeiro's Carnival is a mix of two very different experiences: the huge, ticketed samba school parades at the Sambadrome, and hundreds of free street parties known as blocos across the city. Both are unforgettable, but they need different planning. In 2027 the festival falls earlier than usual because Carnival is calculated backwards from Easter, which lands on March 28. This guide explains the dates, how the parades work, how to find blocos, how to stay safe and how to build a simple five-day plan.

Rio Carnival 2027 Dates

The Sambadrome parades run from Friday, February 5 to the afternoon of Ash Wednesday, February 10, 2027, with Carnival ending on Shrove Tuesday (Mardi Gras) on February 9. Street blocos and pre-Carnival events start earlier, often weeks before, and some official listings describe the wider festivities as running from early February to mid-February. A Champions' Parade is typically held on the weekend after the main parades. Check the official Rio tourism and Liesa (the samba school league) websites for the confirmed schedule. If you normally plan your trip in the last week of January or mid-February, adjust your timing, since flights and hotels for these earlier dates are already being booked.

The Sambadrome: How the Parades Work

The Sambadrome is a purpose-built avenue where the top samba schools parade each night, with floats, costumes, drums and thousands of dancers. The main group, called the Special Group, parades over two nights, and other leagues parade on other nights. Each school has about an hour or more to perform, and each night's parades usually run from the evening until the early hours of the morning. Seating is in different sectors, from grandstands to boxes and standing areas, and prices vary a lot. Tickets for 2027 went on sale in August 2026, and the cheapest Special Group seats sold out within weeks, so if you want a seat, buy from official or authorised sellers as soon as you can.

  • Buy only from authorised sources. Fake tickets and inflated resale prices are common.
  • Choose your sector carefully. Seating close to the start of the avenue sees schools at their freshest, while seats near the end see the final act when dancers may be tired.
  • Plan for a long night. Bring a small bag, water, a light layer and comfortable shoes. Security rules limit what you can bring.
  • Consider a package: some operators sell tickets with hotel transfers, which makes getting there and home easier.

Street Blocos: The Free and Local Side of Carnival

Blocos are neighbourhood street parties with live music, costumes and huge crowds, and they are the best way to see how Cariocas celebrate. Hundreds of blocos run across the city in the weeks before and during Carnival, in places such as Copacabana, Ipanema, Santa Teresa, Centro and Botafogo. Schedules and routes are announced by the city and bloco organisers, usually a few weeks before, so check local listings. Large blocos draw hundreds of thousands, while smaller ones are friendlier for beginners.

  • Arrive early, since roads close and metro stations fill up.
  • Travel light and wear comfortable clothes you do not mind getting wet or dirty.
  • Carry a small amount of cash, a copy of your ID and a charged phone in a secure pocket.
  • Agree on a meeting point and stay with your group.

A 5-Day Rio Carnival Plan

Day 1: Arrival and a gentle evening

Check in, rest and take a walk along Ipanema or Copacabana beach. Eat early and save your energy.

Day 2: Sights and a small bloco

Visit Sugarloaf or Christ the Redeemer in the morning, when queues are shorter, and join a smaller neighbourhood bloco in the afternoon.

Day 3: Beach and rest

Spend the day at the beach, drink water and rest, since evening parades are long.

Day 4: Sambadrome night

Arrive well before your sector opens, enjoy the full night and plan secure transport home. Consider arranging a ride in advance.

Day 5: Recovery and departure

Sleep late, eat a long lunch and leave generous time for the airport, since traffic can be heavy.

Where to Stay

Copacabana and Ipanema are the classic tourist areas, with beaches, restaurants and metro access. Santa Teresa and Lapa are more bohemian and close to bloco action but have steep streets and need more care at night. Hotels near the Sambadrome (Centro) make the parade easy but can be less pleasant at other times. Book as early as possible for February 2027, since prices rise and minimum stays apply. Check cancellation terms and security at your accommodation.

Safety: Practical, Not Scary

Carnival is largely a safe and joyful event, but crowds attract pickpockets and phone thieves. Take simple precautions.

  • Leave passports, spare cards and expensive jewellery at your hotel.
  • Carry only the cash and one card you need, in a secure inner pocket or a travel pouch.
  • Keep your phone in a zipped pocket or a secure strap, and avoid taking it out in dense crowds.
  • Use ride-hailing apps or registered taxis, particularly late at night.
  • Do not accept drinks from strangers, and be careful with alcohol and sun.
  • Check current government travel advice, and avoid unfamiliar neighbourhoods at night.

Getting Around During Carnival

The metro is the best way to move around Rio during Carnival, and it often runs extended hours, but stations get very crowded. Roads close around bloco routes and the Sambadrome, so allow plenty of extra time. Ride-hailing prices surge at peak times. Walking is common, so wear comfortable footwear. Download offline maps; see our offline maps guide.

Entry Requirements

Check Brazil's visa and e-visa requirements for your nationality well before you travel, since rules for some countries have changed in recent years. Your passport must be valid, and you may need proof of onward travel. Consider travel insurance that covers theft and medical care.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Carnival is the most expensive time of year in Rio. Make a list of flights, accommodation (often with minimum stays), Sambadrome tickets if you want them, local transport, food, costumes, tips and a buffer of at least 15 percent. Compare hotel and apartment options, and read the cancellation terms. Free blocos and beaches help keep costs down.

What to Pack

Pack light, breathable clothing, swimwear, sunscreen, a hat, sunglasses, a small crossbody bag, a power bank and a rain layer. Bring comfortable walking shoes. February is summer in Rio, with high heat and humidity, so drink water and take breaks.

More Questions Visitors Ask

Is Rio Carnival worth it without Sambadrome tickets?

Yes. The street blocos are free and often more authentic, and many visitors prefer them.

Do I need to dress up?

It is common, but not required. Many people wear simple costumes, glitter or colourful clothes.

When should I book?

As soon as possible. Hotels and tickets are already selling for the early February dates.

Is Carnival suitable for children?

Some daytime blocos and events are family friendly, but the Sambadrome parades run very late, so consider the child's age.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming Carnival is in mid-February and booking late.
  • Buying tickets from unofficial sources.
  • Carrying valuables and large bags into crowds.
  • Underestimating the heat and the length of the nights.

Final Checklist

  • Dates confirmed, flights and hotels booked with flexible terms.
  • Sambadrome tickets from authorised sellers, if you want them.
  • Visa, insurance and offline maps ready.
  • A plan for safe transport and a secure carrying setup.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Rio Carnival 2027?

The main Carnival runs from February 5 to 10, 2027, with the Sambadrome parades from Friday to Ash Wednesday.

Why is Carnival early in 2027?

Carnival is calculated backwards from Easter, which falls on March 28 in 2027, so it happens earlier than the usual mid-February slot.

Are Sambadrome tickets already sold out?

Many cheaper seats sold quickly after sales opened in August, but other sectors and packages may still be available from authorised sellers.

What are blocos?

Free street parties with live music and costumes across Rio, a popular alternative to the Sambadrome.

Is Rio Carnival safe?

It is generally enjoyable, but crowds attract pickpockets. Carry little, stay in groups and use registered transport.

How many days do I need?

Five days allows for the beach, sights, blocos and a Sambadrome night.
