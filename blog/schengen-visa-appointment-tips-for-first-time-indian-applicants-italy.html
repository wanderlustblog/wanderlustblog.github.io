For many first-time travellers from India, the hardest part of an Italy trip is not the flight or the budget. It is getting an appointment slot. Spaces fill quickly, the portal can feel unforgiving, and one missing document can send you back to the start. The good news is that most problems are predictable, and a few habits remove most of the stress.

This guide collects appointment tips for first-time Indian applicants applying for an Italy Schengen visa: when to start, how to book, what to carry, how biometrics work and what to avoid. How the Italy Appointment System Works

Applicants in India usually book through the VFS Global portal for Italy. The guide we read describes the flow: create an account, complete the form, upload documents, pay the service fee, pick a slot and download the confirmation. It lists Delhi, Mumbai and Kolkata jurisdictions, each covering a group of states and union territories, and tells you to check which centre serves your state before booking.

At the appointment, your documents are checked and your fingerprints and photo are taken. The process is administrative, not an interview in the sense of a US visa. That is reassuring, but it also means your file has to be complete before you walk in.

When to Start

The guide advises starting between 3 and 6 months before travel. A processing-time article gives a useful reason: it says the legal maximum for a Schengen decision is 15 calendar days under the EU Visa Code, that France, Italy, Spain and Germany typically take 10 to 20 working days, and that summer from May to August can stretch to 4 to 8 weeks or more. Add a few working days for VFS and courier.

How to Find and Keep a Slot

Slots are the main stress point. These tips come from the guide, with a warning on one of them.

Check the portal daily

The guide recommends checking daily because cancellations release new slots.

Try weekdays and nearby cities

It suggests weekday slots and nearby centres, which can have better availability. Make sure you are allowed to apply at the centre for your state.

Beware of unverified timing claims

The guide claims slots are announced around midnight IST but gives no source. Treat that as a tip to test, not a fact.

Know the reschedule rules

The guide says up to three reschedules are allowed and that rebooking should happen at least five days before the appointment, though its own FAQ is inconsistent. A no-show is not refunded.

Avoid anyone who sells appointment slots at a premium. Use the official portal and check the fee list there.

Documents to Carry

The guide's list for a standard Italy tourist application is below. Always check the current official checklist, because requirements can vary by applicant type.

  • Passport issued within the last 10 years, with two blank pages.
  • Application form and two 35 by 45 mm photos, plus your appointment confirmation.
  • Flight itinerary or return ticket, hotel booking or invitation letter, and a detailed travel plan.
  • Financial proof: six months of bank statements, an ITR acknowledgement and three months of salary slips, or business registration if self-employed.
  • Travel insurance with at least €30,000 coverage. See our travel insurance guide for Indians for what to look for.

Fees to Expect

The guide lists an adult Schengen visa fee of €90, €45 for children aged 6 to 12 and free for children under 6, with student and business categories at €76 and €114. It puts the VFS service fee at about ₹2,000 to ₹2,500 but gives no exact figure. The guide's euro to rupee conversions are approximate and may be out of date, so check the official fee table on the day.

The Day of Your Appointment

Appointments run on a schedule, and a calm start makes the rest easy.

  1. Arrive 30 minutes early. The guide suggests this, because late arrivals can lose their slot.
  2. Carry originals and copies. Keep documents in the order of the checklist, with the appointment confirmation on top.
  3. Leave electronics at home. The guide warns against bringing laptops or USB drives.
  4. Prepare for biometrics. Fingerprints and a photo are collected. The guide says to avoid henna on fingertips, which can affect fingerprints, and sunglasses for photos.
  5. Attend in person. The guide says not to send someone else in your place.
  6. Get a reference number. Use it with your date of birth on the VFS tracking portal.

After the Appointment: Tracking and Waiting

Once your documents and biometrics are submitted, the waiting begins. The guide says you can track your application with your VFS reference number and date of birth on the VFS tracking portal. Passport dispatch takes one to three working days after a decision, according to the same guide, and a courier or collection step adds a few days.

  • Do not book non-refundable travel until your passport is back with the visa.
  • Check your email and phone in case the consulate asks for extra documents.
  • Keep your travel dates flexible in summer, when processing times can double for some countries, according to the comparison article.
  • Check the visa sticker when it arrives: name, dates, number of entries and number of days.

If you are still deciding between destinations, remember that some countries do not need a Schengen-style process at all. Our last-minute trips from India with visa on arrival lists options that are quicker to arrange.

Prepare Your Story: A Simple Travel Plan

A strong file tells a simple, believable story: who you are, why you are going, where you will stay and how you will pay. For Italy, that usually means a short itinerary in two or three cities, a clear return date and documents that agree with each other. You do not need a perfect day-by-day plan, but your dates, cities and bookings should match.

  • Choose a trip length that fits your leave and your budget.
  • Keep your hotel dates identical to your flight dates.
  • Make sure your bank statements show the income you claim.
  • Write a brief cover letter if the checklist allows it, stating purpose and dates plainly.

Mistakes That Cost First-Timers a Slot

  • Waiting until the last month to book, especially for summer travel.
  • Booking a centre that does not serve your state.
  • Mismatched names or dates between passport, form and bookings.
  • Weak financial proof, such as a sudden large deposit before applying.
  • Buying non-refundable flights and hotels before you have the visa. Use refundable bookings where possible.
  • Skipping insurance or buying cover below the stated minimum.
  • Trusting unofficial portals or paying for slot-selling services.

Planning Around the Visa

Work backwards from your travel date. If you plan to fly in June, aim to book your slot by February, submit documents in March and leave a buffer for a request for more documents. Final Thoughts

An Italy Schengen appointment is mostly about timing and tidy paperwork. Start early, use the official portal, check your documents twice and arrive calm. With a slot in your hand and a complete file, the rest of the trip, from Rome to the lakes, is just planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

How early should I book an Italy Schengen visa appointment from India?

One guide advises starting between 3 and 6 months before travel. For summer trips, book earlier because processing and demand rise.

Where do I book an Italy visa appointment in India?

Applicants usually book through the VFS Global portal for Italy and choose the centre that serves their state. Check the jurisdiction list before booking.

How much is the Italy Schengen visa fee for Indians?

A guide lists €90 for adults, €45 for children aged 6 to 12 and free for children under 6, plus a VFS service fee of about ₹2,000 to ₹2,500. Confirm on the official portal.

How long does an Italy Schengen visa take?

One guide reports 12 to 15 working days, or 20 to 25 working days from May to July, and a comparison article reports 10 to 20 working days with 4 to 8 weeks or more in summer.

What should I avoid at the appointment?

The guide advises against arriving late, bringing laptops or USB drives, wearing sunglasses for photos, having henna on fingertips and sending someone else.

Can I reschedule my Italy visa appointment?

The guide says up to three reschedules are allowed and that rebooking should be done at least five days before, but its FAQ is inconsistent, so check the portal rules. A no-show is not refunded.
