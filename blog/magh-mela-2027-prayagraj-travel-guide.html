Magh Mela 2027 Prayagraj: Dates, Snan Days, Tent City, Cold-Weather Tips and Plan

October 27, 202612 min readby WanderLust Blog

Every winter, a temporary city of tents rises on the sands where the Ganga and Yamuna meet, and the Magh Mela is one of India's most atmospheric and accessible religious gatherings for visitors.

The Magh Mela is an annual religious fair held at the Sangam, the confluence of the Ganga, Yamuna and the mythical Saraswati rivers at Prayagraj (formerly Allahabad). It is often called the "mini Kumbh". Unlike the Kumbh Mela, which is held on a rotating cycle, the Magh Mela takes place every year in the Hindu month of Magh, and it features a month-long stay of devotees called Kalpavasis who live in simple tents and follow strict routines. For visitors, it is a living religious tradition at a very large scale, but one that is more manageable than a Kumbh. This guide explains the dates, how to plan, what to expect and how to visit respectfully.

Magh Mela 2027 Dates and Key Bathing Days

According to the Uttar Pradesh tourism listing, the Magh Mela in 2027 runs from January 15 to March 6. Panchang sources place the main bathing days (snan) as follows, though different sources have slight variations in how they define the start, so check the official programme:

  • January 15: Makar Sankranti, an opening bathing day.
  • January 22: Paush Purnima, a full-moon bath.
  • February 6: Mauni Amavasya, the largest and most crowded day.
  • February 11: Basant Panchami.
  • February 20: Magh Purnima.
  • March 6: Mahashivratri, the final bathing day.

The days around Mauni Amavasya bring the heaviest road and rail traffic into the city, so visitors who want a calmer experience should avoid the days immediately before and after February 6.

What Is the Magh Mela, and How Is It Different From the Kumbh?

The Kumbh Mela is held at four sites on a twelve-year cycle, with the Maha Kumbh being the largest. The Magh Mela, in contrast, is an annual event held at Prayagraj in the same month each year. It is smaller, quieter and more focused on the Kalpavas tradition, but it still draws very large numbers on the major bathing days. If you missed the larger Kumbh gatherings, the Magh Mela is a good way to see the same rituals at a more human scale.

What You Will See

  • The riverbank tent city: a large temporary township with camps, roads, markets, medical posts and cultural stalls.
  • Kalpavasis: devotees who live in simple tents for a month, bathe at dawn and follow a disciplined routine.
  • Sadhus and akhadas: monastic groups with processions and camps.
  • Discourses and cultural events: religious talks, music and community kitchens.
  • The Sangam: boats take visitors to the confluence point, where bathing is concentrated.

How to Reach Prayagraj

Prayagraj has a small airport with direct domestic flights from several Indian cities, and a major railway junction with good connections to Delhi, Varanasi, Lucknow and Kolkata. Varanasi is about 120 km away, which makes it a natural pairing; see our Varanasi Dev Deepawali guide. Fares and seats tighten around the major bathing days, so book early. Fog in January and February can delay trains and flights, so avoid tight connections; read our guide to using trains in India.

Where to Stay

  • Government or private tent city camps: a convenient and atmospheric option, with tents of different standards and facilities. Check the operator, what is included and the distance to the Sangam.
  • Hotels in Prayagraj: more comfortable, with hot water and heating. Rooms near the Mela area fill up on major snan days.
  • Dharamshalas and ashrams: simple pilgrim accommodation with rules. They suit flexible travellers.

Book early, ideally months ahead for the major days, and choose free-cancellation options where possible.

A 3-Day Plan for First-Time Visitors

Day 1: Arrive and walk the Mela

Settle in and explore the Mela ground in daylight. Visit the camps and markets, and watch the evening aarti by the river.

Day 2: Sunrise at the Sangam

Reach the Sangam at dawn, when the light is soft and the atmosphere is intense. Take a boat to the confluence, but avoid bathing in deep or unsupervised areas. Visit the Allahabad Fort area and local temples later in the day.

Day 3: Culture and departure

Explore Prayagraj's city sights and food, and leave with a buffer for transport, since roads near the Mela can be congested.

Cold-Weather Planning

January and February are cold in northern India, with chilly nights and mornings, and fog common. The riverbank is colder and windier than the city. Wear thermal layers, a warm jacket, a woollen cap, gloves, socks and sturdy shoes. If you plan to bathe, bring a spare set of dry clothes and a towel, and be prepared for cold water. Avoid staying in wet clothes. Bring a power bank, since the cold drains batteries.

Crowd Safety

  • Avoid the very large bathing days unless you have a clear plan and experience of crowds.
  • Follow police and volunteer instructions, and keep to marked routes.
  • Stay near your group, agree on a meeting point and keep your phone charged.
  • Bathe only in designated areas, in shallow water, and never go alone.
  • Keep valuables minimal, and carry a copy of your ID.

Etiquette for Non-Pilgrim Visitors

Dress modestly, remove footwear where needed, speak quietly and ask before photographing people. Do not photograph people bathing, especially women. If sadhus ask for a donation for a photo, decide beforehand whether to give and accept refusal politely. Do not litter in the river or on the banks, and follow all local rules about plastic. Food in the langar (community kitchens) is for devotees, so ask before joining.

Food and Health

Choose freshly cooked vegetarian food, since most Mela stalls and camps serve vegetarian meals, and drink bottled or filtered water. Carry hand sanitiser and basic medicines. Take care with street food, and avoid raw or uncooked items. Check health advice and consider travel insurance.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Entry to the Mela is free, but costs add up. Build a list of travel, accommodation, local transport, food, boat rides, tips, warm clothing and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare tent and hotel options for your dates, and check cancellation terms. Prices rise sharply around Mauni Amavasya.

More Questions Visitors Ask

Can foreigners attend the Magh Mela?

Yes. Visitors are welcome if they follow local rules and respect religious customs.

Is it free to enter?

Yes, there is no entry fee, though some camps, boats and special activities charge.

Which day is best for a first visit?

A quieter weekday between the major bathing days offers more comfort and space. The largest days are exciting but very crowded.

How many days do I need?

Two to three days is enough for the Mela and Prayagraj's main sights.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Arriving on or just before Mauni Amavasya without a plan.
  • Underestimating the cold and fog.
  • Photographing bathers without permission.
  • Relying on a tight train or flight connection in fog season.

Final Checklist

  • Official dates and arrangements checked.
  • Accommodation and transport booked with flexibility.
  • Thermal clothing, shoes, power bank and basic medicines packed.
  • A plan for crowds, meeting points and safe bathing.

A Note on Planning Around Fog

Winter fog in northern India regularly delays trains and flights, sometimes by many hours. Build a spare day into your trip, avoid same-day onward connections, and keep a flexible booking for your return. If you are travelling for a specific bathing day, arrive at least a day before it, so that delays do not cause you to miss your plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Magh Mela 2027?

The 2027 Magh Mela runs from January 15 to March 6 at Prayagraj, with the largest bathing day on Mauni Amavasya, February 6.

What are the main snan dates in 2027?

Makar Sankranti (Jan 15), Paush Purnima (Jan 22), Mauni Amavasya (Feb 6), Basant Panchami (Feb 11), Magh Purnima (Feb 20) and Mahashivratri (Mar 6).

Is the Magh Mela the same as the Kumbh?

No. The Magh Mela is held every year, while the Kumbh follows a longer cycle and attracts larger crowds.

Is the Magh Mela free?

Entry is free, but some camps, boats and special services charge.

How cold is Prayagraj in January and February?

Nights and mornings are cold and foggy, and the riverbank is windier, so pack warm layers.

How far is Prayagraj from Varanasi?

About 120 km, so many travellers combine them.
