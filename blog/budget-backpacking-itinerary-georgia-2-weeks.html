Budget Backpacking Itinerary: Georgia in 2 Weeks

August 12, 202612 min readWanderLust Blog

Mountains, monasteries, and wine that costs less than the water — Georgia might be the best-value two weeks left in Europe's backyard.

I'll say this upfront: Georgia doesn't get talked about nearly enough for how cheap it is relative to what you get back. A dorm bed runs $8-12, a proper meal is often under $5, and the marshrutka network — the shared minibuses that connect basically everywhere — makes getting around absurdly affordable if you don't mind a bit of chaos.

This route covers the capital, the high Caucasus, the wine region, and the remote Svaneti mountains, in an order that keeps travel days efficient rather than backtracking across the country.

The route at a glance

Tbilisi: your base for the first few days

Give the capital three full days minimum. The old town's sulfur baths are worth the small entrance fee, and the cable car up to Narikala Fortress gives you the best orientation shot of the city for almost nothing. Tbilisi is also where you'll want to sort out a local SIM and stock up on cash — ATM fees are lower here than in the smaller mountain towns.

Kazbegi: the easy mountain fix

A three-hour marshrutka from Tbilisi's Didube station gets you to Stepantsminda, gateway to the Gergeti Trinity Church hike. It's a manageable half-day trek with a genuinely spectacular payoff at the top — glacier views and a 14th-century church sitting alone against the ridgeline. Two nights is enough unless you want to push further into the Juta valley trails.

Book Svaneti transport a day ahead if you can — marshrutkas there fill up fast in peak summer and there's often only one or two departures a day from Tbilisi or Zugdidi.

Svaneti: the part worth the extra effort

This is the highlight of the trip and also the most logistically demanding stretch. Mestia sits at the heart of Svaneti, ringed by medieval stone watchtowers and some of the best day-hiking in the Caucasus. Budget four days here — one to acclimatize and wander the village, two for hikes to Chalaadi Glacier or up toward Ushguli, and a buffer day in case weather closes the higher trails.

Wrapping up on the coast

Batumi is a strange, fun contrast after the mountains — a Black Sea resort town with a skyline that looks vaguely like a smaller Dubai. It's a good place to decompress for the last couple of days before flying out, and flights out of Batumi are sometimes cheaper than Tbilisi depending on the season.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does two weeks in Georgia actually cost?

Budget travelers typically spend $25-40 a day covering hostels, marshrutka transport, and local food. A two-week trip lands around $400-550 excluding flights.

Do I need a car to see Svaneti and Kazbegi?

No — both are reachable by marshrutka from Tbilisi, though the Svaneti route takes most of a day and is worth breaking up with an overnight stop if you can manage it.

Is Georgia safe for solo backpackers?

Yes, it's consistently rated among the safer countries in the region for solo travel, with low violent crime and a hospitality culture that tends to look out for visitors.

What's the best time of year for this route?

Late May through June, or September, gives mild weather in Tbilisi and clear mountain trails in Svaneti and Kazbegi without July and August's heat and crowds.
