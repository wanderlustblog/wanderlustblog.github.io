Most Santorini beach guides quietly assume you have a rental car, and a lot of the island's wilder coves really do belong to drivers. But you do not need wheels to have a wonderful beach day here. Buses fan out from the capital, a few of the best swimming spots sit at the end of a footpath, and boats reach the corners that roads never will.

This guide sorts the coves by how you get to them, so you can plan a day around a bus timetable instead of a parking space. We explain the bus system, give honest notes on each beach, flag the ones with safety warnings and suggest three easy day plans. If you are visiting outside summer, read our companion guide to Santorini quiet beaches in November as well, because many of these places change completely in the off-season. And for the bigger picture of the island, our hidden coves of Santorini story is a good place to start.

A note on accuracyBus fares, timetables and beach conditions change from season to season, and our notes come from travel guides published in 2026. Check the current timetable at Fira bus station or on the official bus company's page before you travel, and follow any local warning signs.

How Getting Around Santorini Without a Car Works

Santorini is small, but its roads are narrow and its sights are spread out, so planning matters more than distance. The key fact to remember is that Fira, the capital, is the island's bus hub. Most routes begin and end at Fira bus station, and if you are staying in another village you will often need to take a bus to Fira first before heading to a beach.

The guides we read quote fares of roughly €2 to €2.50 each way from Fira, depending on the destination. Perissa, for example, takes about 20 to 40 minutes and costs around €2.50, while Akrotiri, the stop for Red Beach, takes about 25 minutes and costs about €2. Prices and timetables change, and winter timetables are thinner, so confirm them before you go and always check the time of the last bus back.

  • Buy tickets as instructed. Ask at the station or on board how to pay, since methods can change.
  • Go early. Morning buses are cooler and less crowded, and you get the best of the beach.
  • Know your last bus. Write down the final return time from the beach stop and give yourself a margin.
  • Use a taxi for awkward hops. For beaches without a bus route, a taxi with a pre-arranged return can save a lot of effort.

Santorini Coves and Beaches by How You Get There

Use the buttons to filter by how you want to travel. Every card says what to expect when you arrive. We have kept the notes short and honest, including the places where guides disagree.

Perissa

Black sandBus from Fira, about 20 to 40 min

A long black-sand beach with sunbeds and umbrellas for hire, tavernas along the sand and watersports. It is the easiest full-service beach day without a car. A trail to Ancient Thera begins at the end of the beach and takes about an hour on foot.

Perivolos

Black sandBus then a short walk

Just along the coast from Perissa, with beach clubs and a livelier feel. Guides suggest taking the Fira to Perissa bus and walking 10 to 15 minutes, or strolling south from Perissa. Some rocks can be slippery.

Kamari

PromenadeFrequent buses, about 20 to 25 min

Often described as the most easily reached beach on the island, with a seafront promenade, bars and tavernas. It is quieter than Perissa and family-friendly. The northern part has slippery slabs, so take care.

Monolithos

Quiet and localBus then a short walk

A calm, shallow beach with space, a playground and a local atmosphere. Guides suggest taking the Fira to Kamari bus and asking the driver to stop near Monolithos, then walking a short distance. It is about 9 km from Fira, and a taxi is another option.

Vlychada

Pale cliffsReports differ on buses

Famous for its sculpted pale cliffs and a calm, uncrowded feel, with sun loungers and a cafe near the entrance. One guide lists a bus from Fira of about 30 to 40 minutes, while another says no buses run to the beach. Ask at Fira bus station or plan on a taxi with a return pickup.

Red Beach

Dramatic red cliffsBus to Akrotiri, then a walk

Take a bus from Fira to Akrotiri (about 25 minutes), then follow signs on foot from the archaeological site. The path is steep and rocky, there are no facilities and it gets very hot. One guide says the beach is officially judged unsuitable for visiting because of unpredictable landslides and that visitors go at their own risk. Treat it with real caution, and check for current local warnings.

White Beach

Boat onlyNo facilities

Reached by boat from Akrotiri, with one guide quoting about €5, or by water taxi from Red Beach. Walking is not recommended because the cliffs are unstable. Bring water, snacks and sturdy footwear, since there is no food or drink.

Ammoudi Bay

Below OiaBus to Oia, then steps

A tiny pebbly beach beneath Oia with a few fish tavernas. One guide counts about 235 steps down from the Oia castle area, so you will need to climb back. Sunbathing space is limited, and the setting is the real draw.

Katharos

Pebbly and quietAbout 15 min downhill from Oia

The closest beach to Oia, reached on a signed walk downhill from near the old castle. It is a quiet pebbly spot with craggy cliffs, popular for sunsets and snorkelling, with no sunbeds, changing rooms or toilets. The final stretch is a steep dirt road.

Palea Kameni hot springs

Volcanic isletBoat tours

No public ferry runs to the islet, so most visitors join a boat tour or hire a boat. One guide describes warm, mineral-rich springs heated to about 35°C and a barren landscape with no facilities.

Three Easy Day Plans Without a Car

Pick a day style and open its tab. They are flexible, so shift the times to suit your energy and the bus timetable.

Black-sand day

Perissa and PerivolosEasy

Take a morning bus from Fira to Perissa and claim sunbeds early. Swim, have a long lunch at a taverna and walk along the beach to Perivolos for a coffee. Return by bus in the late afternoon, and keep the last bus time in your pocket.

Oia sunset and steps

Oia, Ammoudi and KatharosModerate

Bus to Oia in the afternoon. Walk down to Katharos for a quiet swim, then climb back and wander the village. For a different mood, take the steps to Ammoudi for an early dinner by the water. Check the last bus back to Fira, or plan a taxi.

Boat day

Akrotiri areaAdventurous

Take the bus to Akrotiri, visit the ancient site if you like, then look at boat options for the nearby beaches. Because of the landslide warnings at Red Beach, treat it as a viewpoint unless local authorities say otherwise, and stick to reputable boat operators. Bring plenty of water, a hat and sturdy shoes.

Safety and Comfort Tips for Cove Hopping

The best coves are the least developed, which means you need to look after yourself. A few habits make the day safer and more enjoyable.

  • Water and sun. Several guides stress how hot the paths and beaches get, and many coves have no shade or shops. Carry more water than you think you need, plus a hat and sun cream.
  • Proper footwear. Paths to places like Red Beach and Katharos are steep and rocky. Sturdy shoes are safer than flip-flops, and water shoes help on pebbly shores.
  • Respect warning signs. Cliff areas can be unstable. Do not climb on cliffs, sit under overhangs or ignore closed paths.
  • Check the wind. Some beaches are more exposed when the wind blows, and sea conditions change quickly. If the water looks rough, stay on the sand.
  • Plan your return. Know the last bus, save a taxi number and keep your phone charged.
  • Take rubbish home. Many coves have no bins. Leave the place as you found it.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Planning a beach day around a cove with no transport and no taxi back.
  • Heading to Red Beach without checking local warnings, or climbing on unstable cliffs.
  • Trying to do too many coves in one day, so you spend it on buses instead of in the sea.
  • Forgetting that many remote coves have no shop, no shade and no toilets.
  • Missing the last bus and paying for a long taxi ride.
  • Assuming the beach clubs of July are still open in the off-season.
  • Taking steps at midday in summer heat, when early morning or late afternoon is easier.

Final Thoughts

You do not need a car to find Santorini's best coves. You need a bus timetable, a little curiosity and the sense to turn back when a path or a cliff looks unsafe. Start with the easy black-sand beaches, add a sunset walk from Oia, and treat boat trips and the wild coves as optional adventures rather than essentials.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you visit Santorini beaches without a car?

Yes. Buses run from Fira bus station to popular beaches such as Perissa, Kamari and Akrotiri, a few coves are reached on foot from Oia, and boats serve places that roads do not. Plan around the bus timetable and the time of the last bus back.

Which Santorini beaches can I reach by bus?

Perissa, Perivolos, Kamari and Monolithos can be reached using buses from Fira, sometimes with a short walk. Akrotiri, the stop for Red Beach, is also served. Reports differ on buses to Vlychada, so check at the station.

How much does the bus cost in Santorini?

Guides published for 2026 quote fares from Fira of about 2 to 2.50 euros each way, for example about 2.50 to Perissa and about 2 to Akrotiri. Fares and timetables change, so confirm them locally.

Is Red Beach in Santorini safe to visit?

One guide says Red Beach is officially judged unsuitable for visiting because of unpredictable landslides, and that visitors go at their own risk. Check current local warnings and treat it with caution, and do not climb on or sit beneath the cliffs.

How do I get to White Beach in Santorini?

White Beach is generally reached by boat, from Akrotiri or by water taxi from Red Beach. One guide quotes about 5 euros from Akrotiri. Walking is not recommended because the cliffs are unstable, and there are no facilities.

How many steps are there down to Ammoudi Bay?

One guide counts about 235 steps down from the Oia castle area. Other sources may give a different number, so expect a long climb back up and take water.
