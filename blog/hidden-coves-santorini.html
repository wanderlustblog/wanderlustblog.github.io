Hidden Coves of Santorini No Tourist Finds

April 18, 20268 min readby Luca Ferretti

Beyond the blue domes and postcard sunsets lies a wild, unhurried island that few travellers ever discover on foot.

Everyone comes to Santorini for Oia's sunset, photographs it from the same three spots along the castle ruins, and leaves without ever walking the caldera path south of Fira. That's the trail that actually shows you the island — not the postcard version, but the working, breathing one underneath it.

Vlychada and the moon coast

The beach at Vlychada looks like nowhere else in Greece — pale volcanic cliffs carved into ridges and hollows by centuries of wind, almost lunar in a way photographs undersell, and a fraction of the crowd you'd find at Perissa or Kamari just a few kilometers north. We arrived mid-morning on a weekday and had maybe two hundred meters of beach entirely to ourselves for the better part of an hour, which on an island this popular felt genuinely unusual.

The small marina nearby, tucked in against the cliffs, has a couple of tavernas that don't appear on most tourist maps. We ate grilled octopus at a place with four tables and a view of exactly nothing except rock and water, and it remains one of the better meals either of us had in Greece that year.

The caldera trail, slowly

Walking from Fira to Oia takes most people three hours if they rush it, following the tour-group pace and stopping only for photos. Take five instead. Stop in Imerovigli, sit somewhere with no name on a map, order a coffee you didn't plan for, and let the light change around you as the afternoon moves toward evening. The trail itself is narrow in places, dusty, unglamorous — and that's exactly what makes the views along it feel earned rather than delivered.

Getting there without a tour bus

Renting a small ATV or scooter for a couple of days gave us far more flexibility than relying on the island's bus schedule, especially for reaching Vlychada and Pyrgos, neither of which sit directly on the main tourist bus routes. Roads are narrow and occasionally steep, but manageable at a relaxed pace, and having our own transport meant we could simply stay somewhere an extra thirty minutes if the light was good, without worrying about a bus timetable.

A different kind of village

Pyrgos, inland and up on a hill, gets almost none of the coastal traffic. Its winding stone lanes were built centuries ago to confuse pirates, and they still manage to confuse GPS apps today, which we found out the hard way. Getting mildly lost there for forty minutes turned out to be one of the more memorable parts of the trip — cats sleeping on doorsteps, laundry strung between buildings, an elderly man who gestured us toward a viewpoint we'd never have found on our own.

Akrotiri, before the crowds

The excavated Minoan settlement at Akrotiri, sometimes called Greece's Pompeii, opens early and empties out by mid-morning once the bigger tour groups shift toward Oia. Arriving right at opening gave us almost the entire site without another visitor in sight, which changes the experience considerably — less shuffling through a crowd, more actually looking at what's in front of you.

Where to base yourself

Staying in Imerovigli rather than Fira or Oia put us within easy walking distance of both the caldera trail and a quieter stretch of the sunset views, without the price premium or crowd density of Oia itself. It's a small choice that changes the whole rhythm of a Santorini trip.

Santorini rewards the traveler willing to walk past the tour buses, even just a little. It's still there, underneath the postcard version — just quieter, less photographed, and better for it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Santorini walkable without a car?

Yes — the Fira to Oia caldera path is walkable in 3-5 hours, and local buses connect most villages cheaply.

When is Oia's sunset least crowded?

Outside July–August peak season, or by watching from a spot away from the main castle viewpoint.

Are the black sand beaches worth visiting?

Yes — Vlychada and Perissa have distinct volcanic sand and see far fewer visitors than the caldera-side towns.
