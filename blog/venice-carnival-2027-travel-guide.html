Venice Carnival 2027: Dates, Masks, Balls, Crowd Rules and a 3-Day Plan

October 27, 202612 min readby WanderLust Blog

Venice during Carnival feels like a stage set: masks in the mist, costumed strangers in narrow streets and a calendar that, in 2027, starts earlier than most travellers expect.

The Carnival of Venice is one of the oldest and most visual festivals in Europe, with a history of masks and masquerades that stretches back centuries. It combines street life, costumed performers, historic ceremonies and a series of private balls. The festival always ends on Shrove Tuesday, the day before Ash Wednesday, so its dates move with Easter. In 2027, Easter is on March 28, which pushes Carnival earlier than usual. This guide explains the dates, what to see, how masks and costumes work, how to deal with Venice's crowd-management rules and how to plan three days.

Venice Carnival 2027 Dates

The main festivities run from January 30 to February 9, 2027, with Mardi Gras on February 9. Some listings include pre-Carnival events starting as early as January 23, so check the official programme. The second weekend traditionally features the "Flight of the Angel", and the final weekend, leading up to Mardi Gras, is the busiest and most expensive for accommodation. Because the dates are earlier than the usual mid-February slot, hotel rooms may already be selling, so book early.

What to See and Do

  • Piazza San Marco: the main stage for costumed visitors and performers, crowded with photographers. Arrive early in the morning, when the light is softer and the square is calmer.
  • Costume contests and ceremonies: official events include costume competitions and opening and closing ceremonies. Check the programme for the 2027 schedule.
  • Street performances: music, theatre and parades appear in squares across the city, including Cannaregio, Castello and Campo Santa Margherita.
  • Mask workshops: artisans in Venice and Murano make traditional papier-mâché masks, and some run workshops where you can paint your own.
  • Masked balls: ticketed evenings in historic palaces, often with dinner, music and dress codes. They are expensive and book out early.

Masks and Costumes: How to Take Part

You do not need a full costume to enjoy Carnival, but even a simple mask makes you part of the scene. Traditional masks include the Bauta, the Moretta and the Volto. Authentic masks from workshops cost more than mass-produced ones, and are better quality. If you want a full costume, rent one from a specialist shop, and book ahead in peak weeks. Costumes are not allowed in some venues, and masks may be restricted in certain places, so follow instructions from staff and the police.

Venice's Crowd and Access Rules

Venice has introduced crowd-management measures in recent years, including a day-tripper access fee on selected peak dates. Carnival weekends have been classed as peak in some reports, and overnight guests may need to register or comply with specific rules. Check the city's official site, the Comune di Venezia, for the latest details before you travel. Crowds may also be managed around Piazza San Marco on the busiest days, with controlled flows and temporary closures. Allow extra time to move between sights.

A 3-Day Venice Carnival Plan

Day 1: Arrive and wander

Settle in, take a vaporetto along the Grand Canal and wander the quieter districts of Dorsoduro and Cannaregio. Enjoy dinner at a small restaurant and see the masks come out in the evening.

Day 2: San Marco at sunrise and masks in Murano

See Piazza San Marco early. After lunch, visit a mask workshop and a glass studio in Murano. Keep the evening for a ball or a quiet dinner.

Day 3: Final celebrations

If it is the final weekend, expect large crowds. Choose a few key events, stay flexible and plan your departure well in advance, since transport can be disrupted.

Where to Stay

Staying in central Venice puts you in the middle of the action, but prices are high and rooms are small. Cannaregio and Dorsoduro offer quieter, more local stays, and Castello is a good middle ground. Staying on the mainland at Mestre is cheaper and has easy train access, but you will spend time commuting. Book early, particularly for the final weekend, and check cancellation terms and any tourist tax.

Getting to Venice and Moving Around

Venice Marco Polo Airport and the Santa Lucia railway station are the main arrival points. In the city, you walk or take the vaporetto, the public water bus. Cars are not allowed in the historic centre. Bridges and steps make it hard for wheelchairs and heavy luggage, so pack light. Keep your tickets valid and validated, and avoid peak times if possible. Weather and What to Wear

Late January and early February in Venice are cold and damp, with fog and occasional rain. Wear thermal layers, a warm waterproof coat, waterproof shoes, a hat, scarf and gloves. Water levels can rise (acqua alta) in some conditions, so check forecasts and carry waterproof boots if required. See our winter packing list for layering ideas.

Food and Drink

Carnival has its own traditional sweets: frittelle (fried dough balls, often filled) and galani (thin fried pastry ribbons dusted with sugar). Enjoy cicchetti, small snacks served in bars, with a glass of wine, and seafood dishes. Avoid restaurants right on the main squares, since prices are higher and quality can be lower. Reserve tables for dinner on weekends and Mardi Gras.

Etiquette and Safety

  • Ask before photographing costumed people, and avoid blocking narrow bridges.
  • Do not litter or climb on monuments.
  • Watch for pickpockets in crowded places and on vaporettos.
  • Check opening hours for attractions and book timed tickets in advance.
  • Keep an eye on children and bags, since lanes are narrow and crowded.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Venice is expensive, particularly during Carnival. Build a budget for flights or trains, accommodation, vaporetto passes, food, mask or costume purchases, tickets for events, access fees if they apply and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare central Venice and Mestre. Free street events and walking keep costs under control.

More Questions Visitors Ask

Do I need to wear a costume?

No, but a mask or a simple costume adds to the experience and helps you blend in.

Is Venice Carnival suitable for families?

Yes, particularly during the daytime and on weekdays. Crowds on the final weekend can be overwhelming for children.

Which weekend is best?

The second weekend features the Flight of the Angel, while the final weekend is the busiest. Weekdays between them are calmer.

How early should I book?

As early as possible, ideally several months ahead for the final weekend.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming Carnival is in mid-February.
  • Booking a hotel in the busiest area and ignoring the cost.
  • Forgetting waterproof shoes and warm clothes.
  • Not checking access rules and event tickets.

Final Checklist

  • Dates, access rules and tickets checked on official sites.
  • Accommodation, trains and key events booked early.
  • Warm, waterproof clothing and comfortable shoes packed.
  • A plan for crowds, meal reservations and departure day.

How to Get the Best Photos Without Annoying People

Carnival photographers are everywhere, and the best images come from patience and politeness. Arrive early, when the light is soft and the lanes are quiet, and ask costumed visitors before taking close portraits. Many are happy to pose, and some even enjoy a short chat. Avoid blocking bridges and narrow passages, step aside to let others pass and use a small camera or phone rather than a large tripod. Keep one hand free for your bag, because crowded spots attract pickpockets, and check the weather, since fog can add atmosphere but reduce visibility.

Quieter Alternatives Within Venice

If the crowds feel too much, spend a few hours in less visited areas such as Cannaregio's canals, the Jewish Ghetto or Giudecca island, and visit museums while the peak crowds are in the squares. Many Venetians enjoy Carnival at a more local pace, with small gatherings, children's parades and neighbourhood events.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Venice Carnival 2027?

The main festivities run from January 30 to February 9, 2027, ending on Mardi Gras.

Why is Venice Carnival earlier in 2027?

It ends the day before Ash Wednesday, and Easter falls early on March 28 in 2027.

Do I need tickets for Venice Carnival?

Street events are free, but masked balls and some events are ticketed. Check the official programme.

Is there a fee to enter Venice during Carnival?

Venice has used a day-tripper access fee on peak dates, and carnival weekends may be treated as peak. Check the city's official site.

What should I wear?

Warm, waterproof layers and comfortable shoes, plus a mask or costume if you want to join in.

How many days do I need?

Three days covers the main events and quieter wandering.
