Socotra: The Island That Looks Like Another Planet

August 15, 202611 min readWanderLust Blog

Trees that look like umbrellas turned inside out, beaches with no footprints but your own, and a silence so complete it took genuine effort to get used to.

Socotra sits in the Arabian Sea, closer to the Horn of Africa than to mainland Yemen, and its isolation over millions of years produced a landscape that genuinely doesn't look like it belongs on the same planet as the rest of your trip photos. A large share of its plant species exist nowhere else on Earth, and the island's most famous resident, the dragon's blood tree, looks like something drawn for a science-fiction film rather than something that actually grows out of rock.

The forest that doesn't look real

Standing in the Dragon's Blood Forest on the Diksam Plateau, surrounded by trees whose canopies spread out flat like inverted umbrellas, was the closest we've come on any trip to feeling like we'd walked onto a film set rather than somewhere people actually live. Local guides explained that the tree's unusual shape evolved specifically to minimize evaporation in the arid climate, funneling what little moisture there is down toward the roots — a detail that made the strangeness feel less alien and more like an elegant, practical answer to a difficult environment.

A hidden lagoon most visitors never reach

Detwah Lagoon, near the town of Qalansiyah on the island's western tip, gets a fraction of the already small visitor traffic that the more famous Dragon's Blood Forest sees. Shallow turquoise water, a sandbar you can walk out onto for what feels like half a mile, and not another visitor in sight the entire afternoon we spent there. Our guide mentioned it almost as an afterthought when we asked what else was nearby — it turned out to be one of the quietest, most striking places either of us has ever stood.

Hoq Cave, off almost every itinerary

Reaching Hoq Cave requires a genuinely demanding hike up a steep, loose-rock trail, which keeps visitor numbers low even by Socotra's already minimal standards. Inside, ancient inscriptions left by sailors and traders centuries ago line the walls near the entrance — a reminder that this remote island sat, however faintly, on old trade routes long before anyone thought of it as a travel destination.

Camping under genuinely dark skies

With almost no light pollution anywhere on the island, camping trips here delivered some of the clearest night skies either of us has seen on any trip. Guides typically arrange multi-day camping circuits between the forest, the coast, and inland plateaus, with simple meals cooked over open fire and conversation that, thanks to limited shared language, often happened more through gesture and shared laughter than actual words.

Flights to Socotra are limited and seasonal — confirm current routing and any advisories directly with a specialized operator rather than a general booking site, since availability shifts more than most destinations.

What we'd tell a first-time visitor

Come with flexibility built into your schedule. Flight delays and route changes are common enough that a rigid itinerary will likely cause more stress than the island itself does. Beyond that, come without a checklist mentality — Socotra rewards slow, guided wandering far more than it rewards trying to see everything in a fixed number of days.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safe to travel to Socotra given Yemen's situation?

Socotra has remained largely separate from mainland Yemen's conflict and is administered differently, but travelers should check current advisories closely before booking, since the situation can shift.

How do you get to Socotra?

Limited flights connect Socotra to regional hubs, with schedules that change seasonally and are worth confirming directly with an operator rather than relying on general booking sites.

Do I need a guide?

Yes, effectively — infrastructure is minimal, and most camping and transport logistics are arranged through local guides rather than independently.

What's the best season to visit?

Roughly October through April avoids the strong monsoon winds that can disrupt both flights and boat access during the mid-year months.
