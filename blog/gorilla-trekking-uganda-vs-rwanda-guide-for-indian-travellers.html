Gorilla Trekking: Uganda vs Rwanda for Indian Travellers, Permits, Costs, Seasons and Booking Tips

November 13, 202614 min readby WanderLust Blog

Standing a few metres from a wild mountain gorilla for one hour is among the world's great wildlife experiences, and choosing between Uganda and Rwanda changes your budget, your route and your effort.

Mountain gorillas live in only a few places on Earth: the forested volcanic slopes of the Virunga mountains, shared between Rwanda, Uganda and the Democratic Republic of Congo, and Uganda's Bwindi Impenetrable Forest. Only a little over a thousand individuals remain, and tourism permits help fund their protection. For travellers from India, gorilla trekking has become a bucket-list adventure that is increasingly offered in packages from Indian agencies, but it is also a trip where choosing the right country, season and operator matters a lot. This guide compares Uganda and Rwanda on permits, costs, access, difficulty and experience, explains the rules, and gives a practical booking and preparation checklist. Prices come from operator and trade sources updated in 2026 and change between tariff periods, so always confirm with the national wildlife authorities and your operator before paying.

The Short Answer: Uganda or Rwanda?

  • Choose Uganda if you want a lower permit price, more choice of gorilla groups and sectors, and the chance to combine the trek with chimpanzees, safaris and a longer road trip. The terrain in Bwindi can be steep and muddy, and the drive from Entebbe is long.
  • Choose Rwanda if you value easy logistics, shorter transfers from the capital and well-developed lodges, and you accept a higher permit cost. Volcanoes National Park is a few hours' drive from Kigali, which makes a short trip easier.

Neither is automatically better. Both offer one hour with a habituated gorilla family, and sightings are reported to be very reliable in both.

Permit tariffs are set by the Uganda Wildlife Authority and the Rwanda Development Board for defined periods, and they can change, so always verify the tariff that applies to your exact trekking date. Be careful with websites that quote old prices. Never pay for a permit to anyone who cannot show you the official confirmation and the date, sector and group allocation.

What a Gorilla Trek Is Really Like

Trekkers are assigned to a group of up to eight visitors and a gorilla family, and set off with a guide, trackers and armed rangers who protect against wildlife. The hike to find the family can take from less than an hour to several hours, depending on where the gorillas have moved, through steep, muddy forest with thick vegetation. When you reach the gorillas, you spend one hour with them, usually at a distance of about seven metres, taking photographs without flash and following the guides' instructions. Then you walk back. Fitness requirements are moderate to high, because of altitude, slopes and mud, but porters can carry your bag and help you on difficult sections.

The Rules That Protect the Gorillas

  • Age limit: visitors must generally be at least 15 years old.
  • Health: if you have a cold, flu or other contagious illness, you may be refused a trek. Gorillas can catch human diseases, which can be deadly for them.
  • Distance and behaviour: keep your distance, avoid sudden movements, do not eat or drink near the gorillas and do not use flash photography.
  • Group size: a maximum number of visitors per gorilla group per day.
  • Face masks and hygiene: masks may be required, and you should follow hand hygiene instructions.

Uganda in Detail: Bwindi and Mgahinga

Bwindi Impenetrable Forest is a dense, ancient forest with several sectors, such as Buhoma, Ruhija, Rushaga and Nkuringo, each with its own gorilla groups and lodges. It has the largest number of habituated groups, so permits are easier to find at short notice, though popular months sell out. Terrain is steep and often muddy. Mgahinga Gorilla National Park is a smaller park on the Virunga volcanoes, near the Rwanda and Congo borders, with fewer gorilla groups and a more varied scenery. Reaching Bwindi from Entebbe is a long drive of a day, or a domestic flight to a nearby airstrip plus a transfer. Many travellers combine gorillas with chimpanzee tracking at Kibale and wildlife safaris at Queen Elizabeth National Park.

Rwanda in Detail: Volcanoes National Park

Volcanoes National Park lies in the northwest, about two to three hours by road from Kigali, on the slopes of the Virunga volcanoes. The terrain includes bamboo forest and open slopes, and treks can be shorter on average than Bwindi, though they are still demanding. Lodges around the park range from mid-range to luxury, and the infrastructure is well developed. Rwanda is also known for being clean, safe and well organised. Many travellers add Kigali's memorial sites, a visit to Lake Kivu or an Akagera safari. Permits are more expensive, and demand is high, so book early.

Best Time to Go

The drier months, roughly June to September and December to February, are generally considered better for trekking because trails are less muddy. The wetter seasons, around March to May and October to November, bring rain and slippery trails, but fewer visitors and, in Uganda, discounted permits in some low-season months. Gorilla sightings do not depend heavily on the season, but comfort and photography do. Book as far ahead as possible for the dry-season peaks, since permits are limited.

Costs Beyond the Permit

The permit is the largest single cost, but not the only one. Industry guides in 2026 suggest that a budget Uganda trip can cost from roughly USD 1,200 to 2,000 per person, mid-range trips more, and a short Rwanda trip typically costs more overall because of the higher permit price and lodge rates. Use these ranges only as rough guides. Build your own estimate with the following items.

  • International flights to Entebbe or Kigali, and any domestic flights.
  • Permit, with the exact date and sector confirmed.
  • Accommodation near the park, with the lodge level you prefer.
  • Ground transport, driver and fuel, particularly in Uganda where distances are long.
  • Meals, park fees, porters and tips for guides, rangers and porters.
  • Visa fees, yellow fever vaccination, other health costs and travel insurance.
  • A buffer of at least 10 to 15 percent.

Entry Rules, Vaccinations and Health

Entry requirements depend on your nationality, and Indian travellers should check the official immigration websites of Uganda or Rwanda for current e-visa or visa rules, and any regional visa options for travelling between East African countries. A yellow fever vaccination certificate is commonly required for entry to Uganda and may be needed for other countries, depending on your route. Ask a doctor or travel clinic about other vaccines and malaria prevention. Read our visa guide for a step-by-step method to verify entry rules, and our insurance guide to check cover for remote and high-altitude travel.

How to Book Safely, Especially From India

  1. Decide the country and approximate dates before you contact operators.
  2. Ask for permit proof. Reputable operators book permits directly with the national authority and send you the confirmation. Check dates and names.
  3. Compare at least three written quotes with the same inclusions: permit, lodge category, transport, meals, guides and park fees.
  4. Check the cancellation and rebooking terms. Gorilla permits are usually non-refundable, and some can be moved only under strict conditions.
  5. Check who handles problems. Ask for an emergency contact on the ground.
  6. Verify the operator. Look for registration in the destination country, reviews and membership of industry bodies, and follow our travel agency checklist.
  7. Pay safely into official business accounts, and keep all records.

Fitness and Preparation

Start training two to three months ahead with hill walking, stair climbing and cardio, and practise walking with a loaded daypack. Treks can be steep, slippery and at altitude, often between about 2,000 and 3,000 metres, so build stamina and leg strength. Break in your hiking boots well. Hire a porter if you want, since this supports local employment and makes the trek easier. Tell your guide about any health concerns, and do not hide a cough or fever.

What to Pack

  • Sturdy, waterproof hiking boots with good grip, and gaiters if available.
  • Long-sleeved shirts and long trousers in neutral colours, to protect against nettles and insects.
  • A rain jacket or poncho, warm layer, hat and gloves for gardening-style gloves against thorns.
  • Camera with spare batteries, without flash, and a waterproof bag.
  • Insect repellent, sunscreen, a reusable bottle, snacks and a small first-aid kit.
  • Copies of your permit, passport and vaccination certificate.

Responsible Travel and Conservation

Gorilla tourism funds protection and supports local communities, but only if it is done well. Choose lodges and operators that employ local people, pay porters fairly and follow the park rules. Follow every instruction from guides, never try to touch a gorilla and do not leave waste behind. Consider adding a community visit, such as a village walk or cultural performance, which directly benefits local families.

A Simple 7-Day Plan: Rwanda

Days 1 to 2: Kigali

Arrive, rest, visit the Genocide Memorial and the city's cafés and craft markets.

Days 3 to 4: Volcanoes National Park

Drive to the park, check in to your lodge, and trek on the permit day. Rest or visit a cultural village the next day.

Days 5 to 7: Add-on and return

Visit Lake Kivu or Akagera National Park, then return to Kigali with a buffer before your flight.

A Simple 8-Day Plan: Uganda

Days 1 to 2: Entebbe and the road west

Arrive, rest, and begin the journey to the park, perhaps with a stop at the equator and a night en route.

Days 3 to 5: Bwindi

Spend two nights near your trekking sector, trek on the permit day, and add a community walk or a rest day.

Days 6 to 8: Safari or lakes, then return

Add Queen Elizabeth National Park or Lake Bunyonyi, and return to Entebbe with a generous buffer.

More Questions Travellers Ask

Is gorilla trekking safe?

It is generally safe when you follow the guides, with armed rangers present for protection. The main risks are slippery terrain, altitude and weather.

Can older travellers or those with limited fitness do it?

Often yes, with preparation. Ask for an easier gorilla group, hire porters and consider a sedan chair service where available. Discuss with a doctor.

Are sightings guaranteed?

No wildlife sighting can be guaranteed, but success rates are reported to be very high, since the groups are tracked daily.

Is Uganda or Rwanda better for first-timers?

Rwanda is easier logistically, and Uganda is cheaper for the permit and offers more variety. Choose according to budget and how much time you have.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using outdated permit prices from old blog posts.
  • Paying a permit fee to an operator who cannot show proof.
  • Booking too late for the dry-season peaks.
  • Underestimating the physical effort and the mud.
  • Trekking while ill, which can lead to being refused.

Final Checklist

  • Country, dates and permit tariff confirmed with proof.
  • Entry rules, yellow fever certificate and insurance arranged.
  • Training started, boots broken in, packing list ready.
  • Written quote, cancellation terms and emergency contact received.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a gorilla permit in 2026?

Sources updated in 2026 list about USD 800 in Uganda (lower in low season) and USD 1,500 in Rwanda. Always verify the current tariff.

How long do you spend with the gorillas?

One hour for a standard trek in both Uganda and Rwanda.

What is the minimum age for gorilla trekking?

Generally 15 years.

When is the best time to go?

June to September and December to February are generally drier and easier for trekking.

Do I need a yellow fever certificate?

Often yes for Uganda and some routes. Check current requirements with the official sources and a doctor.

Which is better, Uganda or Rwanda?

Uganda has lower permit prices and more groups, while Rwanda offers easier logistics and shorter transfers.
