Nashik Kumbh Mela 2027: Dates, Amrit Snan Days, Where to Stay and Tourist Tips

October 26, 202613 min readby WanderLust Blog

The Nashik-Trimbakeshwar Simhastha Kumbh is one of the largest religious gatherings on Earth, and the people who enjoy it most are the ones who understand its dates, its crowds and its rhythm before they arrive.

The Kumbh Mela is a Hindu pilgrimage that rotates between four cities, and the Nashik and Trimbakeshwar edition on the Godavari river is known as the Simhastha Kumbh. It takes place roughly every twelve years, when Jupiter is in Leo. For visitors, it is a chance to see a living tradition on a very large scale. For planners, it is a challenge, because the dates are fixed by the religious calendar, the crowds are unlike anything in normal travel, and the monsoon overlaps with the main bathing days. This guide explains the dates, the places, the practical choices and how to visit safely and respectfully.

Nashik Kumbh Mela 2027: The Key Dates

The Maharashtra government announced the schedule in June 2025. The Simhastha formally begins with a flag-hoisting ceremony on October 31, 2026, which is only days away, and the period runs until July 24, 2028. The most important days are the Amrit Snan (the royal bathing days, formerly called Shahi Snan). The announced dates for 2027 are August 2, August 31 and September 11 at Nashik, and September 12 at Trimbakeshwar. Other days in the season, including the full-moon bathing day at Trimbakeshwar in mid-August, also draw large numbers of pilgrims. Dates and arrangements can be refined by the authorities, so check the official Maharashtra government and Kumbh administration announcements before booking anything non-refundable.

Understanding the Two Centres

Nashik: Ramkund and Panchavati

Nashik is a city of temples on the banks of the Godavari. Ramkund is the best-known bathing ghat, and the Panchavati area around it is associated with episodes of the Ramayana. The akhadas, the monastic orders of sadhus, set up camps and take part in the processions. The area is dense, so expect road closures, security checks and diversions on main days.

Trimbakeshwar: Kushavarta Kund

Trimbakeshwar is a smaller town roughly thirty kilometres from Nashik, at the source of the Godavari. It is home to an ancient Shiva temple, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, and to the Kushavarta Kund, where Shaiva akhadas take their holy dip. It is quieter than Nashik on ordinary days and extremely busy on bathing days.

The Best Time to Visit: A Decision Guide

  • If you want the full spectacle: attend a main Amrit Snan day, but only with confirmed accommodation, local help and a safe, simple plan. Crowds are extreme, movement is restricted and the monsoon adds slippery ghats and swollen rivers.
  • If you want to understand the pilgrimage without the crush: visit on a weekday between the main days, when the ghats and temples are still active but manageable.
  • If you are travelling with children, older relatives or limited mobility: choose a quieter period and prioritise comfort and safety over the headline dates.
  • If you are mainly a photographer: early mornings on non-peak days give soft light and approachable scenes; on main days, access to the water's edge is limited.

How to Reach Nashik and Trimbakeshwar

Nashik is in northern Maharashtra, roughly 165 to 170 km from Mumbai. The main ways to arrive are by road from Mumbai or Pune, by train to Nashik Road railway station, or by air to Nashik's airport or to Mumbai followed by a road transfer. During the Kumbh, expect heavy traffic and special arrangements for transport, including temporary bus services and park-and-ride facilities on major days. Trimbakeshwar is a short road trip from Nashik. Book trains and flights early; see our guides to using trains in India as a foreigner and travelling around India.

Where to Stay: Hotels, Tent Cities and Dharamshalas

  • Hotels in Nashik: the most comfortable choice, with a bed, hot water and a place to rest. Rooms near the ghats sell out early and become expensive. Staying a little further away and using a driver can be easier.
  • Government or private tent cities: temporary camps set up for Kumbh visitors, often with basic or premium tents, meals and organised transport. Check the operator, what is included, the distance from the main ghats and the cancellation rules.
  • Dharamshalas and ashrams: simple, low-cost pilgrim accommodation, usually with strict rules and shared facilities. They give a more traditional experience but suit flexible travellers.
  • Mumbai or Pune as a base: possible for day visits on quieter days, but not realistic on main bathing days because of road closures and crowds.

A Simple 3-Day Plan for First-Time Visitors

Day 1: Arrive and orient yourself

Settle into your accommodation, visit Ramkund and the Panchavati area in the afternoon, and watch the evening aarti on the Godavari. Learn the local layout and the crowd flow.

Day 2: Trimbakeshwar

Visit Trimbakeshwar early in the day, see the temple (queues can be long and entry rules apply) and the Kushavarta Kund, and then return to Nashik for a quieter evening.

Day 3: Culture and countryside

Explore Nashik's caves, temples and markets, or visit the wine region, which is one of India's best known, for a very different side of the area. Allow a buffer before departure.

Monsoon and Weather: What the Season Means

The main Amrit Snan days in August and September fall in the monsoon. Expect rain, humid air, slippery steps and strong river currents. Plan for waterproof layers, quick-dry clothing, closed non-slip footwear and flexible schedules. Rain can disrupt transport and events, and flood warnings or water-release decisions from upstream dams may affect access to the river. Follow the instructions of local authorities and do not enter the water if conditions are unsafe.

Crowds, Safety and Health

  • Crowds can be dense and moving. Avoid narrow bottlenecks, listen to police and volunteers, and leave early if you feel unsafe.
  • Agree a meeting point with your group, since mobile networks can become overloaded.
  • Keep valuables minimal, use inner pockets and carry a photocopy of your passport.
  • Drink bottled or filtered water, eat freshly cooked food and carry hand sanitiser and basic medicines.
  • Check the latest health and vaccination guidance for India and consider travel insurance.
  • See our scams guide for common tourist traps around religious sites.

Etiquette for Non-Pilgrim Visitors

The Kumbh is a religious event first and a tourist attraction second. Dress modestly, remove shoes where required, speak quietly near rituals and ask before photographing people, especially sadhus. Some sadhus expect a donation for a photograph. Decide in advance whether you wish to give anything, and accept a refusal politely. Do not step into restricted areas, do not touch ritual objects and do not enter the water as a spectacle. If you are invited to share food or tea, accept respectfully.

Photography Tips

Use a small camera or a phone, keep gear light and protect it from rain with a waterproof cover. Early morning and evening light is best. Be discreet at the ghats, avoid using flash near rituals, and be aware that drones and large tripods may be restricted by the authorities. A long lens allows you to photograph from a respectful distance.

Costs: How to Build a Realistic Budget

Prices will change as the dates approach. Build your own list: travel to Nashik, local transport, accommodation per night, food, entry fees or donations, equipment such as rain gear, insurance and a buffer of 15 percent or more because demand peaks. Compare at least three accommodation options for your exact dates, and check whether tent packages include meals and transfers. Book early, but choose free-cancellation options where possible, because administrative changes are possible.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Arriving on a main bathing day without accommodation or a plan.
  • Underestimating the effect of the monsoon on travel, comfort and safety.
  • Relying on outdated dates from unofficial blogs.
  • Trying to do too much in a single day.
  • Treating the event only as a photo opportunity.

Combine It With Other Festival Trips

The Kumbh runs for many months, so you can combine it with other India trips. If you are visiting India this November, see our guides to Dev Deepawali in Varanasi, the Pushkar Camel Fair and Diwali. For a broader route, read our India itinerary for first-time visitors.

Final Checklist

  • Official dates and local arrangements checked shortly before travel.
  • Accommodation and transport booked, with cancellation terms.
  • Rain gear, non-slip shoes, modest clothing, power bank and basic medicines packed.
  • A plan for crowds, meeting points and safe exit routes.
  • Insurance, passport copies and emergency contacts saved offline.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the Nashik Kumbh Mela 2027?

The Simhastha begins with flag hoisting on October 31, 2026 and runs until July 24, 2028. The main Amrit Snan days in 2027 are August 2, August 31, September 11 (Nashik) and September 12 (Trimbakeshwar).

Can foreign tourists attend the Kumbh Mela?

Yes. Visitors are welcome, but should respect religious customs, dress modestly and be prepared for crowds and security arrangements.

Where is the best place to stay for the Kumbh?

Hotels in Nashik are the most comfortable, tent cities offer an organised experience, and dharamshalas suit simple, budget travel. Book early.

Is it safe to attend a main bathing day?

It can be done with careful planning, but crowds, rain and restricted movement are real risks. Beginners may prefer quieter days between the main dates.

How far is Trimbakeshwar from Nashik?

Roughly thirty kilometres by road, which is a short trip outside peak days.

Should I book now?

Yes, with free cancellation where possible, since accommodation and transport sell out and official arrangements may change.
