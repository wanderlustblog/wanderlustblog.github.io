Loy Krathong and Yi Peng 2026: Chiang Mai Dates, Tickets and Tips

October 22, 20268 min readby WanderLust Blog

Two festivals share the same full-moon nights: Loy Krathong floats small offerings on water, while Yi Peng releases lanterns into the sky, and Chiang Mai celebrates both at once.

Both festivals are tied to the full moon of the twelfth lunar month, and different sources list slightly different dates (November 24 or 25, with Chiang Mai programmes running around November 24–26). Check official event schedules before you book anything date-critical.

Loy Krathong vs Yi Peng

  • Loy Krathong is celebrated across Thailand. People float small decorated rafts called krathongs on water as an offering.
  • Yi Peng is a Lanna tradition from northern Thailand where sky lanterns are released upward.

Chiang Mai: The Best of Both

Chiang Mai combines the two, with a parade, riverside celebrations and lantern events. The riverside along the Ping River is the main ground-level hub, and Nawarat Bridge is the best-known and most crowded spot. Walk a short distance along the bank for more space.

Lantern Tickets

Large organised lantern releases take place at ticketed venues outside the city, and they can be costly and sell out months ahead. Buy only from the official organisers. You can still enjoy the festival for free by watching the parade, visiting temples and floating a krathong at the river.

Bangkok Option

Bangkok's Loy Krathong is free and water-based. Sky lanterns belong to Yi Peng in the north, so if lanterns are your priority, base yourself in Chiang Mai.

Responsible Tips

  • Choose eco-friendly krathongs made from natural materials.
  • Arrive early; riverside spots fill hours before dark.
  • Check lantern release rules, since restrictions may apply.
  • Book flights and hotels early; November is high season.

A Simple 4-Day Chiang Mai Plan for the Lantern Festivals

  • Day 1: arrive, explore the old city and temples at a relaxed pace, and sample northern Thai food such as khao soi.
  • Day 2: morning visit to Wat Phra That Doi Suthep or another temple, afternoon rest, and evening street food or night market.
  • Day 3: festival night. Watch the parade, walk to the Ping River, float a krathong, and, if you have tickets, attend a lantern release.
  • Day 4: slow morning, shopping for crafts and a final meal before departing.

If lanterns are your top priority, allow a second festival night in case of rain or cloud, and avoid scheduling flights early the following morning.

How to Make and Float a Krathong Properly

A krathong is a small floating offering, traditionally made from banana tree trunk or leaves and decorated with flowers, candles and incense. You can buy one from a market or make one yourself in a workshop. Light the candle and incense carefully, make a wish, and gently place it on the water. Choose a natural, biodegradable krathong and avoid ones containing plastic, foam or metal pins. Do not push others, step on riverbanks that are fragile or leave litter behind.

Understanding Lantern Rules and Safety

Sky lanterns can be a fire and aviation risk, and rules about where and when they can be released are strict in some areas. Use only official or licensed events, listen to staff instructions, keep a safe distance from trees and buildings, and avoid releasing lanterns anywhere else. Some years, airports adjust flight schedules or authorities restrict releases for safety. If you are travelling with children, keep them close and supervised. Check current announcements from the Tourism Authority of Thailand and the local municipality.

Ticketed vs Free: How to Decide

  • Ticketed lantern events: best if you want the iconic mass-release experience and photographs. They require early booking, may include transport and meals, and vary widely in price and quality.
  • Free celebrations: the parade, temple events and river floating cost little and offer a more local atmosphere.
  • Mixed approach: many visitors enjoy the free parade and riverside early in the evening and attend a ticketed event on a different night.

Buy tickets only from official organisers or reputable operators, check refund terms and beware of fake sellers on social media.

Crowds, Timing and Getting Around

Chiang Mai becomes very crowded during the festival, with road closures and heavy traffic. Plan to walk or use short rides, and expect to wait for taxis or app cars at night. Arrive at the riverside early, since good spots fill hours before dark. Carry a small flashlight or use your phone light, wear comfortable shoes and keep valuables secure. Agree on a meeting point if you are in a group, since phone networks can be congested.

Where to Stay

The old city is walkable and close to the parade and temples, but it is noisy during the festival. The Nimmanhaemin area is more modern and quieter, and the riverside offers views and easy access to the Ping River, though prices rise during festival nights. Book early, check cancellation terms and ask about noise levels and airport transfer options.

Food, Culture and Etiquette

  • Dress modestly when visiting temples, with shoulders and knees covered.
  • Remove shoes where required and do not point your feet at Buddha images or monks.
  • Women should avoid touching monks or handing items directly to them.
  • Try local dishes such as khao soi, sai oua sausage and northern-style curries.
  • Bargain politely at markets, and support small local vendors.

Weather and Packing

November is usually the dry, cooler season in northern Thailand, with warm days and pleasant evenings. Pack light clothing, a light jacket for nights, comfortable walking shoes, a hat, sunscreen, insect repellent and a power bank. Bring a reusable bottle and a small bag for your hands-free needs. Keep a rain layer in case of unseasonal showers.

Mistakes First-Timers Make

  • Leaving tickets and accommodation to the last minute.
  • Expecting a quiet, uncrowded experience on the main nights.
  • Buying non-biodegradable krathongs or releasing lanterns in unauthorised places.
  • Not leaving buffer time for transport.
  • Ignoring local rules and safety instructions.

More Questions About the Chiang Mai Lantern Festivals

Can I buy a krathong at the festival?

Yes. Vendors sell krathongs along the streets and near the river in the days before and during the festival. Choose a natural one made from banana trunk, leaves and flowers.

Is it safe to attend with children?

Yes, with care. Crowds are dense, there are open flames and fireworks, and roads close or become congested. Keep children close, set a meeting point and avoid very crowded spots.

What if it rains on the festival night?

Events often continue unless conditions are unsafe, but lantern releases can be postponed or cancelled. Keep a flexible schedule, carry a light rain layer and check announcements.

Do I need to speak Thai?

No. Many people in tourist areas speak some English. Learning a few phrases such as hello and thank you is appreciated, and translation apps help.

How do I get around during the festival?

Walk where you can, use short rides when possible and allow extra time for traffic. App cars and taxis may be harder to find at night, so plan your return in advance.

Should I visit Bangkok or Chiang Mai?

Choose Chiang Mai if you want lanterns and a northern Thai atmosphere. Choose Bangkok if you prefer a large city with free river and park events focused on krathongs.

Final Tips Before You Go

Confirm the exact schedule with the Tourism Authority of Thailand and the Chiang Mai municipality shortly before you travel, since events and dates are announced late. Arrive in the city a day early, keep your phone charged and allow time for crowds. Remember that the festival is a spiritual tradition as well as a spectacle, so take part with respect.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Loy Krathong 2026?

Around November 24–25, 2026; confirm with the Tourism Authority of Thailand.

What is the difference between Loy Krathong and Yi Peng?

Loy Krathong floats offerings on water, while Yi Peng releases sky lanterns, mainly in Chiang Mai.

Where is the best place to celebrate?

Chiang Mai combines both festivals.

Do I need tickets for Yi Peng?

Big organised lantern releases are ticketed and sell out early; other celebrations are free to watch.

Can I release a sky lantern in Bangkok?

Sky lanterns belong to Yi Peng in the north; Bangkok's festival is water-based.

How early should I book accommodation?

As early as possible, since November is high season.
