Nyepi 2027 Bali Day of Silence: Rules for Tourists, Dates and How to Plan

November 4, 202612 min readby WanderLust Blog

For 24 hours, an entire island goes dark and silent: no flights, no traffic, no lights, and visitors are asked to stay where they are.

Nyepi is the Balinese Hindu New Year, and its main observance is the Day of Silence. Unlike most New Year celebrations, it is quiet. For 24 hours, the island of Bali stops: no one works, travels or goes out, and lights and noise are kept to a minimum. Even the international airport closes. For visitors, Nyepi is a unique experience, but it also comes with rules that are strictly enforced, so you must plan carefully. This guide explains the 2027 date, the four prohibitions, how the days around Nyepi unfold, what hotels allow, how to plan your arrival and departure and how to enjoy the festival respectfully.

Nyepi 2027 Date

Nyepi in 2027 falls on Tuesday, March 9. It is based on the Balinese Saka calendar and follows the new moon, so the date changes each year. The day before, Monday, March 8, is the evening of the Ogoh-ogoh parades and purification rituals, and the day after, Wednesday, March 10, is Ngembak Geni, when people begin to visit family and neighbours. In 2027, Nyepi falls one day before the expected Eid al-Fitr holiday in Indonesia, so the surrounding days are likely to be very busy with domestic travel; see our Ramadan 2027 travel guide. Always check the official Bali government and airport announcements for confirmation.

The Four Prohibitions of Nyepi

The Day of Silence is based on four traditional restrictions, known as Catur Brata Penyepian, which apply from 6 am on Nyepi until 6 am the following morning.

  • No fire or light: lights are kept off or covered at night, and curtains are drawn, so that the island appears dark from above.
  • No work or activity: businesses, shops and restaurants are closed, apart from essential services.
  • No travel: no one leaves their home, hotel or village. Roads are empty, and the airport is closed.
  • No entertainment or pleasure: loud music, television and parties are not allowed, and noise is kept to a minimum.

Traditional village security officers, known as pecalang, patrol the streets to enforce the rules, and tourists are expected to follow them. Leaving your hotel, going to the beach or being noisy can lead to a warning and may cause problems for your hosts.

What Visitors Must Do

  • Stay inside your hotel or villa grounds for the whole of the day, from the early morning until the next morning. You can usually use hotel pools and gardens quietly, but do not leave the property.
  • Keep lights low and curtains closed after dark, and avoid loud noise or music.
  • Do not use drones, fireworks or any open flame outdoors.
  • Plan your meals in advance. Hotels typically serve limited meals to guests, and delivery services are not available.
  • Be ready for limited connectivity. Mobile data and internet access across Bali have been restricted in recent years during Nyepi, although many hotels keep wi-fi for guests. Check with your hotel before you travel.

Airport and Transport: The Biggest Planning Point

Bali's international airport closes for about 24 hours during Nyepi, so there are no arrivals or departures. Flights are rescheduled or cancelled, and ports are also closed. This means you must plan your trip so that you are on the island, in your accommodation, for the full day. Do not book a flight arriving or departing on March 9, and be careful with early-morning flights on March 10, since the airport usually reopens only after the restriction ends and the schedule can be tight. Allow a buffer of at least one extra day on each side if you have onward connections. Hotels and taxis operate normally again on March 10.

The Days Before Nyepi: Rituals Worth Seeing

Melasti: purification by the sea

In the days before Nyepi, villages hold Melasti ceremonies, in which sacred objects from temples are carried in procession to the sea or a spring to be purified. Beaches fill with people dressed in white and traditional clothes. Visitors can watch from a respectful distance, but should not enter ceremonial areas or block processions.

Ogoh-ogoh parades on March 8

The evening before Nyepi, communities carry giant papier-mâché monsters, called ogoh-ogoh, through the streets accompanied by music and torches. They represent negative spirits, and the parades end with the figures being burned or ritually destroyed. Many towns, including Ubud, Denpasar and Sanur, hold parades. They are loud, crowded and exciting, so arrive early, keep a safe distance from torches and follow instructions from local organisers.

Tawur Kesanga

On the same day, villages hold purification rituals at crossroads, with offerings to restore balance before the new year. Observe quietly if you happen to pass one.

What to Do on Nyepi Day

The Day of Silence is a rare chance to rest. Many hotels offer special Nyepi packages, with limited meals, quiet activities such as yoga or meditation, and a chance to see the stars in an unusually dark sky. If you stay in a place without much light pollution, such as Ubud's outskirts or a rural villa, you may see the Milky Way. Bring a book, a journal or cards, and treat the day as a digital detox. If you travel with children, plan quiet games and snacks.

Where to Stay for Nyepi

  • A hotel or resort with a Nyepi programme: the easiest option. Staff understand the rules, and guests are taken care of with meals and activities.
  • A villa with a private pool: comfortable and private. Check that the host provides food and has a plan for meals.
  • A homestay in a village: a more cultural experience, but make sure the host explains the rules and provides meals.

Avoid staying in a place with limited facilities, and do not plan to find food or a taxi on the day. Book early, since many hotels fill up for Nyepi. Compare Bali with other island itineraries before you commit; Zentrevo's curated travel journeys are a helpful starting point for route ideas.

A 5-Day Plan Around Nyepi

Day 1 (March 6): Arrive and settle

Arrive in Bali, check in and rest. Visit a beach or temple in daylight to see Melasti preparations.

Day 2 (March 7): Culture and shopping

Visit temples and markets, stock up on snacks and water, and check your hotel's Nyepi arrangements.

Day 3 (March 8): Ogoh-ogoh evening

Spend the day relaxing, and in the evening, watch an Ogoh-ogoh parade. Return to the hotel by the end of the night, since the next day begins early.

Day 4 (March 9): Nyepi

Stay in your hotel. Enjoy a quiet day, eat the meals provided and stargaze at night.

Day 5 (March 10): Reopening

Enjoy the first morning after the silence, when the island returns to life. Visit family-friendly activities or leave with plenty of time.

Respect and Responsible Behaviour

  • Treat Nyepi as a religious observance, not as an inconvenience or a photo opportunity.
  • Wear respectful clothing for ceremonies, including a sarong and sash if you attend a temple.
  • Do not step on offerings, and do not point your feet at altars.
  • Do not leave your accommodation, even for a short walk, and avoid being on the beach.
  • Thank your hosts, and ask how you can help the rules run smoothly.

Budget Planning Without Guesswork

Prices for hotels and villas rise around Nyepi, and flights in the days before and after can be expensive. Build a budget from flights, accommodation for at least five nights, meals during the closed day, transport, ceremony-related costs such as sarongs and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare hotel packages, and check whether meals on March 9 are included. Choose refundable bookings if possible.

More Questions Visitors Ask

Can I leave my hotel on Nyepi?

No. Visitors are expected to stay within their accommodation for the entire period.

Can I use the pool or garden?

Usually yes, quietly and within the hotel grounds, but check with your hotel.

Will I have wi-fi?

Many hotels keep wi-fi for guests, but mobile data across the island may be limited. Download anything you need beforehand.

Is Nyepi a good time to visit Bali?

Yes, if you enjoy culture and rest. It is a poor time if you want a busy, active holiday or have fixed travel dates on March 9.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Booking a flight on March 9, or an early flight on March 10 without a buffer.
  • Leaving the hotel for a beach walk or a taxi ride.
  • Arriving without food, water and an offline entertainment plan.
  • Not checking whether your accommodation provides meals.
  • Treating the Ogoh-ogoh parade as a loud party rather than a ritual.

Final Checklist

  • Dates confirmed and flights booked away from March 9.
  • Accommodation with Nyepi meals and rules booked.
  • Modest clothing, a sarong, offline entertainment and a power bank packed.
  • A plan for the Ogoh-ogoh parade and the quiet day.
