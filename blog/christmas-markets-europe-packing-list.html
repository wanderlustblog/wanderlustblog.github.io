Christmas Markets in Europe Packing List: What to Wear and Bring for Cold Evenings

October 22, 20267 min readby WanderLust Blog

Christmas markets are outdoors, in the evening, and you're mostly standing still, which makes warmth and dry feet matter more than style.

Quick TakeDetails

Most importantWarm, waterproof, comfortable shoes

SkipHeavy bags and fragile souvenirs

Most people underestimate how cold it feels after two hours at an outdoor stall. A good packing list prevents that. Plan for standing, walking on wet cobbles, and moving between cold streets and warm cafés.

Clothing

  • Thermal base layer, especially for evenings.
  • A warm mid-layer such as fleece or wool.
  • A windproof, ideally waterproof, outer jacket.
  • Warm socks, plus a spare pair.
  • Hat, gloves and scarf.

Shoes

Choose insulated, waterproof shoes or boots with a good grip. Wet cobbles and cold stone floors are harder on your feet than any other part of the trip.

Small Items Worth Bringing

  • A small crossbody bag you can keep in front of you.
  • Cash for smaller stalls.
  • A portable phone charger; cold drains batteries quickly.
  • Hand warmers, lip balm and moisturiser.
  • A reusable bag for food and small purchases.

What to Skip

Skip heavy backpacks, fragile souvenirs you'd need to carry through crowds, and anything you'd hate to get stained with mulled wine.

The Layering System That Actually Works

Layering is not just a slogan. It is a system with three jobs. The base layer moves sweat away from your skin, the middle layer traps warmth, and the outer layer blocks wind and rain. If one layer fails, the whole system weakens, so choose each deliberately.

  • Base layer: thin thermal top and leggings in merino wool or synthetic fabric. Avoid cotton, which holds moisture and chills you.
  • Middle layer: fleece, wool or a light down jacket. This is the layer you add or remove as temperatures change between outdoors and warm cafés.
  • Outer layer: a windproof, water-resistant coat with a hood and enough length to cover your hips.

Feet, Hands and Head: The Three Places That Get Cold First

Most people lose comfort through their feet, hands and head long before their core. Pair thin liner socks with thicker wool socks, and keep a dry spare pair in your bag. Choose waterproof boots with a thick sole to insulate you from cold cobbles, and break them in before the trip. Gloves with touchscreen fingertips let you use a phone without exposing your hands. A warm hat that covers your ears and a scarf or neck gaiter make a large difference.

Packing List by Category

Clothing

  • 2 thermal base layers
  • 2 to 3 warm mid-layers or sweaters
  • 1 windproof, water-resistant coat
  • 1 pair of warm trousers and 1 spare pair
  • 4 to 6 pairs of socks, including wool socks
  • Hat, scarf, gloves and a spare pair of gloves

Footwear

  • Insulated waterproof boots with good grip
  • Comfortable indoor shoes for hotels and restaurants

Documents and money

  • Passport or ID, plus copies saved offline
  • Travel insurance details and emergency contacts
  • Cards, some local cash in small notes and a money belt or inner pocket
  • Train, hotel and attraction bookings saved on your phone

Electronics and small items

  • Phone, charger, power bank and plug adapter for the countries you visit
  • Offline maps downloaded; see our offline maps guide
  • Hand warmers, tissues, lip balm and a small first-aid kit
  • Reusable water bottle and a foldable shopping bag

Adjusting Your List by Region

Central and eastern Europe, including Vienna, Prague and Budapest, can feel very cold on clear evenings. Western regions such as Belgium and parts of France are often damper than colder, so waterproofing matters more than extra insulation. Mountain areas can be much colder and snowy, so add traction aids for icy paths and extra warm layers.

Packing for a Multi-City Trip

If you travel by train between cities, pack light enough to carry your own bag up stairs and through crowded stations. A cabin-size case or a backpack is usually easier than a large suitcase. Use packing cubes, wear your bulkiest items while travelling and plan to wash a few items if the trip is longer than a week. Many hotels have laundry options, and quick-dry thermal layers make this easy.

What Not to Bring

  • Heavy high heels or smooth-soled shoes that slip on wet cobbles.
  • Large backpacks that are hard to manage in crowds.
  • Fragile gifts you cannot protect in a small bag.
  • Too many cotton items, which dry slowly in damp weather.
  • Valuable jewellery that you would be upset to lose.

Staying Warm: Practical Habits

Eat something warm before you start a long evening, and drink hot drinks regularly. Alternate time outdoors with short breaks in cafés. Keep moving slightly, because standing still is what makes you cold. Change damp socks as soon as you can, and keep your phone warm in an inside pocket, since cold weather drains batteries quickly.

Final Pre-Trip Checklist

  • Check the weather forecast for each city two days before you leave.
  • Confirm official market dates and opening hours; see our Europe Christmas markets guide.
  • Pack essentials in your cabin bag in case checked luggage is delayed.
  • Charge devices and download tickets and maps.
  • Leave space in your bag for souvenirs.

More Packing Questions Answered

How many layers do I really need?

Three is usually enough: a thermal base, a warm middle layer and a windproof outer layer. Add a fourth, such as a light down vest, if you are visiting very cold regions or are sensitive to cold. It is better to remove a layer than to be cold all evening.

Are jeans fine for Christmas markets?

They work for short visits but hold cold and damp. Lined trousers, thermal leggings under trousers or softshell pants perform better for long evenings outdoors.

Do I need a special coat?

Not necessarily, but it should cover your hips, block wind and have a hood. A coat that you can wear over multiple layers is more useful than a very thick but tight one.

What should I carry in my day bag?

A small crossbody bag with your phone, power bank, wallet, tissues, hand warmers, lip balm, a reusable bag and a small umbrella or rain layer. Keep it zipped and in front of you in crowds.

Can I buy winter gear when I arrive?

Yes, most cities sell hats, gloves and scarves, and they make good souvenirs. However, waterproof boots are better bought and broken in before you travel.

What about luggage size?

A carry-on suitcase or a backpack is easiest for trains and cobbled streets. Packing light also leaves space for gifts and edible souvenirs.

How do I dry wet clothes quickly in a hotel?

Hang them over a radiator at a safe distance, use the bathroom fan or heated towel rail, and roll damp items in a dry towel to squeeze out moisture. Bring quick-drying fabrics to help.

Is travel insurance worth it for a short winter trip?

Yes. Winter weather can disrupt flights and trains, and insurance can cover cancellations, delays, medical care and lost luggage. Check what is included and any limits before you buy.

Final Tips Before You Pack

Do a test run before you travel. Wear your full outfit, including boots, for an hour outdoors on a cold evening, and check where you feel cold, tight or uncomfortable. Replace anything that fails the test while you are still at home.

Think about the journey as well as the destination. Airports and trains can be hot while the streets outside are freezing, so keep layers easy to remove and store. Keep a small pouch with your essentials, such as medication, charger and documents, within easy reach.

Finally, leave a little spare room for souvenirs. Ornaments, gingerbread, tea and mulled wine spices are popular gifts, but they are fragile or heavy, so pack them carefully and check airline rules for liquids.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I wear to a Christmas market?

Layers: a thermal base, warm mid-layer and a windproof jacket, plus insulated, waterproof shoes.

Do I need a hat and gloves?

Yes. You will be standing outside for hours, even in less extreme weather.

Should I bring cash?

Yes, smaller stalls may not take cards.

What bag should I carry?

A small crossbody bag you can keep in front of you in crowds.

Are comfortable shoes more important than looks?

Usually yes, because cobbled streets and long standing make cold and discomfort the main problem.

Should I bring a power bank?

Yes, cold weather drains phone batteries faster.
