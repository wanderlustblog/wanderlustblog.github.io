Dev Deepawali Varanasi 2026: Date, Best Views, Boat Tips and Travel Plan

October 23, 202611 min readby WanderLust Blog

Two weeks after Diwali, Varanasi holds a second festival of lights, and for many travellers it is the more beautiful one, because the whole riverfront glows at once.

Quick TakeDetails
DateTuesday, November 24, 2026 (Kartik Purnima; confirm locally)
WhereThe ghats of Varanasi along the Ganga
Best viewFrom a boat on the river, booked well ahead
BookHotels and boats sell out weeks in advance

Dev Deepawali, often described as the Diwali of the gods, is celebrated on Kartik Purnima, the full-moon day that falls roughly fifteen days after Diwali. In Varanasi, devotees and local authorities light a very large number of earthen lamps along the ghats, temples glow, and the river fills with boats. It combines a religious bathing festival with one of the most photogenic evenings in India. This guide explains the date, where to watch, how to organise boats and hotels, and how to avoid the problems that catch first-time visitors.

Dev Deepawali 2026: Date and Timing

Dev Deepawali falls on Kartik Purnima, which is on November 24, 2026 by most calendars. The same full moon is also the climax of the Pushkar Camel Fair and, in Thailand, the Loy Krathong lantern festival, so it is a good week for festival-minded travellers. Exact timings of rituals depend on the lunar calendar and local arrangements, so check official announcements from the Uttar Pradesh tourism department and your hotel shortly before travel. Evening lamp lighting starts around sunset, and the main atmosphere builds in the first two or three hours after dark.

What Actually Happens

  • Holy dips at dawn: on Kartik Purnima many pilgrims bathe in the Ganga at sunrise, so the ghats are busy from the early morning.
  • Lamps on the ghats: from late afternoon, volunteers, families and temple staff line the steps with diyas. By dark, the riverfront from Assi Ghat to Rajghat can look like a continuous strip of light.
  • Ganga Aarti: the evening ceremony at Dashashwamedh Ghat is especially crowded and elaborate on this day.
  • Boats and floating lamps: many visitors watch from the river, and some float small lamps on the water.
  • Cultural events and fireworks: the programme can include music, performances and fireworks, which vary by year.

Where to Watch: Land vs River

From the river you see the full line of lit ghats and the aarti from a distance. It is the best way to understand the scale, but boats are limited in number, prices rise sharply, and authorities may restrict movement. From the ghats you feel the atmosphere, hear the chanting and can see details, but crowds are dense, movement is slow and the steps are crowded and uneven. From a rooftop café or hotel terrace you get a calm vantage point with a view over the ghats. If you can, combine two: watch the early evening from a rooftop or ghat, and then take a boat for a short time once the lamps are lit. Avoid being on a boat during the busiest part of the ghat aarti if you are not comfortable with tightly packed craft.

A Simple 3-Day Varanasi Plan Around Dev Deepawali

Day 1: Arrive and orient yourself

Arrive by midday if you can. Settle into your hotel, walk the old lanes in daylight so you understand the layout, and watch the Ganga Aarti from the ghat steps or a boat. Eat early, since lanes get busy later and some places close or run out of food.

Day 2: Sunrise, Sarnath and the festival evening

Take a sunrise boat ride before the heaviest crowds, then rest. In the afternoon visit Sarnath, the Buddhist site a short drive from the city, which is calm compared with the ghats. Return by mid-afternoon, book your boat or rooftop spot, and spend the evening watching the lamps.

Day 3: Quiet morning and departure

Take a slow morning walk, visit a temple if you wish and shop for silk and handicrafts. Leave extra time for the airport or station, since streets can be congested after the festival.

Booking Timeline That Works

  1. 6 to 8 weeks before: book hotels, ideally in or near the old city or close to the ghats, with free cancellation if possible.
  2. 3 to 4 weeks before: book flights or trains. See our guide to using trains in India as a foreigner.
  3. 1 to 2 weeks before: contact your hotel about boat options and rooftop viewing. Ask them to recommend registered boatmen.
  4. 2 to 3 days before: confirm the schedule, security rules and transport, and check the weather and fog forecast.

With this date so close, if you are reading this late, widen your search to a hotel a few kilometres from the ghats and rely on a taxi or e-rickshaw for the evening.

Boat Tips: Prices, Safety and Scams

  • Agree the price, duration and route before you board, and confirm whether it is per boat or per person.
  • Ask whether lifejackets are available, and avoid overloaded boats.
  • Use hotel-recommended boatmen where you can, and avoid touts who approach you aggressively.
  • Do not hand over money for flowers or lamps until you are sure of the price, and be wary of anyone who insists you must make an offering. Our tourist scams guide explains the common patterns.

Where to Stay

Staying close to the ghats saves time and lets you return to rest when crowds peak, but lanes are narrow and your luggage may need to be carried on foot for a short distance. Hotels farther out offer easier road access and quieter nights, but you must plan transport carefully on festival night, when roads close and traffic builds. Ask about check-in times, noise levels and hot water, since December mornings can be cool.

Food to Try in Varanasi

Varanasi is known for its street food and sweets. Try kachori with sabzi in the morning, tamatar chaat, lassi, and in winter a frothy dairy dessert often called malaiyyo. Betel-leaf paan is a local favourite. Choose busy stalls with fresh turnover, drink bottled or filtered water and carry hand sanitiser. If you have dietary restrictions, ask clearly, since oil and ghee are used widely.

Etiquette and Respectful Behaviour

  • Dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered, and remove shoes at temples.
  • Do not photograph bathers, cremation areas or private rituals. Ask permission before photographing people.
  • Keep your voice low near the ghats and during rituals, and do not litter or throw items into the river.
  • If you float a lamp, choose a natural one and follow local guidance.

Safety and Crowd Management

Crowds on festival night can be extreme. Keep your phone and wallet in front pockets or a zipped bag, agree a meeting point if you are in a group, and avoid carrying valuables. Keep to the edges of crowds if you feel unsafe, and follow police and volunteer instructions. Carry a small torch, because lanes can be dark away from the ghats. Keep a photocopy or photo of your passport separate from the original.

What to Pack

Comfortable closed shoes, light layers for cool evenings, a scarf, a power bank, a small torch, wet wipes, a basic first-aid kit and a reusable bottle. See what to wear in India as a tourist for dress guidance. A phone with a good low-light camera is enough; avoid bringing large equipment into crowds.

Combining Dev Deepawali With Other Festival Trips

Many travellers pair Varanasi with the Pushkar Camel Fair, which finishes on the same full moon, or with the Diwali celebrations earlier in November. Because both places are very busy on the same night, choose one for the full moon and visit the other earlier or later. If you are building a longer India route, our India itinerary for first-time visitors shows how to link Varanasi with Delhi, Agra and Jaipur.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Arriving on the festival day without accommodation or a clear plan.
  • Hiring a boat from the first person who approaches you.
  • Scheduling an early flight the next morning and risking a missed connection after congested roads.
  • Expecting a quiet, solitary spiritual experience on the busiest night of the year.
  • Ignoring fog and weather, which can delay trains and flights in winter.

Final Checklist

  • Passport, visa and insurance in order, and copies saved offline.
  • Hotel, transport and boat or rooftop plan confirmed.
  • Modest clothing, shoes you can walk in, a power bank and a torch packed.
  • A plan for meeting points, emergency contacts and getting back to your hotel.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Dev Deepawali 2026?

Dev Deepawali falls on Kartik Purnima, which is November 24, 2026 by most calendars. Confirm locally closer to the date.

What is the difference between Diwali and Dev Deepawali?

Diwali is the main festival of lights in autumn, while Dev Deepawali, held on Kartik Purnima about two weeks later, is the festival of lamps on Varanasi's ghats.

Where is the best place to watch Dev Deepawali?

From a boat on the Ganga for the full view, or from the ghats or a rooftop for atmosphere. Book early either way.

How early should I book a hotel in Varanasi?

At least six to eight weeks ahead if possible, since rooms near the ghats sell out.

Is Dev Deepawali safe for tourists?

Generally yes, with care. Expect heavy crowds, keep valuables secure and follow instructions from local authorities.

Can I combine Dev Deepawali with Pushkar?

Both happen around the same full moon, so you usually need to choose one for that night.
