Sometimes the trip comes first and the paperwork second. A long weekend appears, a flight fare drops or a friend says "come with us", and you want to know where an Indian passport can actually take you without waiting for a visa decision. The honest answer is: quite a few places, but the rules are more conditional than most lists admit, and the last-minute traveller is the one who gets caught out.

This guide sorts last-minute options into those that are easy, those that need a quick online step before you fly and those that look easy but are not. It also gives you a checklist for the airport, because most visa-on-arrival failures happen at check-in, not at the destination. Visa on Arrival, e-VOA, ETA and Visa-Free: What the Words Mean

The labels matter, because they change what you must do before you board.

Visa on arrival (VOA)

You get the visa at the destination airport or border, usually by paying a fee and showing documents.

e-VOA or ETA

You apply online before you fly and carry the approval. Some countries now require this even where walk-up VOA used to exist.

Visa-free

No visa needed, but you may still need a digital arrival card or proof of funds.

Conditional VOA

Only available if you hold certain visas or residence permits, such as US, UK or Schengen visas.

Last-Minute Destinations That Work for Many Indian Travellers

Use the filter to see destinations by how much you must do before you fly. Details come from a September 2026 guide and a Wikipedia table, and they change, so confirm each one.

Cambodia

30 daysVOA or e-visa

The guide lists a visa on arrival for 30 days at about $30 in cash, with one passport photo and an e-Arrival card. Wikipedia lists a fee of $36 and also an e-visa option, so the amount differs between sources. Check the current price.

Laos

30 daysVOA or e-visa

A 30-day visa on arrival at certain airports and border crossings, with about $40 in cash and a photo according to the guide. Bring exact USD.

Jordan

30 daysVOA or e-visa

A 30-day visa on arrival at about 40 JOD, which the guide says is waived with a Jordan Pass and three or more consecutive nights. Check the pass terms.

Mongolia

30 daysVOA or e-visa

A 30-day visa on arrival at about $50, at Chinggis Khaan airport only, according to the guide.

Myanmar

30 daysVOA or e-visa

A 30-day visa on arrival at about $50 in cash, at Yangon, Mandalay and Nay Pyi Taw airports. Check current travel advice before booking.

Tanzania (Zanzibar)

90 daysVOA or e-visa

A 90-day option at about $50 in cash, with crisp undamaged USD notes, a return ticket and hotel proof, according to the guide.

Indonesia (Bali)

30 dayse-VOA

A 30-day e-VOA at about IDR 500,000, which you can apply for before flying or pay at the airport. A customs card must be submitted within three days of landing, and a Bali tourist tax applies.

Sri Lanka

30 daysETA

The guide says the ETA is not a walk-in option and should be applied for at the official site before flying. It lists it as free for 30 days, but Wikipedia lists a free ETA or VOA. Apply online first.

Kenya

90 dayseTA

The guide says visa on arrival has ended and an eTA is needed, costing about $30 to $34. Apply at least three days before travel.

Seychelles

90 daysVisitor permit

A free visitor permit for up to 90 days, with registration on the official system before departure, according to the guide.

Thailand

Visa-freeDigital arrival card

Guides describe Indians as visa-free for Thailand, with an online Thailand Digital Arrival Card to submit before arrival and possible proof-of-funds checks of THB 10,000 per person. Stay lengths differ between sources, so check the current limit.

United Arab Emirates

14 daysConditional

Visa on arrival is available only if you hold a valid US, UK, EU or similar visa or residence, for 14 days at about AED 100. A UK visa alone no longer qualifies unless residency criteria are met, according to one guide. Without one, apply for an e-visa first.

Oman

14 daysConditional

A conditional 14-day option that depends on holding a valid visa or residence from a listed country. Fees are vague in the guide.

Qatar

30 daysConditions

Listed as an exemption or VOA for 30 days, with a passport valid for six months, a return ticket and a confirmed hotel. The guide's classification is unclear, so verify.

Why Last-Minute VOA Trips Go Wrong

The destination is rarely the problem. The problems are at home.

  • The airline will not board you. Airlines check entry documents at check-in, and a missing return ticket, short passport validity or unmet condition can stop you before you reach the gate.
  • Passport validity. The guide says to have at least six months of validity beyond entry and at least two blank facing pages.
  • Cash only. Several countries want the exact fee in US dollars in clean notes. The guide recommends crisp, uncreased notes printed in 2013 or later, and advises against airport forex counters because of markups of 10 to 20 percent.
  • Pre-registration. Some places now need an online card or ETA even where you used to pay on arrival.
  • Outdated lists. Policies change. Cape Verde, Kenya and others have moved between systems in recent guides.

The Airport Document Pack

Prepare one folder, printed and on your phone.

  1. Passport check. Confirm at least six months of validity and two blank facing pages.
  2. Return or onward ticket. Print it. Airlines and immigration often ask.
  3. First night's hotel. Keep a booking you can show, ideally refundable.
  4. Online approval. Print any ETA, e-VOA or digital arrival card confirmation.
  5. Passport photos. Carry two spares, because some counters still ask.
  6. Exact cash. Take the visa fee in the right currency in clean notes, plus a little extra.
  7. Insurance. Keep your policy handy. Our insurance guide for Indians explains what to look for.

Have a Backup Plan

Because last-minute rules can shift overnight, keep a plan B. Book refundable flights and hotels where you can, and shortlist a second destination with a different entry route. If your first choice needs an online approval that has not arrived by the day before your flight, you can pivot to a visa-free option rather than lose the whole trip.

  • Choose flexible fares if the price difference is small.
  • Save official links for both destinations, so you can recheck the rules in minutes.
  • Tell someone your plan and share your itinerary, especially if you are travelling alone.
  • Read the airline's travel-document page on the day of the flight, since airlines publish their own entry checks.

Final Thoughts

Last-minute travel from India is possible, and the best options are the ones where you only need an online card or a quick approval. Treat visa on arrival as a convenience, not a guarantee, check the rules the same day you book and carry everything the airline might ask for. Then the spontaneous trip stays fun.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which countries give visa on arrival to Indians?

Guides list countries such as Cambodia, Laos, Jordan, Mongolia, Myanmar, Tanzania and others, plus conditional options like the UAE and Oman. Rules change, so confirm on official sites.

Can I travel to the UAE with visa on arrival from India?

Only if you hold certain valid visas or residence, such as US, UK or EU, for 14 days at about AED 100, according to guides. Without one, apply for an e-visa first.

Do I need to apply online before a visa on arrival?

For some countries, yes. Sri Lanka and Kenya require an online ETA, and Indonesia offers an e-VOA. Check each destination before you fly.

How much cash do I need for visa on arrival?

Fees in the guides range from about $30 to $90 depending on the country. Many want exact US dollars in clean notes, so carry the right amount.

What is the biggest risk with last-minute visa on arrival trips?

The airline refusing to board you because of missing documents, short passport validity or unmet conditions. Check the airline's rules and carry printed proof.

Is Thailand visa-free for Indians?

Guides describe it as visa-free with an online digital arrival card, though stay lengths differ between sources and proof of funds may be checked. Confirm the current rule.
