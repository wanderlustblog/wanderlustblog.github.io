Ubud vs Canggu for Digital Nomads: WiFi, Coworking and Monthly Cost

November 27, 20269 min readby WanderLust Blog

Ubud or Canggu for remote work? This guide compares wifi, coworking, monthly costs, traffic and lifestyle from published guides, and shows how to test your connection before you commit.

Bali has two famous bases for remote workers: Ubud, in the jungle-and-rice-field uplands, and Canggu, on the surf coast. Both have coworking spaces, cafes and large nomad communities. They feel very different, and they behave differently when it matters most to a remote worker: wifi that holds through a video call, and a monthly budget that does not surprise you.

This guide compares them on wifi, coworking, cost, traffic and lifestyle, using published guides, with honest warnings about where the data is weak. If you want to explore the quieter side of the Ubud area, read Ubud villages without crowds in Bali, and if you are checking entry rules, see our last-minute trips from India guide.

Read this about the numbersThe Ubud and Canggu figures come from different sources with different methods, so they are not a like-for-like comparison. The Ubud page we read contradicts itself in places, for example calling the internet fast and reliable while its own data shows 14 Mbps labelled slow. A coworking article is published by a Canggu beach club and may be promotional. Treat every number as a rough guide, and test wifi yourself before you commit to a month.

The Short Version

Canggu is the social, surf-and-coworking hub, with heavy traffic and more consistent coworking infrastructure. Ubud is greener and calmer, with wellness and creative communities, a hot desk market and wifi that varies by place. Neither is cheap or fast everywhere, and both reward you for testing the connection before you book.

WiFi: What the Numbers Say

Wifi is the make-or-break factor, and it is also where sources disagree most.

  • Ubud, general. A nomad-data page, updated in November 2026, lists an average internet speed of 14 Mbps and labels it slow, even though its text describes the internet as fast and reliable. We cannot reconcile that, so test locally.
  • Ubud, coworking. A coworking guide published by a Canggu beach club reports about 200 Mbps at one space, Ubud.Space, from the author's test, and describes another space's wifi as unreliable. It gives no speed for most spaces.
  • Canggu, coworking. A Canggu guide gives 50 to 100 Mbps generally, with Dojo Bali at 60 to 100, Outpost at 50 to 90 and BWork at 40 to 80 Mbps.
  • Canggu, villas. The same guide says villa wifi is 10 to 80 Mbps depending on setup, a 4G router gives 10 to 30 Mbps on a good day and 3 to 10 Mbps during evening peaks, and Starlink villas reach 50 to 150 Mbps.
  • Mobile data. In Canggu, 15 to 40 Mbps on 4G, with the author measuring 20 to 40 Mbps in central Canggu and 10 to 25 Mbps in Pererenan.

The takeaway: in both places, coworking is the safe option for important calls, and villa wifi is a gamble unless you test it.

Coworking Costs

Coworking prices are more consistent than villa wifi, though the sources use different currencies and dates.

Ubud day pass

A coworking guide gives a starting day pass of IDR 120,000 (about 11.5 AUD) for shared space. It gives no date for its prices.

Ubud monthly

The nomad-data page lists a coworking hot desk at about $200 a month. The coworking guide quotes Outpost Ubud at around 290 AUD a month for a hot desk. A Canggu guide lists Hubud in Ubud at $14 a day and $168 a month.

Canggu day pass

From $9 at BWork to $15 at Outpost Canggu, according to a Canggu guide.

Canggu monthly

From about $112 at BWork to $186 at Outpost Canggu, with Dojo Bali at $155 and Tropical Nomad at $137.

Outpost's membership covers both its Ubud and Canggu locations, according to the coworking guide, which is useful if you want to move between the two. Prices differ between sources, so confirm directly.

Monthly Cost Compared

Be careful here: the sources do not measure costs the same way. The Ubud page lists a nomad's monthly cost at $1,939, a hotel median of about $24 per night, an Airbnb median of about $90 per night, a centre studio at $1,159 per month and a dinner at $3. It contradicts itself on housing and meal costs. The Canggu guide gives ranges for a single person based in Canggu: budget $653 to 1,055, comfortable $1,005 to 1,520 and premium $1,880 to 3,580 per month, plus rents of $250 to 800 per month depending on area.

Lifestyle: Which Feels Like You?

Open the tab for each place.

Ubud

Calm and greenWellness

The nomad-data page lists pros such as fast internet (disputed by its own data), many coworking spaces, affordability, beautiful surroundings and a strong expat community, and cons such as heavy traffic, not walkable, dirty, overdeveloped, noisy and humid. It scores the city well for fun and safety. If you want quieter surroundings, see Ubud villages without crowds.

Canggu

Social and sportyBusy

A Canggu guide calls it the nomad capital, with cafes, coworking, surf and a large community. Batu Bolong is the social centre, Pererenan is quieter with rice paddies and a developing cafe scene, and Berawa is the most developed with restaurants and beach clubs, though it can feel overdeveloped.

Traffic and Getting Around

Both are scooter-reliant. The Canggu guide says main roads gridlock between 8 and 10 am and 5 and 7 pm, and a 2 km trip takes about 5 minutes at noon and up to 25 minutes at rush hour. It lists scooter rental at IDR 700,000 to 1,200,000 per month ($43 to 74), says an International Driving Permit with a motorcycle endorsement is needed and quotes a fine of IDR 250,000. A Grab ride from Canggu to Ubud costs IDR 100,000 to 150,000 ($6 to 9). A villa company says Ubud scooters cost about IDR 50,000 to 85,000 per day. In Ubud, outside the centre, walking is hard because of hills and missing sidewalks.

How to Test WiFi Before You Commit

The Canggu guide gives sensible advice that works in Ubud too.

  1. Ask the host what kind of connection it is. Fibre, 4G router or Starlink.
  2. Request a screenshot of a speed test taken on a weekday around 8 pm.
  3. Check the upload speed as well as download if you take video calls.
  4. Carry backup data. The guide says a Telkomsel Tourist 35GB SIM costs IDR 100,000 ($6).
  5. Use coworking for critical calls, and keep a plan for power outages.
  6. Book a short first stay so you can leave if the wifi fails.

Practical Things Nomads Forget

  • Visa. The Canggu guide lists the visa process as a con that requires planning. Check Indonesia's rules, and see our visa-on-arrival guide for the e-VOA basics.
  • Health. The guide advises watching for dengue and getting medical evacuation coverage. Read our travel insurance guide for Indians.
  • Power. Expect occasional outages, and keep devices charged.
  • Water and cash. The Ubud page notes tap water is not drinkable and that cash is preferred.
  • Humidity. The Ubud page lists high humidity and air conditioning in only a small share of buildings.

Which Should You Choose?

Choose Canggu if you want a social, surf-friendly base with lots of coworking and a big community, and you can tolerate traffic. Choose Ubud if you want greener surroundings, wellness and a calmer feel, and you will check your wifi carefully. Many nomads split their time, and Outpost's membership covers both towns according to the coworking guide. Whichever you choose, book a month first.

Final Thoughts

Ubud and Canggu are both workable bases, and the right one depends on how you want to live. Treat the numbers as rough, test the wifi, start with a short stay and keep a backup for calls. Then you can decide with real experience, not just a ranking.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ubud or Canggu better for digital nomads?

Canggu is the social, coworking-heavy hub with more traffic, and Ubud is greener and calmer with wellness communities. Both are workable. Test the wifi and start with a short stay.

How fast is wifi in Ubud and Canggu?

Sources disagree. A nomad-data page lists 14 Mbps average for Ubud, one coworking space tested at about 200 Mbps, and a Canggu guide lists coworking at 40 to 100 Mbps and villas at 10 to 80 Mbps. Test locally.

How much does coworking cost in Bali?

A Canggu guide lists day passes from $9 to $15 and monthly from about $112 to $186. A coworking guide gives an Ubud day pass from about IDR 120,000 and a nomad-data page lists a hot desk at about $200 a month.

How much does it cost to live in Canggu per month?

A Canggu guide gives $653 to 1,055 for a budget single person, $1,005 to 1,520 for comfortable and $1,880 to 3,580 for premium, plus rents of $250 to 800 depending on area.

Do I need a backup internet connection in Bali?

Yes. The Canggu guide recommends backup data, such as a Telkomsel Tourist SIM with 35GB for about IDR 100,000, and using coworking for critical calls.

Can I use one coworking membership in both Ubud and Canggu?

A coworking guide says Outpost's membership covers both its Ubud and Canggu locations. Confirm the current terms with Outpost.
