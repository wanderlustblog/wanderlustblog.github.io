Using Trains in India as a Foreigner: A Complete Guide for Tourists

September 3, 202612 min readby WanderLust Blog

Using trains in India as a foreigner looks intimidating from the outside — the class codes, the waitlists, the sheer scale of the network — and turns out to be far more manageable once the pieces are laid out plainly.

Indian Railways is one of the largest rail networks in the world, and for tourists specifically, it's often the most rewarding way to move between cities: comfortable in the right class, genuinely scenic, and a much better way to see the country than staring at the back of an airplane seat. This deep-dive builds on the broader overview in our complete transport guide for India, which compares trains against flights, buses, and city transport at a higher level.

The Basics

At a high level, using trains in India as a foreigner involves four steps: pick your route and preferred class, book through IRCTC (India's official railway booking platform) or an authorized agent, check your booking status as your travel date approaches, and arrive at the station with time to spare and your documents in hand.

Indian Train Classes: Which One Suits Tourists

Indian Railways offers a range of classes, each identified by a short code you'll see throughout the booking process:

For most first-time foreign visitors, 2A or 3A strike the most practical balance — air-conditioned, reasonably comfortable for overnight travel, and considerably less expensive than 1A. Sleeper Class is worth trying for travelers who want a more immersive, budget-conscious experience, but it's a noticeably more intense environment than the AC classes. Amenities can vary by specific train, so it's worth checking the details of your particular service when booking rather than assuming every train in a given class is identical. Booking Tickets: IRCTC, ID Requirements, and the Foreign Tourist Quota

Foreign tourists and NRIs can book directly through IRCTC's official e-ticketing website, and there's a dedicated Foreign Tourist Quota (FTQ) worth knowing about:

  • FTQ bookings can be made up to 365 days in advance, well beyond the standard advance-booking window available to domestic travelers.
  • FTQ is available in the higher classes only — 1A, 2A, and EC — so it's not an option if you're aiming for 3A, SL, or CC.
  • Registration on IRCTC requires your passport details, and you'll need your original passport and valid visa with you during the journey for verification if you've booked under FTQ.
  • If FTQ availability or classes don't suit your plans, general quota booking — the same system domestic travelers use — is also open to foreign tourists, with a shorter advance-booking window.

Because booking rules, fees, and advance-booking windows are periodically updated, it's worth checking IRCTC's official website directly for the current details before you book, rather than relying solely on older guides, including this one.

Understanding Waitlists, RAC, and Confirmed Tickets

Ticket status is where most first-time confusion happens, so it's worth understanding the terms clearly:

  • CNF (Confirmed): You have a guaranteed seat or berth. Your exact coach and berth number are typically assigned when the final chart is prepared, usually a few hours before departure.
  • RAC (Reservation Against Cancellation): You're guaranteed to travel, but initially share a berth (commonly a side-lower) with another RAC passenger. If confirmed passengers cancel before the chart is finalized, RAC tickets are often upgraded to a full berth.
  • WL (Waitlisted): You don't yet have a confirmed seat or berth. Your position can improve as other passengers cancel, but if you're still waitlisted once the final chart is prepared, the ticket typically isn't valid for travel.

You can check your ticket's current status using your 10-digit PNR (Passenger Name Record) number through IRCTC's website or app. If your ticket remains waitlisted close to departure, it's worth having a backup plan — an alternative train, a bus, or a flight — rather than assuming it will confirm.

Cancellations and Changes

Cancellation policies and any applicable charges vary by how far in advance you cancel and the class booked, and these details are also periodically updated by Indian Railways. Rather than quoting a specific figure here, the reliable approach is to check the current cancellation terms directly on IRCTC at the time of booking, so you know exactly what applies to your specific ticket.

What to Expect at Indian Railway Stations

Major Indian railway stations are large, busy, and can feel disorienting on a first visit, but a few things make navigating them considerably easier:

  • Arrive with time to spare, particularly at major stations in Delhi, Mumbai, or other large cities, where finding your platform and coach can take longer than expected.
  • Check the electronic display boards for your train number and platform, and confirm with station staff if anything's unclear.
  • Coach position indicators on the platform show roughly where your specific coach will stop, which is worth locating before the train arrives rather than during the scramble once it does.
  • Official porters, where used, typically wear a visible uniform or badge — agree on a rate before they start carrying your bags.

Luggage, Food, and Onboard Facilities

  • Luggage: Keep valuables in your carry-on rather than checked or overhead storage, and use a padlock or cable lock for extra security on longer overnight journeys, particularly in shared berths.
  • Food: Many long-distance trains offer onboard catering, and vendors also sell food and drinks at station stops along the route. For food safety, stick to freshly prepared items and bottled water rather than anything that's been sitting out, the same general caution that applies to street food anywhere in India.
  • Toilets: Both Indian-style (squat) and Western-style toilets are typically available on longer-distance trains, though facilities and cleanliness can vary by train age and route. Carrying your own tissue or wipes is a sensible habit.
  • Sleeping arrangements: In AC sleeper classes (1A, 2A, 3A), bedding is generally provided; in Sleeper Class, it isn't, so pack a light sheet or sleep sack if you're booking SL overnight.

Safety and Valuables

  • Keep your passport, visa, and valuables in a money belt or a bag you can keep close and secured, particularly overnight.
  • A cable lock to secure your bag to a fixed point in your berth area is a low-effort habit some travelers find reassuring on longer journeys.
  • Be cautious of unsolicited food or drink offered by strangers onboard — while the overwhelming majority of fellow passengers are simply friendly, isolated incidents involving spiked food or drink have been reported on Indian trains over the years, and declining politely costs nothing.
  • Keep your berth or seat area tidy and be aware of your immediate surroundings when the train stops at unfamiliar stations, particularly at night.

Common Train Mistakes and Scams to Avoid

  • Booking through unverified third-party websites instead of IRCTC directly or clearly authorized agents, which can lead to inflated prices or invalid tickets.
  • Trusting unsolicited "help" near ticket counters or platforms, a pattern that sometimes leads travelers toward unofficial services or misdirection rather than the counter or platform they actually needed — the same pattern covered more broadly in our tourist scams guide.
  • Not carrying ID during FTQ travel — this specific quota requires your original passport and visa to be produced on request, and not having them available can result in being treated as ticketless.
  • Assuming a waitlisted ticket will confirm without a backup plan, which can leave you stranded if it doesn't clear before the chart is finalized.
  • Leaving valuables unattended, even briefly, in a shared compartment.

Practical Tips for First-Time Foreign Travelers

  • Book AC classes (2A or 3A) for your first Indian train journey if comfort and predictability matter more to you than saving money.
  • Download the IRCTC app or a reputable train-tracking app to monitor your PNR status and live running times.
  • Carry a printed or offline copy of your ticket and ID documents in case connectivity is patchy at the station.
  • Pack snacks and a refillable water bottle for longer journeys, even if you plan to rely on onboard catering.
  • Build in a buffer before any connecting travel, since Indian trains can run behind schedule, particularly during peak season.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can foreign tourists book Indian train tickets online?

Yes, through IRCTC's official website, including a dedicated Foreign Tourist Quota with its own advance-booking window and available classes.

What is the difference between 2A and 3A train classes?

Both are air-conditioned sleeper classes; 2A offers berths in bays of four with curtains for some privacy, while 3A has three berth tiers and generally no curtains, at a more moderate price.

What does a waitlisted (WL) train ticket mean?

It means you don't yet have a confirmed seat or berth; your position can improve with cancellations, but the ticket typically isn't valid for travel if it's still waitlisted once the final chart is prepared.

What is RAC on an Indian train ticket?

Reservation Against Cancellation — you're guaranteed to travel and share a berth initially, with a chance of upgrading to a full berth if confirmed passengers cancel.

Do I need to carry my passport when traveling by train in India?

If you've booked under the Foreign Tourist Quota, yes — your original passport and visa are required for verification during the journey.

Is Sleeper Class safe and comfortable for foreign tourists?

It's generally safe and a genuinely local experience, though noticeably more basic than the AC classes — no AC, no provided bedding, and closer quarters, which is worth knowing before booking for an overnight trip.

How far in advance should I book Indian train tickets?

As early as your plans allow — general quota has a shorter advance-booking window than the Foreign Tourist Quota's 365-day allowance, and popular routes can fill up, especially in peak season.

Is it safe to eat food sold on Indian trains?

Freshly prepared onboard catering and food from established vendors is generally fine; use the same caution you would with street food anywhere, and stick to bottled water.

What should I do if my train ticket remains waitlisted close to departure?

Have a backup plan — an alternative train, bus, or flight — since a still-waitlisted ticket typically isn't valid for travel once the chart is finalized.

Are Indian railway stations safe for solo foreign travelers?

Generally yes, with the same standard precautions that apply to any busy, crowded transport hub — staying aware of your surroundings, securing valuables, and being cautious of unsolicited help from strangers.
