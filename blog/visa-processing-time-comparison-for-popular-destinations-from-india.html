Most travellers ask the same question before they pick a destination: how long will the visa take? The official answer and the real answer are rarely the same. A portal may promise three working days, but add the appointment wait, the courier and a public holiday, and you are suddenly in week three. That gap is where trips get stressful.

This guide compares processing times for popular destinations from India: UAE, Thailand, Schengen, UK, Singapore, Japan, the USA and a few others. It separates the official time from the realistic end-to-end time, and it shows how to plan backwards from your travel date. If you already have a destination in mind, our step-by-step guides for Morocco, Japan and Italy go deeper.

Treat these as estimatesMost numbers here come from one travel-services article updated in August 2026 for Indian passport holders applying from India, with a few cross-checks from other guides and a Japanese government checklist. The article is approximate, links few official pages and has small inconsistencies, such as UK Super Priority timing. Check each embassy and portal for current times before you book anything non-refundable. We are not a visa agency.

Official Time vs Real Time

The official time is how long the visa authority says it takes to decide once your application is complete. The real time includes everything before and after: waiting for an appointment, submitting documents, courier of your passport and any request for extra papers. For some destinations the decision is quick and the appointment wait is the real problem. For others it is the other way round.

Two to four weeks

Schengen off-peak (2 to 3 weeks), Japan sticker (2 to 4 weeks including slot and courier) and the UK (18 to 25 calendar days) fall here. Appointment availability and VFS courier time are what stretch these. Start at least six weeks before you fly.

Months

SlowApply very early

The USA for first-time applicants (60 to 330 days depending on city, with Mumbai the longest and Kolkata the shortest according to the article), Canada (3 to 4 months) and Australia (3 to 6 weeks) need the most planning. For the USA the interview wait is the main delay, not the decision.

What Slows Things Down

  • Peak season. The article says Schengen summer times can roughly double for some countries, and Japan's cherry blossom (March to April) and autumn (October to November) seasons raise VFS demand.
  • The clock start. For the UK, the clock starts the day after biometrics, not when you submit online.
  • Appointment waits. For Schengen and the USA the wait for a slot can be longer than the decision. Our Italy appointment tips explain how to find one.
  • Courier and VFS handling. The article suggests adding 3 to 5 working days for VFS and courier on Schengen applications.
  • Weekends and holidays. Working days exclude them, and consulates have their own holiday calendars.
  • Additional document requests. A missing paper restarts the clock in practice.

Can You Pay to Go Faster?

Sometimes. The article describes UAE express e-visas at 24 to 48 hours for around ₹8,999 in total, subject to security screening, and UK Priority at 3 to 5 working days for an extra £500 and Super Priority at 24 to 48 hours for an extra £1,000, with limited daily allocations. It says Schengen has no official express route, Japan has none, and the USA allows emergency appointments only for urgent medical, family death or business reasons.

Paying more does not guarantee approval, only faster handling. Compare the extra cost with the cost of changing your flight, and never buy non-refundable bookings until the visa is in your passport or inbox.

Work Backwards From Your Travel Date

Pick your destination, then count backwards.

  1. Fix your travel dates and note any peak-season effect on the destination.
  2. Find the realistic time from the table above, then add the longer end of any range.
  3. Add appointment and courier time for Schengen, Japan and UK applications.
  4. Add a 7 to 10 day buffer for missing documents and holidays.
  5. Subtract from your flight date to get your latest sensible application date.
  6. Book refundable travel until the visa is confirmed.

Time vs Cost: A Quick Way to Think About It

Speed often costs money, and slow routes cost flexibility. Here is a simple way to weigh them.

Fast and cheap

Visa-free entries and low-cost e-visas, such as Thailand's arrival card. Best for short notice and small budgets.

Fast but paid

Express e-visas and priority services. Worth it if your dates are fixed, but check what they cover.

Slow but cheap

Standard Schengen and Japan sticker routes. Fine if you plan early and keep bookings refundable.

Slow and uncertain

Interview-based visas with long queues. Start as soon as you decide, and do not tie money to fixed dates.

Remember that the visa fee is only part of the cost. Flights, insurance and any change fees can outweigh it. Our travel insurance guide explains cover that includes trip delay or cancellation, which can matter when a visa runs late.

Tracking Your Application and Staying Calm

Once you submit, most of the stress comes from not knowing. Save every reference number, use the official tracking page when there is one and avoid contacting the consulate before the stated processing time has passed. If your application is taking longer than the range in this guide, check for emails asking for documents, then contact the visa centre politely with your reference number.

  • Check your email and spam folder daily, since requests for more documents are easy to miss.
  • Do not submit a second application unless the portal tells you to.
  • Avoid paying third parties who promise guaranteed approvals or secret fast tracks.
  • If you hire help, read how to choose an international travel agency from India first.

Rules of Thumb for Indian Travellers

  • Never trust a single number. Add a buffer to every time you read.
  • Apply as soon as the earliest allowed date opens for destinations with long queues.
  • Keep documents consistent, since mismatches cause requests for more information.
  • Use official portals and be cautious about paying for slot-selling services.
  • Keep refundable flights until the visa arrives.
  • Check the visa-free list before you start any paperwork, because a visa-free country saves the whole process.

Final Thoughts

Visa processing time is rarely one number. It is the decision time plus the queue plus the courier plus your buffer. Compare destinations on the realistic end-to-end time, plan backwards from your flight and keep your bookings flexible. That way, a slow visa is an annoyance instead of a cancelled trip.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which visas are fastest for Indians?

According to one article, Thailand is instant on arrival, and Indonesia e-VOA and Turkey e-visa take 24 to 48 hours. UAE and Vietnam e-visas take about 3 to 5 working days. Confirm current times.

How long does a Schengen visa take from India?

The legal maximum is 15 calendar days, but realistic times are 2 to 3 weeks off-peak and 4 to 8 weeks or more from May to August, according to one article. Add time for the appointment and courier.

How long does a Japan tourist visa take?

The official checklist says about one week, and guides report 3 to 5 working days for the e-visa and 2 to 4 weeks for the sticker including slot and courier.

Why is the US visa so slow?

The article says first-time applicants may wait 60 to 330 days depending on city because of interview slots, even though the decision after the interview takes 3 to 7 business days.

Can I pay for faster visa processing?

For some destinations, yes: UAE express e-visa and UK Priority or Super Priority. Schengen and Japan have no official express route in the article. Faster handling is not guaranteed approval.

How early should I apply for a visa from India?

Work backwards from your travel date using the realistic time plus a 7 to 10 day buffer. For Schengen, the Italy guide advises starting 3 to 6 months ahead.
