Pushkar Camel Fair 2026: Dates, How to Get There and Where to Stay

October 22, 20269 min readby WanderLust Blog

The Pushkar Camel Fair is part livestock market, part pilgrimage, and part folk festival, which is why it works for photographers, culture seekers and first-time visitors alike.

Pushkar is a small holy town built around a sacred lake, and during the fair its streets fill with livestock traders, pilgrims and visitors. The main activity is concentrated in the final few days, when camel competitions and cultural performances peak.

Dates and What Happens

The fair is held around Kartik Purnima and runs for roughly a week. Official dates are set by the local administration each year, so treat the window as indicative and confirm before you travel. Expect livestock trading, camel and horse events, folk music and dance, and pilgrims taking holy dips at the lake around the full moon.

How to Get There

Pushkar is about 14 km from Ajmer and about 145 km from Jaipur. Ajmer is the nearest railway hub, with taxis connecting to Pushkar, and Jaipur is the main airport gateway. See our Pushkar travel guide for the town layout, Brahma Temple and dress code.

Where to Stay

Fair week is peak season, so reserve accommodation well in advance, ideally a month or more. Staying overnight in Pushkar gives a very different experience from a day trip, because the evening gatherings along the lake begin after day visitors leave.

Visiting Respectfully

  • Pushkar is a holy town; dress modestly and be mindful around the lake ghats.
  • Ask permission before photographing people, camel owners or pilgrims, and agree prices before camel rides.
  • Meat and alcohol are generally not sold in the town, so plan accordingly.
  • Keep valuables secure in crowds; see our scams guide.

Combining Pushkar With Other November Festivals

Many travellers pair Pushkar with Diwali earlier in the month or Dev Deepawali in Varanasi on November 24. See our Diwali 2026 guide to plan the sequence.

What the Fair Is Really Like Day by Day

The early days of the fair are mostly about livestock. Traders bring camels, horses and cattle from across Rajasthan and nearby states, and you will see animals being groomed, decorated, inspected and sold. The middle days add cultural events, music and competitions. The final days draw the biggest crowds, pilgrims and photographers, culminating around Kartik Purnima, when many devotees bathe in the sacred lake.

If you want to see trading and quieter scenes, go early in the fair period. If you want festivities and the pilgrimage atmosphere, go closer to Kartik Purnima, but expect high prices and heavy crowds.

A Simple 3-Day Pushkar Itinerary

  • Day 1: arrive, walk around the lake and ghats at sunset, and visit the main market streets.
  • Day 2: morning at the fair grounds to see the animals and traders, midday rest, and evening cultural events or a rooftop view over the lake.
  • Day 3: visit temples, take a sunrise walk or a short camel ride, and depart with a buffer for traffic.

If you can add a fourth day, visit Ajmer's historic sites or take a day trip to nearby villages.

Getting There from Jaipur, Ajmer, Delhi and Udaipur

  • From Jaipur: about 145 km by road. Hire a taxi or take a bus or train to Ajmer, then continue to Pushkar.
  • From Ajmer: about 14 km, with taxis and shared vehicles available. Roads become congested in fair week.
  • From Delhi: take a train to Ajmer or a flight to Jaipur, then continue by road.
  • From Udaipur or Jodhpur: longer road journeys, so allow a full day and start early.

Because trains and buses fill up, reserve early. See our guides to trains in India and travelling around India.

Where to Stay and What to Ask Before You Book

Accommodation ranges from budget guesthouses to heritage hotels and seasonal tent camps. Ask whether the price covers meals, how far the property is from the lake and fair, whether the room has heating (nights can be cool in November), how noisy the area is, and the cancellation policy. Tent camps can be atmospheric, but check toilet and security arrangements. Book early, ideally a month or more ahead.

How to Photograph the Fair Respectfully

  • Ask permission before taking close-up portraits, and offer to show the photo.
  • Do not pay people to pose in ways that make them uncomfortable, and avoid pushing for photos near the sacred lake.
  • Do not photograph bathers at the ghats. It is a religious activity, and cameras are often restricted.
  • Be careful around animals, especially camels in crowded areas.

Camel Rides and Animal Welfare

Camel rides are offered to visitors, and you should agree on the price and duration beforehand. Choose operators who look after their animals, with healthy-looking camels and reasonable handling. Avoid rides if an animal looks distressed. Remember that these animals are working livestock, and keep a safe distance when they are being moved or loaded.

Food, Water and Health

Pushkar is a holy town, and meat and alcohol are generally not sold there, so food is vegetarian. Try local dishes, sweets and fresh snacks from busy stalls, and drink bottled or filtered water. The dust at the fair can irritate your throat and eyes, so carry a scarf or mask, eye drops, hand sanitiser and basic medicines. Wear closed shoes in animal areas.

Respecting the Sacred Lake and Temples

Follow local rules at the ghats, including removing shoes, avoiding leather items and not taking photographs where they are prohibited. Be careful about people who offer flowers and then ask for money, a common tourist scam in some pilgrimage towns; politely decline if you do not wish to take part. Our scams guide explains how to handle these approaches.

What to Pack

  • Modest, comfortable clothes and a warm layer for cool evenings
  • Sturdy closed shoes and socks, as the fair grounds are dusty
  • Scarf, sunglasses, hat and sunscreen
  • Power bank, torch, a small first-aid kit and wet wipes
  • A day bag with a secure zip for valuables

See also our what to wear in India guide.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Arriving in fair week without accommodation.
  • Assuming meat and alcohol are available.
  • Relying on exact fair dates from unofficial sources.
  • Planning a tight return journey on the last day.
  • Behaving disrespectfully near the lake or temples.

More Questions About Visiting the Pushkar Camel Fair

Is the fair suitable for first-time visitors to India?

Yes, but it is crowded and busy. Book accommodation early, arrange transport in advance and keep expectations flexible. A guide or reputable local host can help you understand what you see.

How much time should I spend at the fair?

Two full days is a good minimum. One day for the fair grounds and animals, and another for the lake, temples and town. A third day gives you time to rest and explore nearby places.

Are there ticket requirements?

Entry to the main fair area is generally free, but specific events, camps and activities may charge. Check the official programme and ask your accommodation for current details.

What should I do about dust and crowds?

Carry a scarf or mask, sunglasses, water and hand sanitiser. Keep your bag in front of you and avoid the busiest areas during peak hours.

Can I visit as a solo traveller?

Yes. Choose well-reviewed accommodation, avoid walking alone late at night in crowded or quiet areas, and keep emergency contacts handy. Dress modestly to avoid unwanted attention.

Final Tips for Pushkar

Go with an open mind and be ready for dust, noise and crowds. Early mornings are the best time for photographs and quiet moments, while the afternoon heat is a good time to rest. Keep small change for offerings and tips, and always confirm prices before you accept a service. Check the official fair programme close to your dates, since events can shift.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Pushkar Camel Fair 2026?

It runs approximately November 17–24, 2026, climaxing on Kartik Purnima on November 24; confirm exact dates locally.

How far is Pushkar from Jaipur?

About 145 km by road.

Is Pushkar crowded during the fair?

Yes, and accommodation fills up, so book early.

Can tourists attend the fair?

Yes. The fair is open to visitors, but be respectful at religious sites and when photographing people.

Where can I buy train tickets for Pushkar?

Book to Ajmer, the nearest railway hub, and take a taxi onward.

Is alcohol available in Pushkar?

Meat and alcohol are generally not sold in the holy town.
