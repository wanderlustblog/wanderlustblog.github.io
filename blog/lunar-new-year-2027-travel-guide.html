Lunar New Year 2027 Travel Guide: Dates, Crowds and How to Plan

October 22, 20268 min readby WanderLust Blog

Lunar New Year is the biggest travel season in much of Asia, and the trip works best when you plan around crowds and closures instead of fighting them.

Lunar New Year follows the lunar calendar, so the date shifts each year. In 2027 it falls in early February, with celebrations lasting about two weeks in many places. In China it is called the Spring Festival, in Vietnam it is Tet, and it is also widely celebrated in Taiwan, Singapore, Malaysia, South Korea and Hong Kong.

What to Expect

  • Large numbers of people travelling to see family, which strains trains, flights and roads.
  • Many local shops and restaurants closed for several days, especially on the first days of the festival.
  • Festive decorations, temple fairs, flower markets, lion and dragon dances and fireworks in many cities.

Choosing Where to Go

  • Big cities: Singapore, Hong Kong and Kuala Lumpur put on organised events and keep many tourist services open.
  • Vietnam: a very family-focused holiday, with some businesses closed and transport busy.
  • China: the Spring Festival travel rush is huge, so book early and expect crowds.

Planning Tips

  • Book flights, trains and hotels as early as possible.
  • Check which attractions and restaurants will be open.
  • Carry cash and keep a flexible schedule.
  • Learn basic etiquette, such as giving and receiving with both hands.

How Lunar New Year Works for Travellers

Lunar New Year is the start of the new year in the lunar calendar. Celebrations typically begin on New Year's Eve, with a reunion dinner, and continue for about fifteen days, ending with the Lantern Festival in many Chinese communities. Dates and customs vary between countries. For travellers the most important effect is timing: the first few days are family-focused, and many businesses close, while the last days often have public events.

Country-by-Country Planning Notes

China

The Spring Festival travel rush is huge, so trains, flights and roads are very busy around the holiday. Book transport as early as possible, avoid travelling on the busiest days, and expect many shops to close for several days. Major cities still have festive decorations, temple fairs and cultural events.

Vietnam (Tet)

Tet is the most important holiday of the year. Many local restaurants and shops close, and transport is crowded as people travel home. Cities are decorated with flowers and lights, and there are fireworks in some places. Plan accommodation and key meals ahead.

Singapore, Malaysia and Hong Kong

These places keep many tourist services running and host organised events, markets and parades, which makes them popular choices for first-time visitors. Expect higher prices and crowded attractions on the main days.

Taiwan and South Korea

Taiwan has temple events and lantern displays, while South Korea celebrates Seollal, a family holiday where many shops close and transport is busy. Check local calendars for official public holidays.

A Sample 7-Day Plan: Singapore and Kuala Lumpur

  • Days 1 to 3, Singapore: explore Chinatown's decorations, visit museums and food centres, and watch evening events.
  • Day 4: travel to Kuala Lumpur by flight or bus, allowing extra time for holiday queues.
  • Days 5 to 6, Kuala Lumpur: temples, markets and local food, with day trips if you have energy.
  • Day 7: relax and depart with a generous buffer.

A Booking Timeline

  1. 4 to 6 months before: choose your destination and fix dates. Book long-haul flights and, in China, rail or flights early.
  2. 2 to 3 months before: book hotels, with cancellation options if possible.
  3. 1 month before: check visas, insurance and event schedules, and reserve restaurants for key evenings.
  4. 1 week before: confirm closures, download maps and translation apps and prepare cash.

Etiquette and Customs

  • Red is a lucky colour. Wearing red is welcome, but avoid all-black or all-white outfits at celebrations.
  • Red envelopes are given to children and unmarried relatives, so tourists do not usually need to give them unless invited into a family home.
  • Give and receive gifts and cards with both hands.
  • Do not wash hair or sweep floors on the first day in some traditions, and avoid discussing unlucky topics.
  • Be quiet and respectful at temples, and ask permission before photographing worshippers.

Food and Dining Tips

Look for festive foods such as dumplings, fish, sticky rice cakes, oranges and sweet treats, each with symbolic meaning. Many restaurants require reservations or set menus during the holiday, and some close entirely. Make a list of backup places, and keep snacks in your bag. If you have dietary restrictions, learn how to say them in the local language or use a translation card.

Money, Safety and Connectivity

Carry some cash for small vendors and tips, but bring cards for hotels and larger shops. Watch for inflated prices and unlicensed taxis during busy nights. Crowds at fireworks and temple events can be dense, so keep bags in front and agree on meeting points. Get a local eSIM or SIM, and download offline maps and tickets, since networks can be congested.

Common Mistakes

  • Booking too late and finding no flights, trains or rooms.
  • Assuming everything will be open, especially in smaller cities and Vietnam.
  • Planning tight itineraries across multiple cities during the travel rush.
  • Not checking public holiday dates for your specific destination.
  • Ignoring local customs, such as speaking loudly in temples or taking photos without permission.

Final Checklist

  • Confirm the festival date and local public holiday dates for 2027.
  • Book transport and hotels early, with flexible options where possible.
  • Reserve restaurants for key evenings and keep backup meal options.
  • Pack layers, since February weather varies widely across Asia.
  • Check visa rules and insurance.

More Questions About Travelling During Lunar New Year

Is it a bad time to travel in Asia?

Not necessarily, but it is busy. With good planning, you can enjoy festive decorations, events and food. Avoid peak travel days and keep your itinerary flexible.

How long do closures last?

It varies. In many places, shops close for the first few days, while major tourist businesses reopen sooner. Check opening hours in your destination.

Should I exchange money before I go?

Carry a small amount of local currency for arrival and small purchases, and use cards or ATMs for the rest. Banks and exchange counters may have reduced hours during the holiday.

Do I need to book restaurants?

For popular places and reunion-style dinners, yes. Many restaurants offer set menus on the main evenings and sell out early. Have a backup plan and keep snacks.

What is the best destination for first-timers?

Singapore, Hong Kong and Kuala Lumpur are good choices, since they have clear events, strong tourism services and easy transport. They are also more likely to have shops and restaurants open.

What gifts are appropriate for hosts?

Fruit, tea, high-quality sweets or festive snacks are common choices. Avoid clocks, white flowers and sets of four in some Chinese traditions, since they carry unlucky associations.

Final Tips for a Good Festival Trip

The most useful habit during Lunar New Year travel is flexibility. Build one empty day into your plan, keep refundable bookings where possible and accept that some places will be closed. Often the best moments come from unplanned evenings, such as a temple fair, a family meal or a street procession.

Learn a few greetings, such as 'Happy New Year' in the local language, and show interest in local customs. A friendly attitude and some preparation make the holiday far more rewarding, and locals are generally happy to explain traditions to curious visitors.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Lunar New Year 2027?

It falls on February 6, 2027 by most calendars; confirm closer to the date.

Which countries celebrate Lunar New Year?

China, Vietnam, Taiwan, Singapore, Malaysia, South Korea and many other communities.

Is it a good time to travel in Asia?

Yes, if you plan for crowds and closures and book early.

Do shops close during Lunar New Year?

Many do for several days, especially at the start.

When should I book flights and trains?

As early as possible, since this is a peak travel period.

Where is best for first-timers?

Singapore, Hong Kong or Kuala Lumpur keep many services running and host organised events.
