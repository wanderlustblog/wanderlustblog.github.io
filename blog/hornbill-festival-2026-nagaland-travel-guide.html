Hornbill Festival 2026: Dates, Permits and How to Plan Your Trip to Nagaland

October 22, 20269 min readby WanderLust Blog

Often called the Festival of Festivals, Hornbill is where Nagaland's tribes gather in one place to share dance, music, food and traditions, a rare chance to see the state's cultural range in a few days.

Quick TakeDetails
DatesTypically December 1–10 (2026 start: December 1)
VenueKisama Heritage Village, about 12 km from Kohima
Who attendsNagaland's major tribes
Plan aheadPermits, transport and accommodation

Named after the hornbill, a bird revered in Naga folklore, the festival showcases traditional arts, dances, folk songs and games. Each tribe has its own character and festivals, and Hornbill brings them together in replica village settings.

What to Expect

  • Traditional dances and music in tribal dress.
  • Replica tribal houses (morungs) with wood carvings.
  • Naga food, crafts and local markets.
  • Evening music events and the Kohima night market.

Getting There

Kisama Heritage Village is about 12 km from Kohima, the state capital. Dimapur is the main gateway for flights and trains, with road transfers onward to Kohima. Allow extra time for hill roads and traffic around festival days.

Permits and Entry

Nagaland has permit requirements for visitors. Check the current rules for your nationality, including any Inner Line Permit requirements for Indian visitors and the relevant permits for foreign nationals, well before you travel, since rules can change.

Where to Stay and When to Book

Kohima's rooms fill up early for festival week. Book accommodation and transport in advance, and consider a homestay for a more local experience.

Respectful Travel

  • Ask before photographing performers and participants.
  • Most Nagas are Christian; dress modestly and keep in mind that Sundays are quiet in many areas.
  • Be open to local food, but check what's in dishes if you have dietary needs.

A Simple 6-Day Plan for the Hornbill Festival

Nagaland has hill roads and limited transport, so a calm plan works better than a packed one. Here is a flexible outline.

  • Day 1: arrive in Dimapur, take a road transfer to Kohima and settle in. Keep the evening free in case of delays.
  • Day 2: visit the festival at Kisama Heritage Village. Arrive early to see the morning performances and explore the tribal houses.
  • Day 3: spend more time at the festival, focusing on food, crafts and cultural events, and try the evening activities in Kohima.
  • Day 4: visit the Kohima War Cemetery and local museums, and explore the town and its markets.
  • Day 5: day trip to a nearby Naga village if you can arrange a reliable guide and transport.
  • Day 6: return to Dimapur with buffer time before onward flights or trains.

Permits: A Step-by-Step Way to Handle Them

  1. Identify your category. Rules differ for Indian nationals and foreign nationals.
  2. Check official sources. Use the Nagaland government and tourism department websites, or ask an experienced local operator, for the current permit process.
  3. Apply early. Do not leave permit applications for the last week. Processing times and arrival procedures can change.
  4. Carry copies. Keep printed and digital copies of your permit and ID with you, since checks can occur.

Because rules can change, do not rely on old blog posts or forum answers. Confirm directly before booking non-refundable travel.

Getting Around and Transport Tips

Roads in the hills can be slow, and weather and traffic around festival days add delays. Many visitors arrange a car with a driver for transfers. Shared taxis are an option for budget travellers, but schedules can be irregular. Avoid tight onward connections on the same day you leave Kohima. Keep cash for local transport, and download offline maps before you leave Dimapur.

Where to Stay

Options include hotels in Kohima, guesthouses and homestays near the festival and in nearby villages. Homestays give a closer look at daily life and often include home-cooked meals. Ask about hot water, heating (December nights are cold), distance to Kisama and whether transport can be arranged. Book well ahead, since demand is high during festival week.

Culture, Etiquette and Respectful Photography

  • The performers are community members, not actors. Ask before taking close-up portraits and respect refusals.
  • Avoid touching headdresses, weapons or traditional items without permission.
  • Dress modestly and warmly, and avoid clothing with offensive slogans.
  • Be aware that most Nagas are Christian, and Sundays are generally quiet with church services.
  • Support local artisans by buying directly from craftspeople and asking about materials.

Food in Nagaland

Naga cuisine is rich in smoked meats, fermented ingredients, bamboo shoot, boiled vegetables and chilli. Food is often simple and strongly flavoured. If you are vegetarian or have allergies, explain clearly, since many dishes include meat or fish. Try local rice dishes, tea and seasonal vegetables, and ask your host about specialities. Carry bottled water and basic stomach medicines.

Weather and What to Pack

December in the hills of Nagaland is dry but cold, particularly in the mornings and evenings. Pack layers, a warm jacket, comfortable closed shoes, a hat, gloves, a rain layer in case of showers, sunscreen, a power bank and any personal medication. Carry a small daypack, tissues and hand sanitiser, and keep valuables secure in crowds.

Costs: How to Plan Them

Use a budget list instead of guessing. Include flights or train tickets to Dimapur, the road transfer to Kohima, accommodation, permit-related costs, festival entry fees, food, local transport, souvenirs and a buffer of 10 to 15 percent. Compare at least three accommodation options and ask what is included. Festival-week prices are higher than normal, so check whether travelling just before or after the main days suits you.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving permits and bookings for the last moment.
  • Planning tight same-day connections out of Kohima.
  • Treating performers as photo props rather than people.
  • Underestimating the cold on December evenings.
  • Trying to cover too much of Northeast India in one short trip.

More Questions About Attending the Hornbill Festival

How many days do I need for the festival?

Two full days at the festival give you time to see the performances and exhibits, and a third day in Kohima adds museums and local life. Allow extra days for travel to and from Dimapur.

Is the Hornbill Festival suitable for families?

Yes, many families attend, but it is crowded and the weather is cold in the mornings and evenings. Bring layers, snacks and a plan for rest breaks.

Can I buy handicrafts and souvenirs there?

Yes. Stalls sell textiles, bamboo items, jewellery and woodwork. Buy directly from makers when you can, ask about the materials and agree prices politely.

What is the best time of day to visit?

Mornings are usually calmer for exploring exhibits, while afternoons bring more performances and crowds. Evenings in Kohima offer food and music, but the cold arrives early.

How do I deal with altitude and cold?

Kohima is in the hills, with cold winter evenings. Dress in layers, drink water and avoid exhausting yourself on the first day, since long road transfers can be tiring.

Is it safe to travel in Nagaland as a visitor?

Many visitors travel without problems, but conditions and rules can change. Check current official travel advice, follow local guidance and use reputable transport and accommodation.

Final Tips for a Smooth Trip

Treat the Hornbill Festival as a cultural visit rather than a show. Take time to talk to artisans, ask about the meaning of costumes and songs, and buy a few well-made items from the people who produced them. These small interactions are often what visitors remember most.

Keep paper copies of permits, bookings and emergency contacts, since mobile signal can be unreliable in hill areas. Carry cash in small notes, refill your water bottle when you can and check road conditions each morning before you travel between towns.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Hornbill Festival 2026?

It starts on December 1 and typically runs through December 10.

Where is the Hornbill Festival held?

At Kisama Heritage Village, about 12 km from Kohima, Nagaland.

How do I get to Kohima?

Dimapur is the main gateway for flights and trains, followed by a road transfer to Kohima.

Do I need a permit to visit Nagaland?

Permit rules apply and can change, so check current requirements for your nationality before you go.

When should I book accommodation?

As early as possible; rooms around Kohima fill up for festival week.

Is the festival suitable for first-time visitors to Northeast India?

Yes, with advance planning for permits, transport and weather.
