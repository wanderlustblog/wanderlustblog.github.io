International Travel Insurance for Indians: What to Check Before You Buy

November 12, 202613 min readby WanderLust Blog

Travel insurance is the cheapest part of a trip until something goes wrong, and then the details of the policy matter more than the price.

For many Indian travellers, travel insurance is something you buy because a visa form asks for it. But a good policy does much more than satisfy a requirement. It can pay for emergency medical treatment abroad, cover a cancelled trip, compensate for lost baggage and arrange evacuation if you are seriously ill or injured. A poor policy, or one that does not match your trip, may leave you facing a huge bill. This guide explains what international travel insurance covers, which features matter most, what is often excluded, how to compare policies and how to make a claim. It is general information, not a recommendation of any insurer, and you should read the policy wording before you buy.

Why Insurance Matters More Abroad

Medical costs in some destinations, such as the United States, Canada, Australia and parts of Europe, can be very high compared with India. A hospital stay or an air ambulance can cost far more than a typical holiday budget. Even in cheaper destinations, an accident, an illness or a cancelled flight can disrupt your trip and your finances. Insurance does not prevent these problems, but it limits the damage and gives you access to emergency assistance.

The Main Types of Cover

  • Emergency medical expenses: hospital treatment, doctor visits and medicines abroad. This is the most important component.
  • Emergency evacuation and repatriation: transport to a suitable hospital or back to India in a serious case.
  • Trip cancellation and interruption: reimbursement of non-refundable costs if you must cancel or cut short a trip for a covered reason, such as serious illness.
  • Baggage loss and delay: compensation for lost, stolen or delayed baggage, usually with limits per item.
  • Passport loss: help with the cost of replacing documents abroad.
  • Flight delay and missed connection: cash for expenses when delays exceed a set time.
  • Personal liability: cover if you accidentally injure someone or damage property.
  • Adventure sports cover: an add-on for activities such as scuba diving, skiing or trekking, which are often excluded from basic policies.

Schengen and Other Visa Requirements

Many countries require proof of travel insurance for a visa application. For the Schengen area, applicants are generally required to have medical insurance with a minimum coverage of EUR 30,000, valid in all Schengen countries and for the entire stay, including emergency medical treatment and repatriation. Check the current requirements on the official visa website for the country you apply to, and make sure your policy certificate shows the required cover, dates and territory. A policy that is cheap but does not meet the embassy's rules can lead to delays or refusals.

How Much Medical Cover Do You Need?

There is no single answer, since it depends on the destination, your age, your health and the length of the trip. As a rough guide, higher limits are advisable for expensive medical systems such as the United States and Canada, and for older travellers. Instead of focusing only on the headline limit, check whether it is per person, per trip or per claim, whether there are sub-limits for rooms, ICU or specific treatments and whether the insurer pays hospitals directly through a cashless network. Ask the insurer or a reputable agent to explain the limits in plain language.

Exclusions: The Part Most People Skip

  • Pre-existing conditions: many policies exclude or limit cover for conditions you already have, unless you declare them and pay extra. Always declare accurately. A false declaration can lead to rejection of a claim.
  • Adventure and high-risk activities: trekking at altitude, scuba diving, skiing, bungee jumping and motorbike riding are often excluded or limited. If your trip includes them, buy suitable cover. For example, a high-altitude trek such as the one in our Everest Base Camp guide needs explicit cover for the altitude and for helicopter evacuation.
  • Alcohol and drugs: claims related to intoxication may be rejected.
  • Unattended baggage: theft from an unattended bag or a parked car may not be covered.
  • Travel warnings and known events: claims related to places or events that were already subject to warnings when you bought the policy may be refused.
  • Pregnancy and childbirth: usually restricted. Check the terms if relevant.
  • Mental health and routine care: often excluded or limited.

Comparing Policies Properly

  1. Match the policy to the trip. Check the destination, dates, activities and number of travellers.
  2. Compare coverage, not only price. A cheaper policy with a low limit and many exclusions is not a real saving.
  3. Check the excess or deductible, the amount you pay first on each claim.
  4. Check the cashless hospital network, particularly in the countries you plan to visit.
  5. Read the claims process, including the time limit for reporting, documents needed and the insurer's emergency helpline.
  6. Check reviews of claims experience, from independent sources, not only the insurer's website.
  7. Read the policy wording, not just the brochure. The policy document is what counts.

When to Buy

Buy soon after booking your flights and non-refundable trip costs, because trip cancellation cover usually applies only from the date you buy the policy. Buying on the day of departure may leave you without cover for cancellations that occur earlier. Some policies also have a waiting period for certain benefits. If you travel often, an annual multi-trip policy may be cost-effective, but check the maximum days per trip.

Annual Multi-Trip vs Single-Trip Policies

A single-trip policy covers one journey and is simple and often cheaper for occasional travellers. An annual multi-trip policy covers all trips within a year, up to a limit on the length of each trip, and can save money and effort for frequent travellers. Compare the total cost over the year, the per-trip limit and the destinations covered. Some multi-trip policies exclude certain regions such as the United States and Canada, or charge more for them.

What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

  1. In an emergency, seek medical care first. Do not delay treatment because of insurance paperwork.
  2. Call the insurer's 24-hour assistance line as soon as possible, and follow their instructions on hospitals and pre-authorisation.
  3. Keep every document: medical reports, bills, receipts, boarding passes, police reports for theft and written confirmations from airlines for delays.
  4. Report theft to the local police and get a written report, which is usually required for a baggage claim.
  5. Submit the claim within the time limit, with complete and accurate documents.
  6. Follow up in writing, and keep copies of all communication.

Insurance and Your Trip Type

Family trips and seniors

Check age limits and extra premiums, and look at medical limits, pre-existing conditions and emergency evacuation. If a package includes insurance, check what it covers; see our guide on international tour package vs DIY trip.

Adventure and festival trips

Crowded festivals carry risks of pickpocketing and minor injuries, while adventure trips carry higher medical risks. Check baggage and theft limits for festivals such as Mardi Gras and medical limits for adventure activities.

Winter and cold-weather trips

Check cover for winter sports and for trip interruption caused by severe weather, for example on trips like Quebec Winter Carnival or Up Helly Aa in Shetland.

Agency-Provided Insurance: Ask Questions

Some agencies bundle insurance into a package. Ask for the policy document, the insurer's name, the coverage limits and exclusions, and whether you can upgrade. Make sure the policy meets any visa requirement. Our travel agency checklist includes questions about insurance.

More Questions Travellers Ask

Is travel insurance mandatory for all international trips from India?

Not for every country, but it is required for some visas, such as Schengen, and strongly recommended for all international trips.

Does my credit card or health insurance cover me abroad?

Some do, to a limited extent. Check the terms, the limits and whether they cover emergency evacuation, and consider a dedicated travel policy.

Does insurance cover a visa rejection?

Usually not, unless the policy explicitly includes it. Read the terms.

Can I buy insurance after I have left India?

Rarely, and it may have limitations. Buy before you travel.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Choosing the cheapest policy without reading the exclusions.
  • Not declaring a pre-existing condition.
  • Buying a policy with dates that do not match your trip.
  • Forgetting adventure activities, which may not be covered.
  • Not saving the insurer's emergency number offline.

Final Checklist

  • Policy matches destination, dates, activities and visa requirements.
  • Medical limit, evacuation, cancellation and baggage cover checked.
  • Exclusions and pre-existing condition rules read and understood.
  • Policy copy, emergency number and claim steps saved offline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is travel insurance compulsory for Schengen visas?

Schengen visa applicants are generally required to have medical insurance of at least EUR 30,000 valid across the area. Check the current official requirements.

What does travel insurance usually cover?

Emergency medical costs, evacuation, trip cancellation, baggage loss, flight delays and sometimes passport loss.

Are adventure sports covered?

Often not by default. You may need an add-on for activities such as diving, skiing or high-altitude trekking.

Should I declare pre-existing conditions?

Yes. Hiding them can lead to claim rejection.

When should I buy travel insurance?

Soon after booking non-refundable costs, so that cancellation cover applies.

What should I do in a medical emergency abroad?

Get medical help first, then call the insurer's 24-hour helpline and keep all documents.
