US Visa Interview Dress Code: What to Actually Wear

Updated August 15, 2026  ·  8 min read  ·  WanderLust

We spent way too long the night before our own US visa interview debating between a blazer and a plain shirt, convinced the wrong outfit could sink the whole thing. It doesn't. But it's also not nothing — here's what actually matters and what's just anxiety talking.

Key takeaways

  • There is no official dress code for a US visa interview.
  • Business casual, clean and comfortable, is the safest choice.
  • Traditional clothing is fine as long as your face is clearly visible.
  • Your documents and answers matter far more than your outfit.

The Short Answer: There Is No Official Dress Code

The US Department of State does not publish or enforce a dress code for visa interviews. Nothing in your DS-160 or appointment materials specifies an outfit. That said, "no official code" doesn't mean "wear whatever" — officers process well over a hundred applicants a day and form an impression fast, so how you present yourself still plays a small supporting role.

What Actually Works: Business Casual

  • For most applicants: a collared shirt with dress pants or a blouse with dress pants or a knee-length skirt covers nearly every situation
  • Avoid the extremes: a full suit can read as overdone for a 2-5 minute interview, while gym wear or flip-flops read as careless
  • Comfort matters: you may be waiting 2-3 hours at the consulate before your name is called, so prioritize something you can sit in comfortably

Outfit Ideas by Applicant

"Business casual" is vague when you're standing in front of a wardrobe at midnight, so here is what it looks like in practice. These are suggestions, not rules — the goal is to look neat and feel like yourself.

ApplicantEasy, safe outfit
MenIroned collared shirt or plain polo, chinos or dark trousers, clean closed shoes
WomenBlouse or smart kurta/top, trousers or a knee-length skirt, flat or low-heel shoes
Students and young adultsPlain shirt or sweater, dark jeans without rips are usually fine, clean sneakers or loafers
Children and teensClean, comfortable everyday clothes; nothing with loud slogans or that restricts movement
Older applicantsWhatever is neat and comfortable for a long wait, with easy-to-remove shoes and layers

Colors and fit

Solid, muted colors — navy, grey, white, beige, soft blue, olive — look tidy and never distract. You don't need to avoid color entirely, but busy patterns and neon shades draw attention for the wrong reasons. Fit matters more than price: a simple shirt that fits well and is properly ironed looks better than an expensive outfit that's too tight or too loose. Try everything on a day or two before, since you don't want to discover a missing button or a stiff new pair of shoes on the morning itself.

Dress for the weather and the wait

Consulates can mean standing in a queue outside before you even get in, so check the forecast. Bring a light layer if the waiting area is air-conditioned, and choose breathable fabrics if it's hot. If you're traveling to another city for the appointment, pick something that won't look crumpled after hours on the road — plain cotton blends and wrinkle-resistant fabrics are practical choices.

Can You Wear Traditional or Cultural Clothing?

Yes. Consular officers are accustomed to applicants from every part of the world, and traditional attire is entirely acceptable. The one hard rule: your face needs to be clearly visible for identity verification, so anything that covers it should be adjusted for the interview itself.

The broader interview process, including identity checks like this, is outlined in the Wikipedia entry on Form DS-160, the application form tied to your in-person interview appointment.

Grooming and Accessories

Grooming is the part most people overlook. Neat hair, clean nails, and a tidy beard or a clean shave all contribute to the same "put-together" impression as your clothes, and none of it costs anything. Keep fragrance light, since you'll be in a crowded waiting room. For jewelry and watches, simple is best — a plain watch, small earrings, or a wedding ring are all fine. Large, flashy pieces aren't forbidden, but they can slow you down at security screening and don't help your case.

If you wear glasses, wear them — you want to look like your passport photo and be able to read forms comfortably. Heavy makeup isn't necessary; natural and tidy is enough. And remember that your appearance should still resemble the photo you submitted with your application, so major last-minute changes to your look are best avoided.

What to Skip

  • Graphic t-shirts, ripped jeans, or anything overly casual
  • Sunglasses or anything obscuring your face
  • Overly flashy accessories or bright, distracting patterns — plain and neat photographs better in your own memory too, since first impressions form fast
  • Brand-new shoes you haven't broken in, which can turn a long wait into a painful one
  • Clothing with political slogans, offensive text, or military-style items that could invite unnecessary questions

What to Carry (and What Not to Bring)

Your outfit is only half of interview-day preparation. Most consulates have strict security rules, and the details differ by location, so read your appointment confirmation carefully before you pack. In general, bring only what you need: your passport, appointment confirmation, DS-160 confirmation page, a passport-style photo if requested, and any supporting documents for your case, kept neat in a simple folder.

Many consulates restrict large bags, and some do not allow electronic devices such as phones or laptops inside, with nowhere safe to leave them. Rules like this vary, so check the instructions for your specific consulate or embassy rather than assuming. A small, plain bag or folder is the safest option, and it's a good reason to wear clothes with usable pockets.

Tip: Lay out your outfit and documents the night before. It removes last-minute stress, and you can spend your energy practicing your answers instead.

Does the Visa Type Change What to Wear?

Not in any official way. Whether you're applying for a tourist (B-1/B-2), student (F-1), or work visa, the same business-casual guidance applies. Student applicants sometimes lean a little smarter than their everyday campus clothes, and applicants who work in professional jobs often just wear a version of what they'd wear to the office. The officer is assessing your documents and your answers, not your wardrobe, so pick something that matches how you'd normally present yourself on a good day.

Interview-Day Checklist

  1. Outfit ironed, clean, and comfortable to sit in for several hours
  2. Passport, appointment letter, and DS-160 confirmation in one folder
  3. Supporting documents organized in the order you might need them
  4. Small, plain bag, with nothing the consulate prohibits
  5. Water and a light snack only if your consulate permits them
  6. Enough time to arrive early, since queues and security checks can take a while
  7. A calm mindset: answer clearly, honestly, and briefly

Does It Actually Affect Approval?

No single outfit gets anyone approved or denied — the decision rests on your documents, your answers, and your case. But in an interview that lasts 2 to 5 minutes, appearance is one small part of the overall impression you make in that short window, so it's worth the low effort of dressing neatly rather than treating it as irrelevant.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there an official dress code for a US visa interview?

No. The US Department of State has no official dress code for visa interviews. Business casual is generally recommended as a safe, professional default.

Can I wear traditional or cultural clothing to a US visa interview?

Yes. Traditional attire is acceptable as long as your face is clearly visible, which matters for identity verification.

What should I avoid wearing to a US visa interview?

Avoid overly casual items like flip-flops, gym wear, or graphic t-shirts, and avoid anything that obscures your face.

Does what I wear affect my visa approval?

Your outfit alone does not determine approval, but appearance contributes to the officer's first impression during a very short interview, so dressing neatly is worth the effort.

Should I wear a suit to a US visa interview?

A full suit isn't necessary. Smart business casual is enough for a short interview, though a suit won't count against you if it's what you feel most confident in.

What should children and teenagers wear to a US visa interview?

Neat, clean, comfortable clothes are fine. Children don't need formal wear; avoid costumes, loud slogans, or anything that restricts movement during a long wait.

Can I wear jewelry or a watch to the consulate?

Simple jewelry is fine. Keep it minimal, since you may need to pass through security screening, and avoid large or flashy pieces.

Does the type of US visa change what I should wear?

The guidance is the same across visa types. Student and work visa applicants may lean slightly smarter, but there is no category-specific dress code.

Bottom Line

Business casual, comfortable enough to sit in for a few hours, face clearly visible — that covers it. Spend your prep energy on your documents and answers instead.
