Visa on Arrival Countries: What to Know Before You Land

Updated August 15, 2026  ·  6 min read  ·  WanderLust

There's a particular kind of relief in landing somewhere and knowing you don't have to hunt down an embassy weeks in advance. Visa on arrival makes that possible — but it's also the visa type people misunderstand the most, usually by assuming it's guaranteed or that it works the same everywhere. It doesn't.

What Visa on Arrival Actually Means

A visa on arrival is exactly what it sounds like: instead of applying in advance through an embassy or online e-visa system, you apply and receive your visa at the airport or land border itself, right when you enter the country. No pre-approval email, no consulate visit — just a counter, a form, and usually a fee paid on the spot.

The Wikipedia overview on electronic and arrival-based visas is a useful reference point here, since it lays out how visa-on-arrival systems sit between full visa-free entry and requiring a traditional embassy application.

Which Countries Commonly Offer It

Availability depends heavily on your passport, but these destinations are known for offering visa on arrival to a wide range of nationalities:

  • Thailand — a long-standing, well-organized visa on arrival system at major airports
  • UAE — visa on arrival for many nationalities, sometimes tied to airline or hotel bookings
  • Indonesia — pay-on-entry visa on arrival at major international airports
  • Egypt — visa on arrival available for many nationalities, though an e-visa is often faster
  • Jordan — visa on arrival is common, and often bundled through the Jordan Pass for tourists

This list shifts constantly as countries update policy, so treat it as a starting point, not a guarantee for your specific passport.

What You'll Actually Need at the Counter

  • A passport valid for at least 6 months beyond your entry date
  • A passport-sized photo, sometimes required on the spot
  • Proof of onward or return travel
  • Cash or card for the visa fee — some countries only accept local currency

Missing any one of these can mean standing in a slow-moving line only to be turned back to a separate desk, or in rare cases, denied entry entirely.

Where It Can Go Wrong

Visa on arrival isn't a legal guarantee of entry — it's a convenience, not a right. Immigration officers still have discretion, and being visa-on-arrival eligible on paper doesn't override a border agent's judgment call if something about your documents or trip purpose looks off. It's also worth knowing that "on arrival" doesn't mean instant — some airports process it in minutes, others can mean a 45-minute line during peak hours.

What is a visa on arrival?

A visa on arrival is a visa you apply for and receive at the airport or border itself, rather than in advance through an embassy or online e-visa system.

Which countries offer visa on arrival?

Countries like Thailand, the UAE, Indonesia, Egypt, and Jordan commonly offer visa on arrival for many nationalities, though eligibility always depends on your specific passport.

What do I need for a visa on arrival?

Typically a valid passport with enough remaining validity, a passport photo, proof of onward travel, and cash or card for the visa fee.

Can a visa on arrival be denied at the airport?

Yes. Immigration officers can deny entry even if your nationality is generally eligible, especially if documents are missing or your trip purpose looks unclear.

Bottom Line

Visa on arrival is genuinely convenient when it works, but it rewards preparation more than people expect. Bring every document listed above even if you've heard it's "usually not checked" — usually isn't always.
