Spouse Visa Requirements: What Couples Actually Need

Updated August 15, 2026  ·  6 min read  ·  WanderLust

A spouse visa asks for something no other visa category does: proof that your relationship is real. Not just that you're legally married, but that the marriage reflects an actual, ongoing life together. That's why this category is slower and more document-heavy than almost any other — the paperwork isn't just about you, it's about proving a relationship on paper.

The Core Documents

  • Marriage certificate — the legal foundation of the application, often needing to be officially translated and authenticated
  • Proof of ongoing relationship — evidence the relationship is genuine and current, not just a document from your wedding day
  • Proof of sponsoring partner's income — showing the couple can be financially supported without relying on public assistance
  • Police background check — required by most countries for the applying spouse
  • Health insurance or medical exam — required in some countries as part of the standard immigration process

What Actually Counts as "Proof of Relationship"

This is the part that surprises people. Immigration offices generally want to see evidence spanning time, not a single snapshot: joint bank accounts, a shared lease or mortgage, photos together across different periods (not just the wedding), message history, and sometimes signed statements from friends or family who can vouch for the relationship. The goal is showing a pattern, not a moment — a marriage certificate alone rarely satisfies this requirement on its own.

The Income Requirement

Most countries require the sponsoring partner — the one already living in or a citizen of the destination country — to meet a minimum income threshold. This exists specifically to demonstrate the couple won't need public financial support. The exact figure varies significantly by country, and falling short of it by even a small margin can delay or derail an otherwise strong application.

Why This Category Takes So Long

Spouse visas often take 3 to 12 months, among the slowest of any visa category. The Wikipedia overview of family reunification touches on why this process is generally treated with more scrutiny than other visa types — it's a pathway that can lead to long-term residency, so the verification process reflects that higher stake.

What Commonly Causes Delays

The most frequent issues are thin relationship evidence (not enough documentation spanning time), an income that falls just short of the threshold, or a marriage certificate that hasn't been properly translated or authenticated for the destination country's requirements. Building a thorough, dated paper trail well before applying is the single biggest thing couples can do to speed this up.

What documents are needed for a spouse visa?

Most spouse visas require a marriage certificate, proof of an ongoing genuine relationship, proof of the sponsoring partner's income, and often a police background check.

What counts as proof of relationship for a spouse visa?

Common evidence includes joint bank accounts, shared leases or mortgages, photos together over time, message history, and statements from friends or family confirming the relationship.

Is there an income requirement for a spouse visa?

Yes, most countries require the sponsoring partner to meet a minimum income threshold to show they can financially support the couple, and this figure varies significantly by country.

How long does a spouse visa take to process?

Spouse visas are among the slower categories, often taking 3 to 12 months depending on the country and how thorough the relationship evidence is.

Bottom Line

Start building your evidence trail long before you apply — this is one visa category where more documentation genuinely helps, not just checks a box.
