Multiple Entry Visa: What It Is and When You Need One

Updated August 15, 2026  ·  5 min read  ·  WanderLust

We learned this one the hard way on a trip through Southeast Asia — hopped over the border for a two-day side trip, came back, and found out our visa had quietly become useless the moment we'd left. A single entry visa doesn't care that you meant to come back. It just checks whether you already used your one shot.

The Actual Difference

A single entry visa lets you enter a country exactly once during its validity period. The moment you exit — even briefly, even for a quick land-border hop — that visa is done, regardless of how much time was left on it. A multiple entry visa, by contrast, lets you enter and leave as many times as you want within the visa's validity window, without needing a new application each time.

The general distinction is described in Wikipedia's travel visa overview, which notes that entry frequency is one of the core variables countries use to categorize visa types, alongside duration and purpose.

Who Actually Needs Multiple Entry

  • Travelers planning day trips or short hops to a neighboring country during a longer stay
  • Business travelers making repeated visits over several months
  • Anyone doing a regional trip that crosses the same border more than once, like flying in and out of one country while exploring nearby ones by land
  • People visiting family or property who expect to return multiple times within a year

If your itinerary only involves entering once and leaving once at the end, a single entry visa is usually fine and often cheaper. The mistake is assuming a route is single-entry when it actually involves a hidden border crossing you hadn't accounted for.

Does It Cost More?

Generally yes. Multiple entry visas typically carry a higher fee than single entry, since they grant more flexibility over a longer window. The price gap varies a lot by country — sometimes it's a small premium, sometimes it's double the single entry cost. If you're not certain you'll cross the border more than once, it's worth weighing the extra cost against the risk of getting caught out like we did.

Can You Switch After the Fact?

Generally, no. Most countries don't let you upgrade a single entry visa to multiple entry once it's been issued. If you realize partway through your trip that you'll need to cross back and forth, you're usually looking at a fresh application rather than a simple amendment — which is exactly why it's worth thinking through your full route before applying, not after.

What is a multiple entry visa?

A multiple entry visa allows you to enter and leave a country several times within the visa's validity period, unlike a single entry visa which becomes invalid the moment you exit.

Who needs a multiple entry visa?

Travelers who plan to cross a border more than once during their trip, such as day trips to a neighboring country or business travelers with repeated visits, typically need a multiple entry visa.

Does a multiple entry visa cost more?

Yes, multiple entry visas are usually more expensive than single entry visas, though the price difference varies significantly by country.

Can I upgrade a single entry visa to multiple entry?

Generally no. You typically need to apply for a multiple entry visa from the start, since most countries don't allow converting an issued single entry visa afterward.

Bottom Line

Map out your full route before you apply — not just your first entry point. If there's any chance you'll cross a border and come back, the extra cost of multiple entry is almost always cheaper than the headache of a second application mid-trip.
