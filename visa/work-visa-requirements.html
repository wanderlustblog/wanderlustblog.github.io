Work Visa Requirements: What Employers & Applicants Need

Updated August 15, 2026  ·  6 min read  ·  WanderLust

A work visa is the one category where you genuinely can't do it alone. Unlike a tourist visa or even a student visa, most work visas require your future employer to be an active participant in the paperwork — which means the process often stalls not because of you, but because of how quickly HR moves.

The Core Requirement: A Confirmed Job Offer

Almost every standard work visa starts with the same non-negotiable: a real, signed job offer from an employer in the country you want to work in. Tourist and student visas don't need this, which is exactly why work visas are structurally different — the country is essentially vetting your employer as much as it's vetting you.

The general framework is described on Wikipedia's overview of work permits, which outlines how most countries tie eligibility directly to employer sponsorship rather than the applicant alone.

What Your Employer Has to Do

  • File official sponsorship paperwork with the relevant immigration authority
  • In many countries, prove the role couldn't reasonably be filled by a local candidate (often called a labor market test)
  • Confirm salary and job terms meet the country's minimum threshold for that visa category
  • Register as a licensed sponsor in countries that require it, which can itself take weeks if they've never sponsored before

This is the part that surprises people most: your own documents can be flawless, but if your employer's sponsorship paperwork is incomplete, the whole application stalls regardless.

What You Need to Bring to the Table

  • Proof of qualifications relevant to the role — degrees, certifications, or professional licenses
  • A valid passport with sufficient remaining validity
  • Relevant work experience documentation, especially for skilled-worker categories
  • A clean background check in most cases

Can You Apply Without a Job Offer?

In most countries, no — the standard route requires sponsorship. But a growing number of countries offer separate skilled-worker or points-based visas that evaluate your qualifications, experience, and sometimes language ability without requiring a job offer upfront. These operate on a completely different track from employer-sponsored visas, so it's worth checking whether your target country has one before assuming you need a job lined up first.

Why It Takes So Long

Work visas typically take 1 to 6 months, and the range is wide because so much depends on your employer's side of the process. A company that's sponsored visas before moves fast; a company doing it for the first time can add months just getting their own registration sorted. If you're job hunting internationally, asking early whether the company has sponsored before is a reasonable, practical question.

What is required to get a work visa?

Most work visas require a confirmed job offer, employer sponsorship, proof of qualifications, and sometimes a labor market test showing no local candidate was available for the role.

Can I apply for a work visa without a job offer?

In most countries, no. Standard work visas require employer sponsorship, though some countries offer separate skilled-worker or points-based visas that don't require a job offer in advance.

How long does a work visa take to process?

Work visas typically take longer than tourist visas, often 1 to 6 months depending on the country, role, and whether employer sponsorship documentation is complete.

Does my employer need to do anything for my work visa?

Yes, in most sponsored work visa categories, the employer must file sponsorship paperwork, sometimes including proving the role could not be filled locally.

Bottom Line

A work visa is a two-sided process — your documents matter, but your employer's sponsorship paperwork often determines the actual timeline. Ask early, and don't assume a delay is your fault.
